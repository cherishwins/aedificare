## Who pays: the lenders

[[footage#wall-street: bank towers in lower Manhattan, looking up]] Citigroup, Goldman Sachs, JPMorgan and Morgan Stanley led the dollar sale. [s1] Goldman Sachs and JPMorgan had also lent SoftBank the bridge. [s3] In America, the notes could be offered only to qualified institutional buyers that are also qualified purchasers. [s1] For most institutions, that means owning and investing at least 100 million dollars in securities. [s13] The savers with the tote bags are not invited.

[[footage#sb-tokyo-2: Tokyo at night again, a slower shot]] The dollar notes were not offered in Japan, just as the yen bond was not offered in America. [s1, s5] Neither bond has any collateral behind it. [s1, s5] In the week of the sale, insuring SoftBank's debt against default for five years cost more than 400 basis points a year. [s7] In June it cost about 280. [s7]

The notes are due to settle on the twenty-ninth of September. [s1] From then, SoftBank owes their buyers [[fig: $941.25M | A YEAR IN INTEREST · DOLLAR NOTES · OUR ARITHMETIC FROM THE TERMS]] 941.25 million dollars a year, by our arithmetic. [s1] That is about 2.58 million dollars a day, before the euros. [s1]

## OpenAI: not 2026

[[footage#son-roosevelt: the Stargate announcement in the Roosevelt Room, wide, Son, Altman and Ellison at the lectern]] For the 30 billion, SoftBank gets preferred shares in OpenAI, which turn into ordinary shares if OpenAI lists. [s2] In February, Sam Altman called SoftBank the perfect long-term partner. [s2] SoftBank's longest notes run until 2034. [s1]

CreditSights' Mark Chapman sees room for a ratings upgrade if OpenAI's listing is timely and successful. [s7] Fortune asked Altman whether OpenAI would list this year, in an interview it published on the twelfth of September. [s8] He said now would be an ill-advised moment, given everything happening with safety. [s8] [[quote: s8 | "I would say not 2026." | SAM ALTMAN, CEO, OPENAI, TO FORTUNE]] The notes were priced eleven days later. [s8, s19] SB Energy, another company SoftBank controls, has delayed its listing too. [s7, s9]

## The AI circular deal

[[map: Tokyo 35.68,139.69 > San Francisco 37.77,-122.42 > Piketon 39.07,-83.01 | TOKYO · SAN FRANCISCO · PIKE COUNTY, OHIO]] In August, 17 subsidiaries of SB Energy signed 20-year leases with an OpenAI company, for a campus in Pike County, Ohio. [s9] OpenAI also holds warrants to buy SB Energy shares at one cent each. [s9]

[[footage#piketon-site: the Portsmouth gaseous diffusion plant at Piketon, Ohio, from the air | find: energy.gov/pppo photo and video galleries (public domain), or a Landsat frame of Piketon; video if there is any]] The campus sits on and around the Energy Department's old gaseous diffusion plant, which is still being cleaned up. [s9] Rent is due to start in stages from 2028, as the buildings and their power are ready. [s9] SB Energy believes the campus, at about 8 gigawatts, will be among the largest data centres in the world. [s9] SB Energy has no data centre capacity in operation yet. [s9] The campus's value depends in significant part on Nvidia guaranteeing a declining minimum value for the first nine of its 17 buildings, not the rent. [s9]

[[quote: s9 | "…the NVIDIA residual value guaranty supporting the OpenAI tenant’s lease obligations, as OpenAI is not currently an investment-grade tenant." | SB ENERGY, SEC FORM S-1/A, 21 SEP 2026 | hl: OpenAI is not currently an investment-grade tenant.]] The filing's reason: OpenAI is not currently an investment-grade tenant. [s9] SoftBank controls the landlord, and pays the tenant. [s9, s2]

## Who pays: SoftBank

[[footage#server-hall: rows of supercomputer racks, blinking]] SoftBank pays both sets of lenders from one balance sheet. CreditSights' Mark Chapman calculates that Arm, the chip designer, and OpenAI are three-quarters of SoftBank's asset value. [s7] In August, its Vision Fund signed a 10 billion dollar loan using its OpenAI shares, due in 2028. [s18, s14] If those shares fall significantly in value, it must post cash or repay early. [s18] The shares bought with borrowed money now help it borrow more.

[[footage#tokyo-street: a Tokyo street by day, office workers, a securities branch]] SoftBank's own gauge is its net debt against what its holdings are worth. At the end of June, that was 13.0%. [s14] Its ceiling in normal times is 25%. [s14]

S&P's Makiko Yoshimura wrote that Arm's share price has supported SoftBank's credit quality. [s7] [[over: ¥49.9T | ARM · OF ¥83.1T IN HOLDINGS · NET OF MARGIN LOANS · 30 JUN 2026]] By SoftBank's own count, net of margin loans, Arm alone was 49.9 trillion yen of its 83.1 trillion in holdings. [s14] By the fifth of August, SoftBank's own pro forma had Arm at [[fig: ¥36.8T | ARM, SOFTBANK'S PRO FORMA · 5 AUG 2026 · ¥49.9T ON 30 JUN]] 36.8 trillion. [s14] The ratio is comfortable for as long as Arm's share price is.
