## The auction

[[footage#power-plant: a coal-fired power station, stacks and cooling towers, a black and white survey photograph | find: Library of Congress, Historic American Engineering Record (HAER), search "power plant" or "generating station" in Pennsylvania or Ohio (public domain)]] PJM runs the high-voltage grid for all or part of 13 states and Washington, DC. [s1] It buys capacity years in advance: not electricity, but a plant's promise to perform when the system is most at risk. [s1, s5] For the year from June 2024, [[over: $28.92 | PER MW-DAY · JUNE 2024 TO MAY 2025]] capacity cost 28 dollars and 92 cents a megawatt-day. [s2] [[gag#saturn-v: a Saturn V lifting off, the first second of flame | find: NASA Apollo 11 launch footage (public domain), images.nasa.gov; one second, ungraded]] [[over: $269.92 | PER MW-DAY · ONE YEAR LATER]] A year later it cost 269 dollars and 92 cents. [s2]

[[chart: bars | Eleven times the 2024 price, then a cap | sub: RTO CLEARING PRICE · $ PER MW-DAY · BY DELIVERY YEAR | prefix: $ | note: THE LAST THREE CLEARED AT A PRICE CAP | src: s2, s10 | 2024/25=28.92 | 2025/26=269.92 | 2026/27=329.17 | 2027/28=333.44 | 2028/29=325.00*]] The three auctions since have each cleared at a price cap, the latest at 325 dollars. [s2, s10] [[gag#safety-last: Harold Lloyd hanging from the hands of a clock high above the street, Safety Last (1923) | find: Wikimedia Commons or archive.org, Safety Last, 1923 (public domain in the US); one to two seconds]] The cap arrived after the price did. [s1]

[[quote: s1 | "These auction results show that demand for electricity continues to grow faster than electricity supply." | DAVID MILLS, PRESIDENT AND CEO, PJM, 14 JUL 2026 | hl: grow faster than electricity supply]] David Mills, PJM's chief executive, described the result as demand outrunning supply. [[fig: 6,831 MW | SHORT OF THE RELIABILITY REQUIREMENT]] The auction on the fourteenth of July cleared 6,831 megawatts short of PJM's reliability requirement. [s1] The one before was about 6,500 megawatts short, and until those two, the whole grid had never fallen short. [s1] [[fig: 525 MW | NEW GENERATION, OF 138,318 MW CLEARED]] Of 138,318 megawatts cleared, 525 were new power plants or upgrades, under half of one percent. [s1]

## The rerun

[[footage#ferc-bowring: Joseph Bowring of Monitoring Analytics at FERC's resource adequacy conference, 4 Jun 2025, at the panel table, silent | find: ferc.gov, "Commissioner-led Technical Conference Regarding the Challenge of Resource Adequacy in RTO and ISO Regions", Day 1 webcast (federal, public domain)]] Joseph Bowring is president of Monitoring Analytics, PJM's independent market monitor. [s5] His method is a rerun: clear the auction again with a load forecast that leaves out existing and planned data centres, then compare. [s4] It is an estimate, not an invoice. [s4]

[[chart: stack | Data centre load: $6.3 billion of $16.4 billion | sub: 2028/29 CAPACITY MARKET CHARGES · $ BILLION | prefix: $ | unit: B | note: THE MONITOR'S ESTIMATE · THE REST IS OUR SUBTRACTION | src: s3 | Everything else=10.1 | Data centre load=6.3*]] Rerun that way, 6.3 billion of the auction's 16.4 billion dollars in charges was a result of data centre load. [s3] That is 38.2%. [s3] Over the last four auctions, the monitor's total is 29.4 billion dollars of 63.6 billion, or 46.2%. [s3, s5]

[[chart: bars | Data centres in the forecast: 7,927 MW to 21,470 | sub: DATA CENTRE LOAD IN PJM'S PEAK LOAD FORECAST · MW · BY AUCTION | src: s3 | 2025/26=7,927 | 2026/27=11,993 | 2027/28=17,071 | 2028/29=21,470*]] The forecast behind the auction for mid-2025 counted 7,927 megawatts of data centres. [s3] The forecast behind this one counted 21,470. [s3] [[quote: s5 | "PJM is continuing to act like it's business as usual." | JOSEPH BOWRING, MONITORING ANALYTICS, TO UTILITY DIVE, JUL 2026 | hl: business as usual]] Bowring's review of the grid operator fits in one sentence.

## The bill

[[footage#electric-meter: an electricity meter's disc turning, from a 1930s or 1940s electrification film | find: Prelinger Archives on archive.org, or "Power and the Land" (1940, US Department of Agriculture, public domain)]] The 16.4 billion is PJM's own arithmetic: megawatts cleared, times the price. [s2] [[quote: s1 | "The total value does not equate to the total cost to load because load that is hedged through self-supply or bilateral contracts is not exposed to the clearing prices in the auction." | PJM INTERCONNECTION, 14 JUL 2026 | hl: does not equate to the total cost to load]] PJM adds that it does not equal the cost to customers, because some of them are hedged. [s1] It also does not reach anyone until June 2028, and it runs to the end of May 2029. [s11]

[[chart: bars | Data centres added $11.11 to every megawatt-hour | sub: TOTAL COST OF WHOLESALE POWER · $ PER MWH · JANUARY TO JUNE | prefix: $ | note: THE MIDDLE BAR IS THE MONITOR'S ESTIMATE WITHOUT DATA CENTRE LOAD IN THE CAPACITY AUCTIONS | src: s3 | 2025=76.16 | 2026, without data centre load=103.40 | 2026, actual=114.50*]] What has arrived is the cost of the earlier auctions. In the first half of 2026, wholesale power in PJM cost 114 dollars and 50 cents a megawatt-hour, all in. [s3] A year earlier it was 76 dollars and 16 cents. [s3] Without data centre load in the two capacity auctions now being paid, the monitor puts it at 103 dollars and 40 cents. [s3] The difference is 11 dollars and 11 cents, or 9.7%. [s3] Most of the rest of the rise was energy itself, which cost 47.7% more. [s3]

## The pledge

[[footage#wh-pledge: the signing of the Ratepayer Protection Pledge at the White House, 4 Mar 2026, wide on the table of executives, nobody speaking | find: whitehouse.gov/videos or the White House YouTube channel, "Ratepayer Protection Pledge" (federal, public domain); a wide shot]] On the fourth of March, seven companies signed a pledge at the White House: Amazon, Google, Meta, Microsoft, OpenAI, Oracle and xAI. [s9] [[quote: s8 | "companies agree to protect American consumers from price hikes due to data center energy and infrastructure requirements" | RATEPAYER PROTECTION PLEDGE, THE WHITE HOUSE, 4 MAR 2026 | hl: protect American consumers from price hikes]] They agreed to build, bring or buy new generation, and to pay for the grid upgrades their data centres need. [s9] [[fig: 38.2% | FIRST AUCTION AFTER THE PLEDGE · DATA CENTRE SHARE]] In the first capacity auction after the pledge, the monitor's share for data centre load was 38.2%. [s3, s2]

[[quote: s5 | "There's only one way to do what hyperscalers agree is the right thing to do, and that is to run a separate auction." | JOSEPH BOWRING, MONITORING ANALYTICS, TO UTILITY DIVE, JUL 2026 | hl: run a separate auction]] Bowring says that under PJM's current rules, the pledge cannot be met. [s5] His fix is for data centres to bring their own generation. [s5] Those that cannot would buy capacity in separate auctions, on 15-year contracts. [s5] The hyperscalers, by his account, agree.

## The backstop

[[footage#transmission-lines: high-voltage transmission towers in a long row across open country | find: US Department of Energy photo library at energy.gov, or the Library of Congress Carol M. Highsmith Archive, "transmission lines" (public domain)]] PJM's answer to the shortfall is a one-time backstop auction, [[over: 6.8 GW | ONE-TIME BACKSTOP AUCTION]] for about 6.8 gigawatts. [s6] [[chart: timeline | Bids open on 30 September, if FERC accepts in time | src: s1, s7, s6 | 2026-07-14=6,831 MW short | 2026-07-31=Plan filed at FERC | 2026-09-30=Bids open* | 2026-10-21=Bids close | 2026-12-02=Results due]] PJM filed the plan with FERC on the thirty-first of July. [s7] Bids run from the thirtieth of September to the twenty-first of October. [s6] The window opens only if FERC accepts the plan. [s7] Results are due by the second of December, and winners get commitments of up to 15 years. [s6, s7] Which customers pay is left to each state. [s6]

[[chart: bars | The next cap sits 28 cents above July's uncapped price | sub: $ PER MW-DAY · UCAP | prefix: $ | note: NOT DIRECTLY COMPARABLE · THE BACKSTOP CAP LIMITS THE WEIGHTED AVERAGE OF SELECTED OFFERS, UP TO 15 YEARS | src: s2, s7 | July auction, as capped=325.00 | July auction without the cap, PJM's simulation, most of the grid=554.72 | Backstop, proposed cap=555.00*]] PJM also ran July's auction again without its cap and floor. [s2] Across most of the grid, it cleared at 554 dollars and 72 cents a megawatt-day. [s2] The total would have been 29.7 billion dollars, not 16.4. [s2] The cap PJM proposes for the backstop is 555 dollars. [s7, s6]

[[card: 28 CENTS APART.]] The two numbers are 28 cents apart. [s2, s7]
