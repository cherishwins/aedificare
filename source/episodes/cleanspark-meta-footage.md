# Episode 3 footage: where each shot is

For `cleanspark-meta.md`. Download each file, rename it exactly as the **file**
column says (the `-at-N` part is where the shot starts, in seconds), and
attach it to the GitHub Release `footage-cleanspark-meta`; then run the
`episode` workflow with slug `cleanspark-meta`. A shot left out renders as a
slate, so the film can go out with any of them missing. The renderer takes
one file per shot, the first whose name starts with the shot's id, so where
a report offers two or three frames the first is the file and the others are
swaps, not extras. Two of the script's hints were wrong and the sheet
corrects them: Meta's Prineville campus is west and south-west of the town
(around 44.295 N, 120.885 W, beside the airport and the solar field), not
east; and there is no Highsmith photograph of Washington County, Georgia, so
the pine roads come from Harris and Banks counties.

Checked 3 Oct 2026: every link below answered a one-byte range request (200
or 206) as the stated type, its licence was read on the item itself or on
the publisher's own rule, and a thumbnail or frame was fetched and looked
at; where a check could not be completed the row says so. "Public domain"
here means a work of the US government (17 U.S.C. 105) or an item marked
CC0 or Public Domain Mark by its archive. DVIDS search sat behind an AWS
WAF challenge all day, so its items were reached by id, not by search.

## Credits and the file names

Beside the files, attach `credits.txt` to the same Release: one line per
file, `id: source, licence`, in the order above (for example
`bitcoin-rigs: Voice of America, VOA Connect "A Cleaner Bitcoin", 21 Jan 2022, public domain`). The
film's description carries them under "Footage:", and the render names
every file in use that has no line. Names are matched case-blind, with
spaces and underscores read as hyphens, so `Bitcoin Rigs-at-225.MP4`
still lands; a file whose `-at-` number is past its own end is refused for
a clip and played from its start for footage, and the render says so.
When a DVIDS reel is used (construction-steel, site-grading), DVIDS asks
for the line "The appearance of U.S. Department of War (DoW) visual
information does not imply or constitute DoW endorsement" in the credits,
and notes that its postings do not waive publicity or privacy rights, so
keep faces out and military markings incidental. The Landsat frames ask
for a data-source statement and NASA GIBS for its acknowledgment line;
the Highsmith stills for "Photographs in the Carol M. Highsmith Archive,
Library of Congress, Prints and Photographs Division." The portrait stills
(Hearst Tower, the Pine Mountain Valley road) sit between two black bars
in the wide film rather than filling the frame.
