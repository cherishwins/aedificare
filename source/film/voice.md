# The film voice

Owner, 2026-09-24: "Let's be like Fireship but Patrick Boyle dry humor,
a unique Frankenstein voice." This is that voice, for the narrated films
only. The editions on the page keep their own register; a film script is
written FROM an edition, never pasted from it, and every number in the
script must exist in the edition it is cut from.

## What is borrowed, and from whom

From Fireship: density. Every sentence carries a fact or a turn; nothing
warms up, nothing recaps, nothing says "in this video". The open is the
number, inside the first ten seconds. Technical terms are used correctly
and never explained twice. A beat is one sentence; cuts land on the noun.

From Patrick Boyle: temperature. The joke is the fact, stated flat. The
aside comes after the fact, in the same breath, and is never signposted.
Understatement over exaggeration: "which was generous of them", not
"which is insane". The narrator never laughs, never says "I", never
promises, never asks. When a number is absurd, the number is allowed to
be absurd on its own.

From the kit: declarative, no hedging, no exclamation, no self-praise,
no call to action, no tagline, no building pun, never a sentence that
explains what the entity does. The voice speaks; the entity still does
not.

## The mechanics

- Sentences: eight to eighteen words. One idea each. A paragraph is three
  to six sentences and ends on the driest one.
- Pace: Kokoro `am_michael`. Measured on the sample: speed 1.05 gives
  about 140 words a minute while speaking, 1.2 about 155. Fireship runs
  faster than either; Boyle slower. The owner picks; the model delivers
  flat at any speed, which is the point, and the writing does the work.
- Pauses are written, not performed: a full stop is a beat; a paragraph
  break is two; a section heading is the rose.
- Numbers are spoken as the page prints them and shown as they are said;
  a number said is a number on screen. Nothing is rounded for effect.
- One aside per paragraph at most. Two is a comedian; zero is a
  datasheet.
- Comparison is the engine: the two prices, the two radios, the two
  buyers. Put them in the same sentence whenever the facts allow.
- No adjectives of scale ("massive", "tiny", "huge"). The number is the
  adjective.
- No "you" except to hand the viewer a consequence ("unless you are the
  one paying").
- The record line and the dove close every film unchanged; the script
  ends on a fact, never on a moral.

## What the checker will hold

Every figure in a script (money, number with unit, spelled number) must
appear in the built edition page. A script that invents a number fails
the build. Wording is free; facts are not.

## A test

Read a paragraph aloud at a dinner. If someone laughs, it worked. If
someone asks what the company does, it failed.
