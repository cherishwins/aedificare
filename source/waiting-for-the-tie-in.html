Tie-In

The chips are bought. Everything they wait for is not.

15 Oct 2026 · Jesse Jamesr = cos(kθ) · seed 20261015
00

The chips are
in the building.

In November 2025 the chief executive of Microsoft said the quiet part on a podcast. "The biggest issue we are now having is not a compute glut, but it's power," Satya Nadella said. "You may actually have a bunch of chips sitting in inventory that I can't plug in." F His chief financial officer told analysts the company had been "short now for many quarters" on space and power. F

The most valuable company in the largest build-out in history bought the hardware and could not turn it on.

This edition is about why. Not the chips. Everything the chips wait for.

00 · The brief

It started with a summary that said America was stuck. Turbines come from China. Rare earths cannot be refined at home. Electricians are being bid away from housing. Banks are getting nervous. I checked every claim against primary sources. Some were wrong, and the ones that were wrong were the ones that would get the whole argument dismissed.

What survived is a queue. Chips wait for a building. The building waits for a grid connection. The connection waits for a study, the study waits for a rule, and the power behind it waits for a turbine with a 2031 delivery slot. Each wait is rational on its own. Together they stall.

Every material claim below carries one of three marks.

F
Verified fact. Confirmed in a primary source or credible reporting, listed at the back.
I
Sourced inference. My reasoning from the facts. Mine to defend, yours to argue with.

Open question. Unknown, disputed, or untested.
01

474 gigawatts
asking.

01 · The queue

Texas runs its own grid. In August 2026 its operator, ERCOT, told state regulators that about 474 gigawatts of large loads were asking to connect, roughly nine in ten of them data centres. F The grid's record peak demand, set in July, is about 91 gigawatts. F The requests add up to more than five Texases.

Oncor, one Dallas utility, holds nearly 300 gigawatts of requests by itself. F In September ERCOT sorted its first batch: 66 gigawatts conditionally cleared, 302 gigawatts excluded. F On 3 August the governor paused new data-centre energisations pending an audit. F

The rest of the country is slower. About 2,061 gigawatts of generation and storage sat in US interconnection queues at the end of 2025, and the median project built that year took more than five years from request to operation. F In northern Virginia, Dominion has said a large load can wait up to seven years. F PJM, the grid that serves Virginia, has now cleared its capacity auction short of its reliability requirement three times running, the first shortfalls in its history. F

A request is not a project. Many are speculative, filed in several places at once to hold a place in line. That is part of the problem: studying the ghosts delays the real ones. I

02

Sold out
to 2031.

02 · The machines

Suppose the grid said yes tomorrow. The power still has to come from somewhere, and firm power in America means gas turbines.

Three companies make nearly all the large ones the US buys. GE Vernova's backlog and slot reservations reached 116 gigawatts in July, and it is now taking reservations for 2031 delivery. F Siemens Energy holds 69 gigawatts and quotes lead times of three years or more. F Mitsubishi Power's large-frame backlog is 35 gigawatts, up from 23 a year earlier. F Wood Mackenzie counted about 110 gigawatts on order at the end of 2025 against 60 to 70 gigawatts of global factory capacity a year, with prices heading for roughly 195 percent above 2019. F

The summary said the turbines come from China. They don't. No Chinese large-frame turbine has been delivered to the United States. Dongfang's first self-developed F-class export was a 50 megawatt unit to Kazakhstan in late 2025. F The bottleneck is in South Carolina, Berlin and Takasago, and those factories are sold out.

Then the transformer. Every plant and every data centre needs one to step voltage up or down. A large power transformer takes about 128 weeks to arrive and a generator step-up unit about 144, and prices are up 77 percent since 2019. F One American company makes the grain-oriented electrical steel inside them. F China makes about 60 percent of the world's transformers, and since 26 August 2026 an executive order bars Chinese bulk-power equipment from new transactions on the US grid. F

An order placed today for the machine that makes the power arrives around 2030. I

03

Without this,

03 · The chain

Twenty-one pieces the build needs. Select one to see what it needs, what it enables, how long it takes to replace from a standing start, and where the number comes from. Then remove it, and watch what becomes impossible downstream.

Try the transformer first. Then try EUV lithography, the one piece with nothing upstream of it and a decade to replace.

04

Thirty-five
dollars, or twenty.

04 · The hands

Somebody has to wire it.

In Abilene, Texas, where the first Stargate campus is going up, data-centre electricians earn about 35 dollars an hour plus overtime and per diem. A local homebuilder's electrical contractor pays about 20, and lost five workers to the site. F Another Abilene builder told the Texas Tribune his houses now take two months longer than before the data centres came. F

It shows in the national numbers. Spending on data-centre construction ran at an annualised 85 billion dollars in August 2026, up 73 percent in a year. Single-family construction spending fell 3.5 percent over the same year. F Lennar's chief executive told investors in September that "immigration enforcement and enthusiastic data center construction continue to create tightness in certain geographies." F KB Home said the same month it had no trouble getting labour to its sites. F Both can be true in different places.

The country has 821,000 electricians, and about one in five is over 55. F Immigrants are 26 percent of the construction workforce, a record, and enforcement surges cut construction employment by about 2 percent in the cities they hit. F

04 · Where the answer is less dramatic

A hyperscale site needs roughly one peak worker per megawatt. F At that ratio, even the 66 gigawatts ERCOT just cleared would occupy a small share of American electricians at any one time. Labour is not what caps the data centres. It is where their cost lands: in wages a homebuilder cannot match, and in houses that take longer to finish. I

Move the

05 · The model

Each year, data-centre load asks for power. It can get it through a grid tie-in or by building its own plant. The grid path is capped by whichever is smallest that year: interconnection throughput, firm generation free for new load, or transformer deliveries. Both paths draw on the same electricians. Chips keep arriving regardless.

Defaults are set from the published figures above. Every slider says where its number comes from, and the ones that are my assumptions say so.

At the defaults, transformers bind first, then the tie-in, then firm generation from 2029, when spare capacity runs out and the new turbine slots have not yet opened. The queue never clears. Stranded compute peaks at about 34 gigawatts. I The 2028 backlog in the model, about 34 gigawatts, sits close to Morgan Stanley's reported estimate of a 32 gigawatt shortfall through 2028, which the model was not tuned to. F

06

Build your
own plant.

06 · The bypass

The fastest way around the queue is to leave it. Cleanview counts about 90 gigawatts of on-site generation announced at 59 US data centres, more than a quarter of planned data-centre capacity. About 2 gigawatts of it runs today. F

Stargate's Abilene campus has 29 GE Vernova aeroderivative turbines on site, about a gigawatt. F xAI's Memphis-area campuses run roughly 1.5 gigawatts of gas turbines, helped by a Mississippi rule that allows twelve months of operation without a permit. F Meta is paying the full revenue requirement of three new Entergy gas plants in Louisiana for fifteen years. F

This is the turbine queue again, in a smaller size. Aeroderivative and reciprocating units come from a different, faster factory pool than the large frames, which is why they are being bought. I

06 · Three grids, barely touching

Texas keeps its own interconnection to stay outside federal transmission rules. That is why it connects faster, and why nobody can rescue it when it runs short. Its direct-current ties to the rest of the country total 820 megawatts, plus 400 to Mexico, about 1.3 percent of its peak. F A 3 gigawatt line to Mississippi, Southern Spirit, is scheduled for service somewhere between 2029 and 2032, depending on which of its own documents you read. F

07

Negative
free cash flow.

07 · The money

The hyperscalers can afford this. Can't they?

In the second quarter of 2026 Alphabet reported negative free cash flow, its first negative quarter since it listed in 2004: minus 5.9 billion dollars, on 44.9 billion of capital spending. F Amazon's free cash flow over the trailing twelve months went to minus 7.6 billion. F Meta's second-quarter free cash flow was 784 million dollars on 31 billion of capital spending. F Microsoft is the only one of the four still clearly positive. F Bank of America projects the eight largest hyperscalers move from 180 billion dollars of combined free cash flow in 2025 to minus 64 billion this year and minus 186 billion in 2028. F

These are still among the most profitable companies ever built. The profit comes from search, advertising, cloud and software. The spending goes to AI. What has not been shown is that the second pays back the first. I Bain estimates AI needs about 2 trillion dollars a year of revenue by 2030 to fund the build, and puts the shortfall at 800 billion. F OpenAI reportedly expects to burn about 25 billion dollars this year. F

The rest is borrowed. Data-centre securitisations outstanding reached about 61 billion dollars, from 4 billion in 2020. F That debt is written against compute revenue that starts when the power comes on. Every month the tie-in slips is a month of interest with nothing to pay it. I

08 · Side by side

Matched pairs, latest full-year figures, one source on every row. Where China is behind, the row says so. Bars are proportional within each pair.

08 · China's own queue

The comparison is not a scoreboard. Up to 80 percent of newly built Chinese computing centres sat unused in 2025, by MIT Technology Review's reporting. F Huawei shipped about 800,000 Ascend accelerators in 2025 against roughly 4.5 million from Nvidia, its leading node is held at a 7 nanometre class without EUV, and its stockpile of foreign high-bandwidth memory is expected to run out this year. F Listed solar makers lost more than 50 billion yuan in 2025 at 40 to 50 percent utilisation, and the car industry's margin fell to 4.1 percent, a record low. F Youth unemployment was 18.9 percent in August. F

China builds faster and overbuilds. America plans more and builds less. Each is paying for its habit. I

Magnets,
not megawatts.

10 · The other queue

Rare earths and gallium are a real chokepoint, but they choke a different line. They gate permanent magnets, gallium-nitride power electronics and radar, not the turbines and transformers that hold up the data centres. Turbines and transformers run on steel, copper and factory slots. I

China refines 91 percent of the world's rare earths, 99 percent of its gallium and 94 percent of its germanium. F The United States has produced no primary gallium since 1987. F China's October 2025 export package is suspended until 10 November 2026, its ban on gallium and germanium sales to the US until 27 November. Its April 2025 licensing of seven heavy rare earths was never lifted. F Whether the suspensions are extended is the open question of the next six weeks. Q

11

The order
of things.

11 · What it would take

Money is not the binding constraint. Over 700 billion dollars of capital spending is guided for this year. F The constraints are things money buys slowly: a factory slot, a permit, a study, an apprentice who takes four years to qualify.

In my view, three changes would move the dates. Queue rules that make speculative requests cost something, so the ghosts leave and the studies speed up. Transformer and turbine factory capacity committed now, since anything ordered today lands around 2030. And transmission permitting measured in years, not decades, because SunZia took about twenty. I

None of this is a technology problem. Every piece in the chain is a solved engineering problem with a waiting list. I

The chips are the fast part.
