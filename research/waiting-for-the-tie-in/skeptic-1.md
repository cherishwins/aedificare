## 1.2. CFO "short now for many quarters" on space and power
Researcher's verdict: overstated. **Skeptic: amended.** The verdict stands, but for a different reason. The researcher's quote and its context also need correcting.

Evidence: Microsoft's own transcript of the FY26 Q1 call, 29 Oct 2025, is on its investor relations event page (transcript link https://aka.ms/transcriptfy26q1). https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/investor/events/fy-2026/earnings-fy-2026-q1 . From Amy Hood's answer to Mark Moerdler, in order:
- "And as you know, we've spent the past few years not actually being short GPUs and CPUs per se, we were short the space or the power, is the language we use, to put them in."
- "We spent a lot of time building out that infrastructure." … "Those are very long-lived assets, as we've talked about, 15 to 20 years." … "It is very high."
- "And so, when I think about balancing those things, seeing the pivot to GPUs, CPUs, short lived, seeing the pivot in terms of how those are being utilized, we are, and I said this now, we've been short now for many quarters. I thought we were going to catch up. We are not. Demand is increasing."

Findings:
(a) A primary was available. The researcher said Microsoft's transcript "was not readable" and quoted the Motley Fool instead. The IR event page carries the transcript text. The Fool's "as the language we use" is "is the language we use" in Microsoft's own transcript. The quote in lane-1 should be corrected to the primary's wording.
(b) The scope is not what the researcher said. In Hood's words, "short now for many quarters" is about being short of capacity in general against rising demand. It comes several sentences after the space-or-power sentence, not in it. The edition attaches the quoted words to "space and power", which joins two separate remarks. The time element is still sound in substance, because the space-or-power sentence itself covers "the past few years".
(c) "And" versus "or". The researcher put the most weight on this. It is weak: "the space or the power, is the language we use" is Microsoft naming one constraint two ways, not two alternatives. It is not the main problem.
(d) The CFO is unnamed on the page. Hood is named on the transcript.

The page should say: "His chief financial officer, Amy Hood, told analysts that for "the past few years" the company had been short not of chips but of "the space or the power" to put them in, and that it had been "short now for many quarters"." The smallest fix: "...the company had been "short now for many quarters", and that what it lacked was not chips but "the space or the power" to put them in." Carousel slide 02 and the 09 row ("short on space and power "for many quarters."") need the same fix.
