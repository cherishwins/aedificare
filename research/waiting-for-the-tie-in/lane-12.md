## 12.4. [06 · The bypass] xAI's Memphis-area campuses run roughly 1.5 gigawatts of gas turbines, helped by a Mississippi rule that allows twelve months of operation without a permit. (attributed to none) (cites 30)
Verdict: wrong
Primary: Mississippi Free Press, "xAI faces fierce opposition over Southaven, Mississippi power plant permit", Mar 6, 2026 (updated Apr 22, 2026). https://www.mississippifreepress.org/xai-faces-fierce-opposition-over-southaven-mississippi-power-plant-permit/
Also read: Mississippi Today, Mar 2026 and Jul 31 2026 (the Jul 31 page returned HTTP 403 and was not read); WDAM, "State approves xAI's use of gas turbines in Southaven", Mar 10 2026 (https://www.wdam.com/2026/03/11/state-approves-xais-use-gas-turbines-southaven/), which has no exemption or 1.5 GW figure; SELC press release, Mar 10 2026 (https://www.selc.org/press-releases/mississippi-regulators-rubber-stamp-air-permit-for-xai-power-plant-ignoring-overwhelming-public-pushback/), which has no exemption or total-GW figure.
Read: 2026-11-01
Quote: "MDEQ has allowed xAI to forgo permits for the turbines currently housed at the Southaven plant," claiming that "federal rules exempt turbines classified as 'temporary-mobile' engines from requiring permits for up to a year."
Quote: "MDEQ, meanwhile, maintains that" "the EPA's new standards do not explicitly impose permitting requirements" "on temporary turbines like the ones located in Southaven."
Quote: "Those machines would replace the so-called temporary turbines currently housed at the facility," which "have operated without permits for the better part of a year."
True as: Mississippi's environmental regulator (MDEQ) has let xAI run temporary-mobile gas turbines at Southaven without a permit, saying federal rules exempt them for up to a year; environmental groups dispute that reading. xAI's Colossus 2 sits in Southaven, Mississippi, across the state line from Memphis.
Notes: (1) Attribution is wrong: the exemption is a federal rule as MDEQ describes it, not a Mississippi rule, and it is contested. No Mississippi rule text was opened. (2) Twelve months is "up to a year" in MDEQ's claim; the GEM Colossus 2 page cited as source 30 (https://gem.wiki/Colossus_2_power_station) was read and contains no turbine count, capacity, or permit rule. (3) The roughly 1.5 GW figure was not in any page I could open. A search summary attributed about 1.5 GW to Cleanview's Colossus snapshot (about 198 MW at Colossus 1 and about 1,300 MW at Colossus 2), but Cleanview's Colossus full report is customer-only and the fetched page did not contain it. Other figures conflict: up to 27 unpermitted turbines at Southaven (Mississippi Free Press, Mar 2026), 69 temporary turbines (Mississippi Today, Jul 2026, search summary only), as many as 35 turbines in Boxtown, Memphis (Mississippi Free Press), and a permanent 41-turbine plant of about 1.2 GW permitted in Southaven in March 2026. Permits and appeals were still pending in the sources I found. (4) I did not search for or confirm a status update after July 2026.
