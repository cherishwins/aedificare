# Lane 14: AI finance and the build pipeline

Edition: "Waiting for the Tie-In" (15 Oct 2026). Sections 07 (The money) and 05 and 03 (The model, The chain).

## 14.1. [07 · The money] Bain estimates AI needs about 2 trillion dollars a year of revenue by 2030 to fund the build, and puts the shortfall at 800 billion. (attributed to Bain) (cites 38)
Verdict: holds
Primary: Bain & Company, "$2 trillion in new revenue needed to fund AI's scaling trend" (Bain's 6th annual Global Technology Report release, distributed via PR Newswire), 23 Sep 2025. https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/2-trillion-in-new-revenue-needed-to-fund-ais-scaling-trend---bain--companys-6th-annual-global-technology-report-302563362.html
Read: 2026-11-01
Quote: "Two trillion dollars in annual revenue is what's needed to fund computing power needed to meet anticipated AI demand by 2030." / "even with AI-related savings, the world is still $800 billion short to keep pace with demand."
True as: Bain says about 2 trillion dollars a year in revenue is needed by 2030 to fund the computing power for anticipated AI demand, and that even with AI-related savings the world is still 800 billion dollars short of keeping pace with demand.
Notes: The release gives no time basis for the 800 billion (annual or cumulative) and does not say whether it is revenue or capex. It also does not give a scenario. The release quotes Bain's Crawford describing "about $2 trillion in new revenue" (without "annual") alongside "about $500 billion in capital expenditures"; the edition's word "annual" follows the release sentence. The full Bain report was not opened; the release is Bain's own. "To fund the build" is the edition's paraphrase of "to fund computing power".

## 14.2. [07 · The money] OpenAI reportedly expects to burn about 25 billion dollars this year. (attributed to reported (The Information and FT via TradingKey)) (cites 39)
Verdict: overstated
Primary: The Decoder (Matthias Bastian), "OpenAI adds 111 billion to its cash burn forecast as AI costs spiral beyond projections", 21 Feb 2026, citing The Information's internal financial documents. https://the-decoder.com/openai-adds-111-billion-to-its-cash-burn-forecast-as-ai-costs-spiral-beyond-projections/ (The Information's own article was not opened; it is paywalled in the search results and the text was not retrievable.)
Read: 2026-11-01
Quote: "OpenAI forecasts a cash burn of $25 billion in 2026 and $57 billion in 2027" (The Decoder, lead sentence). "Citing internal financial documents, The Information reports that OpenAI has revised its projections again."
True as: A February 2026 report by The Information, citing internal financial documents, said OpenAI forecast a 2026 cash burn of about 25 billion dollars.
Notes: The cited TradingKey page (https://www.tradingkey.com/analysis/stocks/us-stocks/261972340-openai-cost-revenue-q1-2026-tradingkey, 17 Jun 2026) does not attribute the 25 billion to The Information or the FT. It says: "According to OpenAI's forecasts, full-year cash burn will reach $25 billion this year" with no named source. It cites The Information only for Q1 2026 figures ("According to a report by The Information on June 16") and the FT only for 2025 spending ("OpenAI's full-year spending reached $34 billion last year"). So the attribution "The Information and FT via TradingKey" is not what TradingKey says for this figure. The figure is also a projection dated early 2026, not a current expectation. Later coverage (search summary only, not opened) cites about 27 billion for 2026 after a renegotiated Microsoft deal, so the number has moved. "This year" must be read as 2026 as of February. The word "expects" is OpenAI's forecast as reported, not a reported expectation of today.

## 14.3. [07 · The money] Data-centre securitisations outstanding reached about 61 billion dollars, from 4 billion in 2020. (attributed to Structured Finance Association) (cites 40)
Verdict: overstated
Primary: Structured Finance Association, Research Corner, "How Data Center ABS and CMBS Fit in a Broader Financing Ecosystem", 23 Jul 2026 (the figure is attributed in the note to Barclays Research). https://structuredfinance.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/SFA-Research-Corner_How-Data-Center-ABS-and-CMBS-Fit-in-a-Broader-Financing-Ecosystem.pdf
Read: 2026-11-01
Quote: "According to Barclays Research, outstanding issuance has grown from $4 billion in 2020 to $61 billion year-to-date 2026, reflecting the growing role of securitization in financing stabilized data-center assets." (SFA note, page 3, section "Within Securitized Credit, ABS and CMBS Serve Different Roles"; read from the rendered PDF pages.)
True as: Per Barclays Research, as cited in a July 2026 Structured Finance Association note, outstanding data-centre ABS and CMBS issuance was about 61 billion dollars year-to-date 2026, up from about 4 billion in 2020.
Notes: The figure is Barclays', relayed by SFA; the SFA note does not give it as its own estimate. The period is year-to-date 2026 as of July, not a year-end or current total, and the metric is "outstanding issuance", not a stock of outstanding bonds. Scope is data-centre ABS and CMBS only, not all "data-centre securitisations" in the wider sense. The same note says data-centre ABS is about 12% of esoteric ABS and data-centre CMBS about 6% of SASB CMBS. The Barclays report itself was not found (a search summary said so). The raw PDF fetch returned binary; the text was read from the rendered pages.

## 14.4. [05 · The model] Morgan Stanley's reported estimate of a 32 gigawatt shortfall through 2028 (attributed to Morgan Stanley, as reported by Benzinga) (cites 27)
Verdict: overstated
Primary: Benzinga, "Nvidia, Broadcom May Be Surprisingly Safe From a 32-GW Hole in the AI Boom, Morgan Stanley Says", Oct 2026. Could not open: WebFetch returned HTTP 403 Forbidden. https://www.benzinga.com/markets/prediction-markets/26/10/62174172/nvidia-broadcom-ai-power-shortage (the Morgan Stanley note itself, "Powering AI: Racking Up the Power Shortfall", 21 Sep 2026, was not opened; it is known only through search summaries of it).
Read: 2026-11-01
Quote: Not retrieved from the primary. Search-result summary of the coverage: "Morgan Stanley estimates that US data center developers face a net power shortfall of about 32 GW through 2028, roughly 34% of projected demand, even after counting behind-the-meter generation and fuel cells." (secondary search summary; not verbatim from the note.)
True as: Morgan Stanley's 21 September 2026 note, as summarised in press coverage, estimates a net US shortfall of about 32 gigawatts of data-centre power through 2028.
Notes: Scope the edition's sentence drops: the figure is US (not global or North America), net of behind-the-meter generation, and the shortfall is against demand the note models at 97 GW (up from 68 GW). Other coverage gives different numbers for other notes or baselines: 38 GW (an August 2026 report, per a search summary), 49 GW against up to 74 GW of demand, and 57 GW before other supply. Some outlets describe the shortfall as 32 to 33 GW. The edition's own model note says the 2028 backlog is "close to" this estimate, which is fair as a comparison but the scopes differ (the model is not a US-only, net-of-on-site-generation figure). Benzinga is a secondary report; the primary note was not accessible, so the figure rests on press summaries of a paywalled note.

## 14.5. [03 · The chain (Data-centre shell)] 66 GW under construction in North America; vacancy about 1%. (attributed to chain source: JLL; Caixin) (cites none in list)
Verdict: holds
Primary: JLL, "North America Data Center Report Midyear 2026", 11 Aug 2026. https://www.jll.com/en-us/insights/market-dynamics/north-america-data-centers.html
Read: 2026-11-01
Quote: "More than 66 GW of data center capacity is under construction in North America, an extraordinary volume." (section "Frontier markets account for 77% of all capacity under construction", first paragraph.) "Vacancy remains at 1% for the third consecutive year, despite unprecedented construction." (section "Data center vacancy is likely to remain near zero through 2028", first paragraph.)
True as: JLL's midyear 2026 North America report (11 Aug 2026) says more than 66 GW of data-centre capacity is under construction in North America, and vacancy is 1%.
Notes: The 66 GW is a floor ("more than"), not an exact figure; the edition should keep "more than" or say "over 66". The 66 GW is North America, not US only, and the vacancy sentence is not tied to a named geography on the page; the page does not give a US-only figure. The edition's list gives no cite number for this claim. "Caixin" is attributed as a second chain source, but the Caixin article in the edition's own source list (Caixin, "How a windswept city in north China became an AI powerhouse", 14 Aug 2026) is about a city in northern China, and was not opened. It does not support a North American figure and should not be cited for this claim. Search-result coverage of the JLL report also reports 95% of the 66 GW pre-committed (DCD, not opened as primary), which the edition does not use.
