## 21.1. [09 · What did not hold; 08 · Side by side (Grid)] Power is cheaper in China. | Partly | The national industrial average is about level, 8.8 cents against 8.6. (attributed to China Briefing; EIA) (cites 50)
Verdict: overstated
Primary: Tianyi Xiao and Qian Zhou (China Briefing, Dezan Shira & Associates), "China's Industrial Power Rates 2025: A Guide for Investors", 19 May 2025. https://www.china-briefing.com/news/chinas-industrial-power-rates-category-electricity-usage-region-classification/
Read: 2026-11-01
Quote: "the average industrial power rate in China has increased slightly, reaching approximately US$0.088/kWh in 2024." / "up from US$0.084/kWh in 2019." / "China's electricity prices remain lower than those in most developing countries, such as India and Mexico."
True as: China's average industrial power price is about the same as the US's, or slightly higher: about 8.8 US cents per kWh in 2024 (China Briefing) against the US figure in the edition (8.6, see notes). Power is cheaper in China only at certain subsidised hubs (see 21.2), not on the national average.
Notes:
- China figure is 2024, from China Briefing (published 19 May 2025). The article makes no US comparison.
- The 8.6 US figure is not verified. The edition cites EIA STEO table 7a (https://www.eia.gov/outlooks/steo/tables/pdf/7atab.pdf). The fetch returned compressed PDF data with no readable text, so I could not read the table or confirm 8.6. The STEO data-browser landing page (https://www.eia.gov/outlooks/steo/tables/?tableNumber=7#table_07a) showed no table and no China figures. I did not find China in the table I could open, but that is not a full-text search, so I make no claim that STEO 7a lacks China.
- Scope mismatch: China is a 2024 average, and the US figure's year is unknown. Check both are the same year before printing a gap.
- The EIA monthly table (5.6.A, see 21.2) gives US industrial prices of 8.98 cents (Aug 2025) and 9.64 cents (Aug 2026, preliminary), which are monthly figures and not comparable with an annual 8.6 without the same basis.
- The row's "Partly" tag does not fix the headline: "cheaper" is not what the national figures show.

Notes:
- The "roughly 4.5 cents" is not in Caixin. It is my conversion of a yuan figure. The conversion uses about 6.78 yuan per dollar, from secondary FX reports in search snippets (PBOC fixings in August 2026, roughly 6.78–6.79). I did not open a primary FX page, so the rate is approximate. The range is 4.4–5.2 cents, and 4.5 sits at its low end.
- Caixin's article is paywalled. The only text I saw was an AI digest, which I treat as a lead only. The digest does not say whether 0.3–0.35 yuan is an industrial, data-centre or residential tariff, so the customer class is unverified.
- Location: Caixin's headline calls Ulanqab a city "in north China", not a western hub. The edition's "western data-centre hubs" does not match the Caixin source.
- The Virginia range matches EIA table 5.6.A (monthly, industrial). It is not the EIA STEO table 7a cited in the edition (not readable, see 21.1).
- OIES: I searched for an OIES analysis of data-centre prices in Virginia and China and found none. I did not open any OIES page. The OIES attribution is unsupported in my search.

## 21.4. [08 · Side by side (Grid)] Data-centre electricity use | 150–200 | 176 | TWh | China 2025, government figures via Carbon Brief; US 2023, LBNL. Years differ. Roughly level. (attributed to Chinese government figures via Carbon Brief; LBNL) (cites 52)
Verdict: overstated
Primary: China: Carbon Brief explainer "How China is managing the rising energy demand from data centres", 16 April 2025 (publication date shown on the page). https://www.carbonbrief.org/explainer-how-china-is-managing-the-rising-energy-demand-from-data-centres . US: Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory, "2024 Report on U.S. Data Center Energy Use" (announced 20 Dec 2024), via Berkeley Lab's energy analysis news page. https://energyanalysis.lbl.gov/news/berkeley-lab-report-evaluates-increase-electricity-demand-data-centers
Read: 2026-11-01
Quote: Carbon Brief (as read by the fetch tool; article dated 16 April 2025): "More recent government figures put demand at 77TWh in 2022, 150-200TWh in 2025 and 400TWh by 2030." Carbon Brief, same article: "the electricity consumption of China's data centers in 2020 was 200TWh". LBNL page: "The total data center electricity usage climbed from 58 TWh in 2014 to 176 TWh in 2023" and "Data centers consumed about 4.4% of total U.S. electricity in 2023".
True as: China's 2025 data-centre electricity use was estimated at 150–200 TWh in government-linked figures reported in April 2025, against 176 TWh measured for the US in 2023 (LBNL). The two figures are in different years and different kinds (a 2025 estimate versus a measured 2023 total), so they are not a like-for-like comparison.
Notes:
- The China "2025" figure is a 2025 estimate from an article dated 16 April 2025. It is not a 2025 measurement. The edition's label "China 2025" needs that qualifier.
- Carbon Brief's same article gives China 2020 at 200 TWh, which is above its 2025 range of 150–200. The article's figures are internally inconsistent, which the edition does not mention.
- The primary Chinese government document was not opened. Carbon Brief attributes the figures to the Development Research Centre; I did not verify that attribution.
- LBNL's 176 TWh (2023) and 4.4% share are confirmed on the Berkeley Lab page. The LBNL page does not mention cryptocurrency exclusion, so I did not confirm that point. The LBNL newscenter page returned 403 and was not used.
- "Roughly level": 150–200 against 176 is a fair description of overlap, but only as an approximation given the different years and bases.
