## 2.1. In August 2026 its operator, ERCOT, told state regulators that about 474 gigawatts of large loads were asking to connect, roughly nine in ten of them data centres.
Researcher: overstated. Skeptic: **amended** (the researcher's verdict that the claim does not stand as written is right; the "true as" and three of the notes are wrong).

Evidence:
1. ERCOT's own primary exists, and the researcher said none was opened or found. ERCOT, "ERCOT Update", Senate Committee on Business & Commerce, Pablo Vegas, President and CEO, July 29, 2026, slide 6, "Large Load Interconnection Requests (as of June 2026)": "Key Takeaway: ERCOT is tracking approximately 474 GW of Large Loads seeking interconnection, of which ~90% are data centers." The chart gives the total as 474.7 GW and the data-centre slice as "90.2% (420812 MW)". https://www.ercot.com/files/docs/2026/07/29/ERCOT-Senate-July-29-Panel-1-Assessing-The-Grid.pdf
   Scope: told to a Senate committee (legislators, not regulators), on 29 July (not August), with data as of June 2026.
2. The governor's letter, which the researcher said was "not located in search", is linked from the Utility Dive article the researcher cited. Gov. Greg Abbott to Thomas Gleeson (PUCT) and Pablo Vegas (ERCOT), August 3, 2026: "The Electric Reliability Council of Texas (ERCOT) is currently considering approximately 474 gigawatts of requests to connect to the Texas grid. That is more than five times Texas' record peak electricity demand for ERCOT. Approximately 90 percent of the new power requests are data centers." https://gov.texas.gov/uploads/files/press/Thomas_Gleeson_Pablo_Vegas_Data_Centers_Directive_Letter_to_PUCT_ERCOT_August_2026_.pdf . The governor's press release of the same day: "ERCOT is currently considering approximately over 474 gigawatts of requests to connect to the Texas grid". https://gov.texas.gov/news/post/governor-abbott-directs-comprehensive-data-center-audit
   So in August it was the governor who wrote the figures to the regulator (and to ERCOT), not ERCOT who told the regulator.
3. ERCOT's August presentation to the regulator does not carry the figure. ERCOT, "ERCOT Updates: Good Cause Exceptions for PGRR 145; Batch Zero Verification and Audit Process; Community Impact Review Process", PUC Open Meeting, August 20, 2026 (13 slides, all read): no 474 GW total and no data-centre share appear on any slide. https://www.ercot.com/files/docs/2026/08/20/Batch-Zero-Verification-and-Audit-PUCT-Presentation.pdf
4. Utility Dive confirms the August attribution is to the letter. The researcher's citation gives the wrong headline. The page at the cited URL is headlined "ERCOT aims to complete Texas governor's data center audit by December" (Robert Walton, Aug. 21, 2026), not "ERCOT tells Texas PUC large-load queue reaches about 474 GW". It reads "approximately 90% are data centers, according to Abbott's letter to the grid operator". https://www.utilitydive.com/news/ercot-texas-puc-data-center-audit/828472/ . The 5 Aug Utility Dive piece also attributes the figures to Abbott's letter. https://www.utilitydive.com/news/texas-hits-pause-data-center-interconnections/827046/
5. KWTX (3 Aug 2026), reopened: "ERCOT is currently tracking more than 1,800 projects in the queue, representing over 474 gigawatts of electricity" and "Approximately 90% of the new power requests are data centers, Abbott said." https://www.kwtx.com/2026/08/03/data-center-approvals-texas-halted-until-audits-completed-gov-greg-abbott-says/ . The researcher quoted the first sentence but not the second, which attributes the 90% to the governor.

Where the researcher went wrong:
- Note (a): "I did not open an ERCOT document that carries the 474 GW figure". One exists (evidence 1). Overturned.
- Note (d): governor's letter "not located in search". It is linked from the researcher's own cited article (evidence 2). Overturned.
- Citation: wrong headline for the Utility Dive article (evidence 4).
- Counts line: "could not open 0" sits beside notes saying the letter and filing could not be opened. That is internally inconsistent.
- "True as" ("Reports in August 2026 put...") swaps ERCOT's primary for press reports. It is weaker than it needs to be and drops a primary that exists.
- Not settled (and the researcher did not try): ERCOT's 10 Aug 2026 filing to the PUCT ("Filed with Commission a request for three good cause exceptions for PGRR 145 along with a status update on related matters", slide 2 of the 20 Aug deck) is on the PUCT Interchange, behind the 402. It might repeat the 474 GW figure. Until someone opens it, "ERCOT told state regulators in August" is unsupported, and the July Senate deck is the primary on record.

The page should say: "In late July 2026 ERCOT told a Texas Senate committee it was tracking about 474 gigawatts of large loads seeking to connect, roughly nine in ten of them data centres." The source line on edition page 03 should change from "ERCOT TO TEXAS PUC, AUG 2026" to "ERCOT TO TEXAS SENATE, JUL 2026". If the August framing is wanted, the true version is: "In August 2026 the governor, citing ERCOT's count, told regulators..."

## 2.2. The grid's record peak demand, set in July, is about 91 gigawatts.
Researcher: holds. Skeptic: **upheld**.

Evidence:
1. Records page reopened. ERCOT, 2026 peak demand records, "Last updated: Aug 21, 2026": July table row "July 22 91,134*". Notes: "The unofficial demands reported during, or just after, an operating day are preliminary." and "The numbers may change slightly as the data goes through the settlement process." https://www.ercot.com/static-assets/data/news/content/a-peak-demand/2026/all-time-records.htm
2. A better primary that states the all-time framing outright, which the records-page table does not. ERCOT Monthly Operational Overview (July 2026), dated August 17, 2026, slide 2: "ERCOT set a new unofficial all-time peak demand record of 91,134 MW* at hour ending 18 on 7/22/2026; This is 9,400 MW more than the July 2025 demand of 81,734 MW on 7/30/2025 and 5,581 MW more than the previous all-time peak demand record of 85,508 MW set on 8/10/2023." Footnote: "* Preliminary value from August Demand and Energy 2026 report." https://www.ercot.com/files/docs/2026/08/17/ERCOT-Monthly-Operational-Overview-July-2026.pdf
3. I searched for a later peak that would overturn "set in July". ERCOT Monthly Operational Overview (August 2026), dated September 16, 2026, slide 2: "ERCOT's maximum peak demand for the month of August was 90,817 MW* at hour ending 18 on 8/24/2026". That is below 91,134 MW. https://www.ercot.com/files/docs/2026/09/16/ERCOT-Monthly-Operational-Overview-August-2026.pdf . The September 2026 overview (dated 16 Oct 2026), per a search summary I did not open, gives a September peak of 86,647 MW, also lower. https://www.ercot.com/files/docs/2026/10/16/ERCOT-Monthly-Operational-Overview-September.pdf
4. Independent check: EIA, "Hourly peak load in ERCOT set a new record, exceeding 91 GW on July 22" (3 Aug 2026): "reached a record 91.1 gigawatts (GW) on July 22, 2026". https://www.eia.gov/TodayinEnergy/detail.php?id=67906

Notes: (a) The record is ERCOT's "unofficial", preliminary hourly-integrated figure. The edition's sources note already says the peak is preliminary, so no change is needed. (b) The records page says "Last updated: Aug 21, 2026" but lists entries for 22 and 23 August. This inconsistency is in ERCOT's page and does not affect the July record. (c) The researcher's "true as" is accurate but not needed; the claim stands as written.

## 2.3. Oncor, one Dallas utility, holds nearly 300 gigawatts of requests by itself.
Researcher: holds. Skeptic: **upheld** (with a better primary and a corrected citation).

Evidence:
1. Oncor's own release, which the researcher did not open. "ONCOR REPORTS SECOND QUARTER 2026 RESULTS", dateline "DALLAS, Aug. 6, 2026 /PRNewswire/", read from two syndicated copies of the PR Newswire text (Oncor's newsroom returned 503; StreetInsider 403): "Oncor's active transmission LC&I interconnection queue included 737 requests at the end of the second quarter of 2026." ... "Those requests included approximately 282 gigawatts from data centers" ... "over 16 gigawatts of load from various other industrial sectors". 282 + 16 = about 298 GW, which is "nearly 300". https://www.stocktitan.net/news/SRE/oncor-reports-second-quarter-2026-87prshmy0zb6.html ; https://finviz.com/news/378522/oncor-reports-second-quarter-2026-results . The release's own URL (not opened, 503): https://www.oncor.com/content/oncorwww/wire/en/home/newsroom/oncor-reports-second-quarter-2026-results.html
2. Utility Dive reopened. The page's headline is "Oncor weighs impact of Texas data center freeze on nearly 300-GW load pipeline" (Emma Penrod, Aug. 7, 2026), not the researcher's "Fate of Oncor's nearly 300 GW load pipeline unclear following Texas data center..." (that is the URL slug). It reports Nye saying "service requests at that company had increased from 289 GW at the end of the first quarter, to 298 GW this past quarter." https://www.utilitydive.com/news/fate-of-oncors-nearly-300-gw-load-pipeline-unclear-following-texas-data-ce/827303/

Notes: (a) Scope: the count is Oncor's active transmission large commercial and industrial (LC&I) interconnection queue at 30 June 2026. About 282 GW of it is data centres. These are requests, not contracted or energised load. (b) "Dallas utility" is supported by the release's Dallas dateline. (c) The same release says only "approximately 44 gigawatts of large-load requests are expected to be eligible as base or studied load" in Batch Zero. That is context the edition may want, but the claim does not need it to be true. (d) The researcher's note that Oncor's requests sit inside ERCOT's 474 GW and are not additional to it is a fair reading, but I found no source that reconciles the two counts. They come from different perimeters and dates (ERCOT as of June; Oncor at end of Q2). (e) The researcher's "true as" ("about 298 gigawatts of service requests in its own Q2 2026 results") is close. But Oncor's release gives 282 GW plus over 16 GW, not a 298 GW total; 298 GW is the CEO's figure on the call as Utility Dive reports it. No rewording of the edition is needed.

## 2.4. In September ERCOT sorted its first batch: 66 gigawatts conditionally cleared, 302 gigawatts excluded.
Researcher: overstated. Skeptic: **upheld**.

Evidence (ERCOT, "Item 14: Batch Zero Update", Jeff Billo, Board of Directors Meeting, September 14-15, 2026; all 8 slides reopened, text layer and rendered image agree). https://www.ercot.com/files/docs/2026/09/11/14-Batch-Zero-Update.pdf
- Slide 1: "ERCOT has provided conditional classification for all Batch Zero Large Loads: 204 projects (66.4 GW) met the qualifications to be conditionally included as base load; 158 projects (127.9 GW) met the qualifications to be conditionally included as studied load."
- Slide 4: "ERCOT provided provisional Batch Zero Large Load project classification (base, studied, excluded) to the Interconnecting TSPs on September 3, 2026. Final project inclusion/ exclusion determination will be based on the results of the eligibility verification and audit and provided in December. (Originally, PGRR 145 required ERCOT to provide final classification on August 7, 2026.)"
- Slide 5: "362 Large Loads in either base or studied load categories"; "Large Loads: 75 MW or greater".
- Slide 6: "Base (66.4 GW, 204 projects)"; "Studied (125.4 GW, 158 projects)"; "This chart omits excluded load (302.2 GW, 373 projects)".

All four of the researcher's quotes are on the page verbatim. The scope finding stands: "66 GW conditionally cleared" names only the base-load group and leaves out the 125-128 GW conditionally included as studied load. Without it, "cleared" against "excluded" reads as if 66 GW were the whole of what got in. The slide 1 and slide 6 conflict (127.9 vs 125.4 GW) is real and in ERCOT's deck. "First batch" is a fair gloss: ERCOT calls Batch Zero its "one-time transitional study" (slide 5).

On the researcher's unreconciled note (d), that the three groups total about 496 GW against 474 GW: ERCOT's July 29 Senate deck, slide 10, "Preliminary Overview of Batch Zero Eligibility", "Data as of July 28, 2026", already shows a Batch Zero perimeter of about 498 GW (bars of 274 + 20 + 114 + 25 + 65 GW) over 688 projects (315 + 47 + 127 + 49 + 150). https://www.ercot.com/files/docs/2026/07/29/ERCOT-Senate-July-29-Panel-1-Assessing-The-Grid.pdf . So the classification covers a later and larger perimeter than the June 474.7 GW count. ERCOT gives no reconciliation, but the gap comes from different dates, not from an error in the edition's 66/302 figures.

Minor: the researcher said the deck's text layer did not extract. On my read it did, and it matches the slide images.

The researcher's "true as" is accurate. A slightly smaller change that keeps the edition's shape: "In September ERCOT provisionally sorted its first batch: 66 gigawatts conditionally included as base load and about 128 more as studied load, 302 gigawatts excluded; final decisions are due in December." The same fix applies to the chain text ("Batch Zero cleared 66 GW conditionally and excluded 302 GW.") and the slider ("Batch Zero: 66 GW conditional."), which repeat the omission.

## 2.5. On 3 August the governor paused new data-centre energisations pending an audit.
Researcher: overstated. Skeptic: **upheld** (verdict and "true as" stand; the primary the researcher called unopenable is open, and it confirms them).

Evidence:
1. The governor's letter, August 3, 2026, to PUCT Chairman Gleeson and ERCOT CEO Vegas (read in full, 2 pages): "To keep the grid stable and reliable, I am issuing the following directive: The Public Utility Commission of Texas (PUC) and ERCOT must conduct a comprehensive verification and audit of all data centers advancing through ERCOT's interconnection process before any additional data centers are approved to move forward. Any project that fails to comply with the requirements set forth by the PUC and ERCOT, and by state law, must be denied connection to the Texas grid." The letter does not use "pause", "moratorium" or "energize", and sets no completion date. https://gov.texas.gov/uploads/files/press/Thomas_Gleeson_Pablo_Vegas_Data_Centers_Directive_Letter_to_PUCT_ERCOT_August_2026_.pdf
2. Governor's press release, "Governor Abbott Directs Comprehensive Data Center Audit", August 3, 2026: "The PUCT and ERCOT must complete this audit before any data center project moves forward." Per the extraction, it does not use "pause", "moratorium", "halt", "energize" or "deadline". https://gov.texas.gov/news/post/governor-abbott-directs-comprehensive-data-center-audit
3. ERCOT's own 3 August Market Notice, M-A080326-01, "Update Regarding Batch Zero Timelines and Processes" (August 3, 2026): ERCOT is directed to "conduct a verification process before advancing any data center Large Loads" and will not issue Batch Zero classifications by August 7. Per the extraction, it does not mention an energisation pause, crypto or 75 MW. https://www.ercot.com/services/comm/mkt_notices/M-A080326-01
4. ERCOT, PUC Open Meeting deck, August 20, 2026, slide 2, "Actions taken by ERCOT": "August 3, 2026 – Issued a Market Notice pausing the Batch Zero study process and delaying final classification of all large loads" and, as a separate undated bullet, "Paused approvals to energize data centers or virtual currency mining facilities (crypto facilities) that are 75 MW or greater". Slide 7: "ERCOT has paused approvals to energize large load data centers and crypto facilities until the Batch Zero eligibility verification is complete" ... "ERCOT may approve energization for non-data center large loads. The pause does not impact medium loads". https://www.ercot.com/files/docs/2026/08/20/Batch-Zero-Verification-and-Audit-PUCT-Presentation.pdf . The Board deck (14-15 Sep), slides 3 and 4, carries the same wording the researcher quoted, which I confirmed. https://www.ercot.com/files/docs/2026/09/11/14-Batch-Zero-Update.pdf

What this settles:
- The governor ordered an audit before any further data centre is "approved to move forward". The energisation pause is ERCOT's action, described in ERCOT's own words, and it is scoped to data centres and crypto facilities of 75 MW or more. So "the governor paused new data-centre energisations" is overstated, as the researcher found.
- ERCOT's documents do not date the energisation pause to 3 August. The 3 August Market Notice does not mention it, and the 20 Aug deck lists it without a date. The researcher's "ERCOT then paused..." is correctly undated. The edition should not attach "3 August" to the energisation pause.
- The researcher's note (d), "The governor's letter itself would settle the wording; it is the one primary I could not open", is overturned. The letter was linked from Utility Dive's 21 Aug article (the researcher's own source for 2.1). It is open and it does settle the wording. The researcher's "search summary says the letter sets none" is now confirmed from the letter itself: it sets no deadline.
- Press framings ("moratorium", KWTX; "pause", several law firms) are reports, not the governor's words.

The researcher's "true as" is accurate. It can now quote the letter instead of ERCOT's paraphrase: "On 3 August the governor ordered regulators and ERCOT to audit every data centre in the interconnection queue before any more are approved to move forward; ERCOT then paused energisation approvals for data centres and crypto-mining facilities of 75 MW or more."
