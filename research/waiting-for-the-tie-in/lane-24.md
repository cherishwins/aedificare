## 24.1. The US put 174 million dollars into a gallium plant in Western Australia.
Verdict: overstated
Primary: US Department of War, "Investment in Alcoa's Wagerup gallium project", Sep 2026. https://www.war.gov/News/Releases/Release/Article/4587183/ (could not open: HTTP 403 from war.gov). Read via a secondary that quotes it: defence-industry.eu, "U.S. Department of War announces estimated $174 million equity investment to secure gallium supply through new Alcoa facility in Australia", published 1 Sep 2026. https://defence-industry.eu/u-s-department-of-war-announces-estimated-174-million-equity-investment-to-secure-gallium-supply-through-new-alcoa-facility-in-australia/
Read: 2026-11-01
Quote: "plans an estimated $174 million equity investment in a new gallium production facility" (defence-industry.eu, quoting the release); "The dollar value of the investment remains subject to exchange-rate movements" (same); "The financing structure gives the department an equity position in a facility operated by Alcoa" (same); "The plant is projected to produce 100 metric tons of gallium metal annually" (same); "a new gallium production facility at Alcoa's Wagerup alumina refinery in Australia" (same).
True as: The US Department of War announced a planned, estimated $174 million equity investment in a gallium plant at Alcoa's Wagerup refinery in Western Australia, alongside Japan and Australia; the plant is projected to make 100 tonnes of gallium a year.
Notes: Scope. The money is "plans" and "estimated", with the US-dollar value moving with exchange rates. It is not shown as paid in. The plant is projected output, not running output. Japan and Australia are partners (Export Finance Australia, the Japanese government and Sojitz). Secondary searches also show a Business Wire release of 14 Jul 2026 announcing a final investment decision and an Alcoa groundbreaking on 24 Aug 2026, so "announced September 2026" is the date of the US announcement, not of the project. The war.gov primary could not be opened (403), so the quotes are from the secondary. The edition's "Confirmed" label is too strong for an estimated, planned figure.

Read: 2026-11-01

True as: MP Materials said in its FY2025 10-K that it expects to commission its heavy rare-earth (HREE) facility at Mountain Pass in 2026.

The edition's cited source 88 (Creamer Media, 26 Mar 2026, https://newsletter-mw.creamermedia.com/article/us-achieves-first-domestic-heavy-rare-earth-oxide-output-in-decades-2026-03-26) is about Energy Fuels' White Mesa mill in Utah, not MP Mountain Pass, so it does not support this claim. Could not settle whether MP has started HREE separation as of 1 Nov 2026; the Note 15 text would settle it.

## 24.3. 2028 MP's Texas magnet plant commissions, 7,000 tonnes a year.
Verdict: holds
Primary: MP Materials Corp., Form 10-K for fiscal 2025. https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1801368/000180136826000008/mp-20251231.htm
Read: 2026-11-01
Quote: "The 10X Facility is expected to begin commissioning in 2028" (Factors Affecting Our Performance, "Development of Our Downstream Manufacturing Capabilities"); "it will produce an estimated 7,000 MTs of magnets per year" (same section).
True as: MP Materials expects its 10X magnet plant to begin commissioning in 2028 and to produce an estimated 7,000 tonnes of magnets a year.
Notes: The 10-K does not give the 10X site location; the Texas siting comes from secondary reporting (Northlake, Texas, in the AllianceTexas development, less than 10 miles from the Independence facility in Fort Worth; one press report puts the project at $1.3 billion, the 10-K says more than $1.25 billion). Secondary source: REBusiness, "MP Materials selects AllianceTexas for $1.3B rare earth magnet manufacturing site" (https://rebusinessonline.com/mp-materials-selects-alliancetexas-for-1-3b-rare-earth-magnet-manufacturing-site/), not opened directly. The edition's cited source 87 is the 10-K itself, which supports the 2028 date and the 7,000 tonnes. "Commissions" is a forecast; the rewording keeps it as MP's expectation. The 10-K's separate Independence expansion is 3,000 tonnes a year, not 7,000, so do not merge the two.

## 24.4. 2027 Energy Fuels Phase 1 commercial: up to 1,000 tonnes of NdPr a year.
Verdict: wrong
Primary: Energy Fuels Inc., press release "Energy Fuels Achieves Commercial Production of On-Spec Separated Rare Earths at its White Mesa Mill in Utah, While Simultaneously Advancing Uranium Production", 10 Jun 2024. https://investors.energyfuels.com/2024-06-10-Energy-Fuels-Achieves-Commercial-Production-of-On-Spec-Separated-Rare-Earths-at-its-White-Mesa-Mill-in-Utah,-While-Simultaneously-Advancing-Uranium-Production
Read: 2026-11-01
Quote: "it has achieved commercial production of separated neodymium-praseodymium" (10 Jun 2024 release); "850 to 1,000 metric tons ("tonnes") of NdPr per year" (same); "The Company expects to have commercial quantities of separated NdPr available for shipment by the end of June 2024." (same). Energy Fuels 10-Q for the period ended 30 Jun 2026 (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001385849/000138584926000029/efr-20260630.htm), Note 1: "the Mill has produced separated neodymium/praseodymium ("NdPr") at commercial scale."
True as: Energy Fuels' Phase 1 circuit at White Mesa, Utah, has produced NdPr at commercial scale since mid-2024, at a design capacity of 850 to 1,000 tonnes a year. Energy Fuels' planned Phase 1 modifications, aimed at heavy rare earths, are expected to be operational in late 2027 to early 2028.
Notes: The "2027 commercial" date is wrong because Phase 1 was already commercial in 2024. The 2027 to early 2028 date is for Energy Fuels' Phase 1 REE circuit modifications, as stated in its 11 Jun 2026 release (reported via stockwatch and secondary coverage; the release itself was not opened, and the 10-Q excerpt read did not contain the timing sentence). Per the same reporting, the modifications are meant to enable heavy-REE output, and the Phase 2 expansion would bring total White Mesa REE capacity to nearly 6,300 tonnes a year. The edition's cited source 88 (Creamer Media) is about Energy Fuels' heavy rare earth output at White Mesa (terbium and dysprosium oxide, Mar 2026), not the Phase 1 NdPr timeline. A Zacks article gives Phase 1 as about 1,000 tonnes; the filings give 850 to 1,000. Use the range.
