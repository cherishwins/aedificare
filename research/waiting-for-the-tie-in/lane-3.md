## 3.1. [03 · The chain (Grid tie-in)] about 20 months in Texas, now paused (attributed to chain source: ERCOT) (cites none in list)
Verdict: wrong
Primary: Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory, "Queued Up: 2025 Edition" (cited via Carbon Direct, "Carbon Direct releases new analysis of power grid interconnection queues, PJM, ERCOT", 14 May 2026, now at relae.co). LBNL's report itself was not opened. Carbon Direct page: https://relae.co/press/carbon-direct-releases-new-analysis-of-power-grid-interconnection-queues-pjm-ercot . Pause: Utility Dive, "ERCOT, Texas PUC data center audit", Aug 2026 (edition source 02). https://www.utilitydive.com/news/ercot-texas-puc-data-center-audit/828472/
Read: 2026-11-01
Quote: "However, average wait times are 25 months nationally, 20 months in ERCOT, and 40 months in PJM." (Carbon Direct, relae.co mirror)
Quote: "On Aug. 3, Texas Gov. Greg Abbott, R, called for a moratorium on new data center interconnections" (Utility Dive)
True as: Generation and storage projects in ERCOT's interconnection queue averaged about 20 months' wait (LBNL, Queued Up 2025 edition, as cited by Carbon Direct in May 2026). Separately, on 3 Aug 2026 the Texas governor called for a moratorium on new data-centre interconnections, pending an ERCOT audit.
Notes: The 20-month figure is an average for the general queue, not for large loads, and it is not an ERCOT figure; the attribution to ERCOT is wrong as to source and population. The Carbon Direct page says the 20-month average is for the generation queue ("Their context is the generation interconnection queue, which includes both generation and storage") and gives 36 to 48 months for active projects in data-centre load-growth zones (Carbon Direct's own analysis). The "paused" wording is a governor's call for a moratorium per Utility Dive; the edition's own text says "paused new data-centre energisations". Not opened: LBNL report, ERCOT's own filings. No ERCOT source found giving a 20-month large-load timeline (searched "ERCOT large load interconnection 20 months"; not found).

## 3.2. [03 · The chain (Connection study)] $100,000 study fee in Texas (attributed to chain source: ERCOT; PJM) (cites none in list)
Verdict: overstated
Primary: Public Utility Commission of Texas, Project No. 58481 final large-load interconnection rule, as reported by Utility Dive, "Texas PUC adopts data center interconnection rules", 21 Sep 2026. https://utilitydive.com/news/texas-puc-adopts-data-center-interconnection-rules/830899 . Statute: Texas SB 6 (89th Leg., 2025), enrolled bill, https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/89R/billtext/pdf/SB00006F.pdf (PDF not readable through the fetch tool; see Notes).
Read: 2026-11-01
Quote: "the adopted rule settled on a flat $100,000 study fee for all large load customers." (Utility Dive, 21 Sep 2026)
Quote: "A flat screening study fee of at least $100,000." (Foley, "Public Utility Commission of Texas issues proposed rules for large load interconnection", Mar 2026, describing the enacted SB 6 requirement)
True as: Texas's PUC adopted a flat $100,000 study fee for large-load customers in September 2026, the minimum SB 6 (2025) requires for initial transmission screening studies.
Notes: The fee is a PUC rule fee, not an ERCOT fee; ERCOT is not the source of the number. The March 2026 proposal was different ("Study fees of $100,000 to $300,000 depending on the size of the project", Foley, describing the proposed Intermediate Agreement requirement), and the final rule replaced the proposed $50,000 per MW non-refundable fee with the flat $100,000 (Utility Dive). PJM was not checked; the claim lists PJM as an attributed source and the PJM figure was not opened, so the PJM attribution is unverified. The statutory wording was taken from the search-tool summary of the enrolled bill and from Foley; the enrolled PDF returned only compressed binary to WebFetch, so I could not quote the statute verbatim. The Utility Dive article's date is 21 Sep 2026 and says "voted Friday"; the exact adoption date is not in the article (most likely 18 Sep 2026; unconfirmed). White & Case alert (cited as the 18 Sep date source in search) returned HTTP 403 and was not opened.

## 3.4. [06 · Three grids, barely touching] Texas keeps its own interconnection to stay outside federal transmission rules. That is why it connects faster, and why nobody can rescue it when it runs short. (attributed to none) (cites 32)
Verdict: not in the source
Primary: ERCOT, "ERCOT Grid Insights: Electricity Connection and Transfer", May 2025 (edition cite 32). https://www.ercot.com/files/docs/2025/05/12/ERCOT-Grid-Insights-Electricity-Connection-and-Transfer.pdf
Read: 2026-11-01 (read as page images, 2 pages, through the Read tool, because WebFetch returned binary)
Quote: "Grid Significance: DC Ties support ERCOT's ability to quickly transfer power with other power grids without interrupting power, allowing electricity to pass back and forth to maintain grid reliability." (p. 1, DC Ties section)
True as: ERCOT's transmission stays inside Texas, which places it outside FERC's federal transmission jurisdiction under the Federal Power Act. (Secondary support only; see Notes.) The "connects faster" and "nobody can rescue it" parts are the edition's own reasoning and are not the source's claims.
Notes: The cited ERCOT Grid Insights issue does not mention FERC, federal jurisdiction, the Federal Power Act, or faster connection; it covers DC ties, switchable generation and block load transfers. Its only relevant point is that DC ties and block load transfers let ERCOT move power with other grids during scarcity (p. 1 and p. 2). FERC's ERCOT page (ferc.gov/industries-data/electric/electric-power-markets/ercot) returned HTTP 403 and was not opened. A search summary attributed to FERC says transmission "occurring wholly within ERCOT falls outside its authority under FPA sections 203, 205, and 206", but that is a snippet, not a read page. "Connects faster" is not supported by any source read. The "nobody can rescue it" clause is opinion and is not supported by the source. Note that the edition's own text (section 06) adds a claim of an 820 MW plus 400 MW tie capacity, which is verified under 3.5.

## 3.5. [06 · Three grids, barely touching] Its direct-current ties to the rest of the country total 820 megawatts, plus 400 to Mexico, about 1.3 percent of its peak. (attributed to ERCOT) (cites 32, 03)
Verdict: holds
Primary: ERCOT, "ERCOT Grid Insights: Electricity Connection and Transfer", May 2025, p. 1, DC Ties section. https://www.ercot.com/files/docs/2025/05/12/ERCOT-Grid-Insights-Electricity-Connection-and-Transfer.pdf
Read: 2026-11-01
Quote: "two DC Ties with the Southwest Power Pool (SPP) (820 MW) and a DC Tie and a VFT with CENACE, Mexico's independent operator of the national grid (400 MW)." (p. 1)
True as: Texas's DC ties to the rest of the US total 820 MW (two ties with the Southwest Power Pool), plus 400 MW to Mexico (a DC tie and a variable frequency transformer with CENACE). Against the July 2026 record peak of about 91 GW, the 1,220 MW total is about 1.3 percent.
Notes: The 820 and 400 MW figures match ERCOT's own map on p. 1 (North 220, East 600, Laredo 100, Railroad 300). The "about 1.3 percent of its peak" is the edition's own arithmetic (1,220 MW against about 91,000 MW), not a figure in the ERCOT document; ERCOT's document gives no percentage. The 91 GW peak comes from the edition's cite 03 (ERCOT preliminary 2026 records), which I did not open. The May 2025 document is older than the edition; the MW figures are as of that issue. Southern Spirit (up to 3,000 MW, "proposed") is excluded from the 820 and 400 totals, as in the source.

## 3.6. [06 · Three grids, barely touching] A 3 gigawatt line to Mississippi, Southern Spirit, is scheduled for service somewhere between 2029 and 2032, depending on which of its own documents you read. (attributed to Pattern Energy's own documents) (cites 33)
Verdict: overstated
Primary: Pattern Energy, "Southern Spirit Transmission" project page (edition cite 33). https://www.patternenergy.com/projects/southern-spirit-transmission/ (undated page; read 2026-11-01)
Read: 2026-11-01
Quote: "Target Full Construction Start" (2029) and "Target In-Service Date" (2032), under "Projected Schedule"
Quote: "Southern Spirit could be operational as early as late 2029." (FAQ, "What is the history of the Project and how was the route selected?")
Quote: "Southern Spirit will begin in Louisiana at the Texas border and extend into Mississippi." (Project Benefits)
Quote: "~3,000 MW high voltage direct current (HVDC) transmission line." (Project Overview)
True as: Pattern Energy's Southern Spirit project page gives a 2032 target in-service date and says the line "could be operational as early as late 2029"; the project would run from Louisiana at the Texas border into Mississippi, about 3,000 MW. The 2029 date on the page is the target start of construction, not service.
Notes: "Scheduled for service between 2029 and 2032" overstates the page: 2032 is the scheduled in-service date, and late 2029 is an "as early as" possibility. The 2029 figure is a construction start on the same schedule. "Depending on which of its own documents you read" describes a single web page with two statements, not separate documents. The page does not say "3 gigawatt"; it says ~3,000 MW, which is the same figure. The page is undated, so I cannot say when it was last revised. ERCOT's May 2025 issue describes Southern Spirit as "proposed for up to 3,000 MW" (p. 1), consistent with the project being at proposal stage on both sources. No regulatory approval is mentioned on the page; construction "will commence after the project obtains the necessary regulatory approvals and secures all land rights" (FAQ).
