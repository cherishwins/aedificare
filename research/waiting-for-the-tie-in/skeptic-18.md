## 18.1. Up to 80 percent of newly built Chinese computing centres sat unused in 2025 (MIT Technology Review)
Skeptic verdict: **amended** (the researcher's "overstated" verdict and true-as stand; note 3 is wrong and must go)

Source reopened: https://www.technologyreview.com/2025/03/26/1113802/china-ai-data-centers-unused/amp/ (also the canonical URL: byline Caiwei Chen, dated March 26, 2025)
Verbatim, confirmed:
- "The local Chinese outlets Jiazi Guangnian and 36Kr report that up to 80% of China's newly built" / "computing resources remain unused."
- "In 2023 and 2024, over 500 new data center projects were announced everywhere from Inner Mongolia" / "to Guangdong, according to KZ Consulting, a market research firm."
- "According to the China Communications Industry Association Data Center Committee, a state-affiliated" / "industry association, at least 150 of the newly built data centers were finished and running by the end of 2024."

Findings:
1. All three quotes are on the page, verbatim. The context does not change them. The attribution to two local outlets (note 1), the unit "computing resources" rather than "centres" (note 2) and the period stretch (note 4) are all confirmed. The article is dated 26 March 2025. It gives no 2025 measurement, so "sat unused in 2025" is not in the source.
2. **Note 3 is wrong.** The edition does not drop "up to". The PDF prints "UP TO" above the 80% figure, and the lane file's own claim line begins "Up to 80 percent". Strike note 3.
3. Note 5 ("at least one outlet puts the figure at 'over half'") rests on a search summary the researcher did not open. The MIT page has no "over half" figure (WebFetch: "The article contains no 'over half' figure"). I could not run a search to find or rule out that outlet. Keep note 5 out of anything printed unless a source is opened.
4. Better primary: the 80% figure starts with Jiazi Guangnian and 36Kr, not MIT. I could not search for the original Chinese articles. That gap is still open.

Notes should read: delete (3). The rest stands. True-as stands as written: "In March 2025, MIT Technology Review reported that local Chinese outlets Jiazi Guangnian and 36Kr said up to 80% of China's newly built computing resources remained unused."

## 18.3. and the car industry's margin fell to 4.1 percent, a record low (Caixin)
Skeptic verdict: **amended** (the researcher's "holds" is too generous: the same sentence of the same source reports a lower margin after 2025)

Source reopened: https://www.caixinglobal.com/2026-04-15/chinese-automakers-report-shrinking-profits-for-2025-amid-brutal-price-war-102434087.html ("Chinese Automakers Report Shrinking Profits for 2025 Amid Brutal Price War", "By Yu Cong", "Published: Apr. 15, 2026 12:13 a.m. GMT+8")
Verbatim, confirmed (one sentence, given in two fragments): "The industry's average profit margin sank to a historic low of 4.1% in 2025" / "and dropped further to 2.9% in the first two months of 2026, according to the China Passenger Car Association."
Also verbatim: "Industry-wide vehicle sales in the country fell by more than 20% in the first quarter of 2026" / "compounding the financial pain revealed in recent annual reports."

Findings:
1. The quotes are on the page, but the researcher cut the 4.1% sentence at "in 2025". **The rest of that sentence changes the claim.** CPCA, via Caixin, says the margin "dropped further to 2.9% in the first two months of 2026." The edition is dated 15 October 2026 and gives no period: "the car industry's margin fell to 4.1 percent, a record low". A reader takes that as the current low. By the source's own sentence, 4.1% was the record low for a full year (2025) and has since been undercut. The claim needs its year, and the 2.9% reading belongs beside it.
2. Both sentences sit in the free part of the page, before the paywall. The Digest Hub ("AI generated, for reference only") repeats 4.1% and adds no definition.
3. Note 3 is confirmed. The article does not define the margin (sales or net; listed firms or all; passenger cars or all vehicles).
4. Note 1's 36Kr "record low" wording and note 3's Gasgoo "sales profit margin" wording were not opened by the researcher, and no URL is given. I could not reopen them (search budget exhausted). Unverified. Note 4 (no CPCA release found) could not be retested by search. It is still open.

The page should say instead: "the car industry's average profit margin fell to a record low of 4.1 percent in 2025, and to 2.9 percent in January and February 2026 (China Passenger Car Association, via Caixin)."
True-as should read: "Caixin reported, citing the China Passenger Car Association, that the auto industry's average profit margin sank to a historic low of 4.1% in 2025 and dropped further to 2.9% in the first two months of 2026."
Verdict field should read: "holds with scope: 2025 only; a lower figure (2.9%, Jan to Feb 2026) is in the same sentence."

Skeptic verdict: **amended** (the figure holds; the true-as states it as NBS's own words, but the NBS release was never opened)
