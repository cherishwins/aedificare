## 8.1. 128 / 144 weeks; prices up 77 percent since 2019 (cites 15)
Ruling: **amended**
Evidence: POWER, Sonal C. Patel, "Transformers in 2026: Shortage, Scramble, or Self-Inflicted Crisis?", Friday 2 Jan 2026. https://www.powermag.com/transformers-in-2026-shortage-scramble-or-self-inflicted-crisis/
- Both of the researcher's quotes are on the page, verbatim. The text just before the lead-time quote is: "distribution transformer availability has improved, large units remain locked above two years," then "with power transformers averaging 128 weeks and GSUs 144 weeks in WoodMac's second quarter 2025 survey".
- The text just before the price quote is: "WoodMac's second quarter 2025 survey. Meanwhile, costs have also climbed. Since 2019," then "unit prices have increased 77% for power transformers and 45% for GSUs". The paragraph ends: "and 45% for GSUs, with some classes of distribution transformers rising as much as 95%." The paragraph has no hyperlinks.
- Wood Mackenzie press release, 14 Aug 2025 (https://www.woodmac.com/press-releases/power-transformers-and-distribution-transformers-will-face-supply-deficits-of-30-and-10-in-2025/): I confirm the researcher's finding that it gives no 128-week, 144-week, 77 percent or 45 percent figures. It mentions lead times but gives no number for them.
Problem: The researcher's Primary line says the 77/45 percent figures are "reported by POWER from Wood Mackenzie's Q2 2025 survey". POWER names Wood Mackenzie as the source of the lead times only. The price sentence comes next, after "Meanwhile, costs have also climbed", and names no source. So pinning the 77 percent on Wood Mackenzie is an inference from where the sentence sits, not something the page says. The edition credits all three figures to Wood Mackenzie. The verdict "overstated" and the scope notes (77 percent is power transformers only; lead times are a Q2 2025 reading) still stand.
Better primary: none reached. I could not open Wood Mackenzie's Q2 2025 survey, and I could not search for it (budget used up). The WoodMac press-release index loads its list by script and showed nothing.
What the page should say instead: "Power transformers averaged about 128 weeks and generator step-up units about 144 weeks in Wood Mackenzie's Q2 2025 survey, as reported by POWER (2 Jan 2026). POWER also reports that power transformer unit prices have risen 77 percent since 2019 (GSUs 45 percent), but gives no source for the price figures." The Primary line should say the same: only the lead times are tied to Wood Mackenzie in POWER.

## 8.2. One American company makes grain-oriented electrical steel (cites 16)
Ruling: **amended** (the verdict "holds" stands; the primary and the attribution change)
Evidence:
- SteelOrbis, 7 Oct 2026 (https://www.steelorbis.com/steel-news/latest-news/cleveland-cliffs-hosts-us-energy-officials-at-butler-works-to-showcase-goes-expansion-1482174.htm): the quote is on the page, verbatim: "Butler Works is the sole US producer of GOES, a material used in transformers throughout the US power system." It is the article's own narration, not in quotation marks. The visit was on 6 October. No other US GOES maker is named.
- Better primary, the company's own page. Cleveland-Cliffs, Butler Works operations page, https://www.clevelandcliffs.com/operations/steelmaking/butler-works : "TRAN-COR® is the only high-permeability electrical steel made in the United States, used in power transformers." and "Regular Grain-Oriented (RGO) products are used for power and distribution transformers." / "Cleveland-Cliffs is the only producer of this in the United States."
- Cleveland-Cliffs, Zanesville Works page, https://www.clevelandcliffs.com/operations/steelmaking/zanesville-works : "Regular Grain-Oriented (RGO) electric steels for power and distribution transformers" (opening clause, quoted as fetched). So Cliffs also lists RGO at its Zanesville, Ohio plant, not only at Butler.
Problem: The researcher's notes say no Cliffs statement was found and that "sole US producer" is unverified as a Cliffs claim. Cliffs makes the claim itself, on its own Butler Works page, for both grain-oriented grades it lists (high-permeability TRAN-COR and regular grain-oriented). The page never uses the words "grain-oriented electrical steel" as a whole phrase, but those two grades are the grain-oriented grades. Two limits remain. First, it is the company's own claim; no independent count of producers was reached, and I could not search for one. Second, Cliffs lists RGO at Zanesville as well, so "at its Butler Works" names the steelmaking site, not every plant involved.
What the page should say instead: "Cleveland-Cliffs says it is the only US producer of grain-oriented electrical steel: its Butler Works page calls its TRAN-COR the only high-permeability electrical steel made in the United States and says Cliffs is the only US producer of regular grain-oriented steel (also reported by SteelOrbis, 7 Oct 2026)." The Primary line should be the Cliffs Butler Works page, with SteelOrbis as a report of it.

The Primary should be the people.cn URL, with the Henan mirror as a copy. The IEA/China Daily lead in note (2) was not opened by either of us, and I could not search for it.

## 8.4. US imports 72% of its need (Goldman Sachs; cites none)
Ruling: **amended** (the verdict "could not settle" stands, since the Goldman note is still unread; note (1) is wrong, and there is a primary figure the owner should see)
Evidence:
- Deep News on Tiger Brokers, "Global Transformer Shortage Intensifies as China's Exports Surge 61% in Two Months, with US Shipments Soaring 182%", 27 Mar 2026, https://www.itiger.com/news/1112652422 : "China currently supplies about 4% of US demand, with imports overall meeting about 72% of the need." The same page says "According to a Goldman Sachs research report dated March 26," and also: "given the US's overall import dependency of approximately 72%"; "Previously expecting the deficit to narrow from the current 72% to 57% by 2028,"; "The bank has raised its forecast for US reliance on transformer imports from 57% to approximately 60%." The page gives no report title and no analysts, and it never says whether the 72 percent covers all transformers or power transformers only.
- Wood Mackenzie, 14 Aug 2025 (primary), https://www.woodmac.com/press-releases/power-transformers-and-distribution-transformers-will-face-supply-deficits-of-30-and-10-in-2025/ : "In 2025, imports will account for an estimated 80% of US power transformer supply" / "and 50% of the distribution transformer supply."
Problem: (1) Note (1) says "the word 'need' is not in the quote". It is. The secondary page says "imports overall meeting about 72% of the need", which is almost word for word the edition's line. The researcher missed that sentence. (2) The Goldman note was not reached. Goldman research notes are normally client-only, I could not search for a public copy, and no wall was worked round. (3) A primary source, Wood Mackenzie, gives a different import share: about 80 percent for power transformers and 50 percent for distribution transformers in 2025. The scopes differ (supply by unit class, as an estimate for 2025, against Goldman's undefined "need"), so this does not overturn the 72 percent. But the edition should not present 72 percent as the single settled figure.
What the page should say instead: "Not settled from a primary. Deep News (27 Mar 2026) reports that a 26 March 2026 Goldman Sachs research note puts imports at about 72 percent of US transformer need; the note was not reached and does not define the scope. Wood Mackenzie (14 Aug 2025) separately estimates imports at about 80 percent of US power transformer supply and 50 percent of distribution transformer supply in 2025." Note (1) should be struck and replaced with that sentence.

## 8.6. New plants in NC, SC and VA, 2027 to 2028 (cites none)
Ruling: **amended** (verdict "overstated" and True as upheld; note (3) is corrected)
Evidence:
- POWER, 2 Jan 2026 (URL above): "Siemens Energy is building its first U.S. large power transformer plant in Charlotte, North Carolina" and "expected to begin production in early 2027"; "Eaton has committed $340 million to a three-phase transformer facility in South Carolina, also targeting a 2027 start."; "including a medium-power facility in Goldsboro, North Carolina" (Prolec GE, no date); "including a $457 million facility in South Boston, Virginia" and "set to become the nation's largest large power transformer plant by 2028". All verbatim as fetched.
- Hitachi Energy release, 29 Jun 2026, at the URL given and at its English twin, https://www.hitachienergy.com/news-and-events/press-releases/2026/06/hitachi-energy-breaks-ground-on-the-nation-s-largest-facility-for-the-production-of-large-power-transformers-in-south-boston-virginia : "The $457 million previously announced investment advances the construction of a new facility"; "Hitachi Energy today broke ground on a major expansion of its South Boston campus"; "The expansion will take place at Hitachi Energy's existing South Boston campus". The release names no year in the future (the only year in it is 1968). It does not use "complete" or "operational"; it says only that the groundbreaking "marked the start of construction". The researcher's negative (the release gives no completion date) is confirmed.
Problem: (1) Note (3) says "new plant" overstates the Hitachi project. Hitachi's own release says both things: "construction of a new facility" and an "expansion" at its "existing South Boston campus". The fair wording is a new facility on an existing campus. "New plant" is Hitachi's own framing, not an overstatement. The overstatement in the claim is that these plants are announced or under construction, not operating, as note (1) says. (2) Note (5) credits the 2028 date to "a spokesperson quoted in secondary coverage (plantservices/tdworld-type reports)" without naming a page. POWER's sentence gives no spokesperson, and no such page was cited or opened. The 2028 date should rest on POWER alone unless a named page is added. (3) I could not open the Siemens Energy or Eaton announcements themselves, and could not search for them or for any change to their dates since 2 Jan 2026. The 2027 dates rest on POWER.
What the page should say instead: the True as stays, with "as reported by POWER (2 Jan 2026)" added at the end. Note (3) should read: "Hitachi calls the South Boston project 'the construction of a new facility' and an 'expansion' at its 'existing South Boston campus'; it is a new facility on an existing site." Note (5) should read: "The 2028 date is from POWER; the Hitachi release of 29 June 2026 gives no completion date."
