## For the owner

- **The 20 months is gone from the chain.** It was generators' average wait for an interconnection agreement in LBNL's 2025 edition, not a data centre's wait for a tie-in. If a Texas number is wanted there, it has to come from LBNL's Queued Up 2026 (source 06), which answered 403 here; a blog (Reslink, 24 Oct 2026) says the 2026 edition gives ERCOT "24 months from request to agreement", unconfirmed. Carousel slide 04 cites "LBNL QUEUED UP 2026". With the number gone, the tie-in piece's src may not need LBNL for Texas; whether it still needs it for northern Virginia is lane 2's.
- **"That is why it connects faster" was the author's inference under an F mark**, and it is taken out. LBNL, as pv magazine reports it (read by the skeptic only), credits ERCOT's speed in part to its connect-and-manage design, not to its being outside federal rules. If the owner wants the causal claim, it goes back marked I and as the author's view.
- **"The help it can draw from its neighbours is small"** is a judgment from the figures (1.2 GW of ties against a 91 GW peak, plus about 3.6 GW of switchable generation). It is printed under the paragraph's F mark; the owner may prefer it marked I.
- **Motive for Texas's separate grid** now rests on the Texas Tribune, a secondary. FERC's own ERCOT page answered 403 to everyone and was not worked round.
- **"Direct-current ties ... plus 400 to Mexico"** is left as written. 100 MW of the 400 is a variable frequency transformer, which ERCOT says "acts similar to a DC Tie". The stat block now says "tie capacity" to stay true; the prose could say the same if the owner wants it exact.
