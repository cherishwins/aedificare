## 12.1. Cleanview: about 90 GW at 59 US data centres, more than a quarter of planned capacity, about 2 GW running
Verdict: **upheld**
Reopened: https://cleanview.co/reports/behind-the-meter-data-centers (fetched 2026-11-01; no publication or last-updated date on the page)
Evidence:
- "we identified 59 data centers with a combined capacity of ~90 GW" and subtitle "An analysis of 59 behind-the-meter data centers with ~90 GW of announced capacity"
- "That represents more than 25% of all planned data center capacity in the United States"
- "only about 2 GW is actually operating today—2.2% of the total."
- Scope check on the unit, which the researcher did not do: the page calls the 90 GW data-centre capacity in its summary ("59 data centers with a combined capacity of ~90 GW") but generation capacity in its status section ("Of the roughly 90 GW of generation capacity we've identified across all projects"), and it works out the 2.2% on that generation basis. So the edition's "90 gigawatts of on-site generation" matches what the source itself says, even though the source never squares its two framings.
- Date check: the page says "as of mid-2026, approximately 2 GW of behind-the-meter generation capacity is online across four projects", and a satellite caption reads "Captured May 13, 2026." That supports the edition's stat line "Cleanview, mid 2026". The researcher's "around May 2026" can be firmed up to the page's own "as of mid-2026".
Rewording: none needed. If the owner wants the date in the sentence, the smallest change is "About 2 gigawatts of it was running as of mid-2026", since "today" in an edition dated 15 Oct 2026 points to a mid-2026 snapshot.

## 12.2. Slider notes: "Cleanview: 2.8 to 3.2 GW by end 2026." [also "Cleanview: 5 to 13 GW by 2027."]
Verdict: **amended** (the 2026 note holds; the 2027 note, which the researcher did not check, is wrong in scope)
Reopened: https://cleanview.co/reports/behind-the-meter-data-centers (fetched 2026-11-01)
Evidence, 2026 note: "We expect ~3 GW of capacity to be online by the end of 2026" and "By the end of 2026, total operating capacity should grow to between 2.8 and 3.2 GW." This matches the 'btm' slider, "On-site generation running, 2026". Upheld as a forecast.
Evidence, 2027 note: the claim record (claims.json 12.2) includes a second note, "Cleanview: 5 to 13 GW by 2027.", which lane-12 never addresses. In the edition it sits on the 'ramp' slider, labelled "Added each year" (data.ts line 44). The source's figures are cumulative totals, not annual additions:
- "another 10 GW could come online in 2027, bringing the cumulative total to 13 GW" (the page adds that this assumes "all projects with signed tenants meet their timelines", and it doubts that will happen)
- "cumulative behind-the-meter capacity could be as low as 5 GW by the end of 2027"
Read as an annual addition, Cleanview's range is about 2 to 10 GW added in 2027 (5 or 13 GW cumulative, less the 2.8 to 3.2 GW expected at end 2026). The source gives only the 10 GW upper figure in those words.
What the page should say: the 2026 note stays as it is. Change the 2027 note to "Cleanview: 5 to 13 GW cumulative by end 2027." Or, if the slider is to keep the label "Added each year", use "Cleanview: up to 10 GW added in 2027." Either way the note and the slider label must measure the same thing.

## 12.4. "xAI's Memphis-area campuses run roughly 1.5 gigawatts of gas turbines, helped by a Mississippi rule that allows twelve months of operation without a permit." (cites 30)
Verdict: **amended**. Lane 12 is right that the "Mississippi rule" is misattributed. Lane 12 is wrong that the 1.5 GW figure is "not in any page I could open". It is on the Cleanview public page, which the researcher fetched for 12.1.
Evidence for 1.5 GW (overturns lane-12 note 3):
- Cleanview, https://cleanview.co/reports/behind-the-meter-data-centers (public page, fetched 2026-11-01): "Colossus 1 and 2 outside Memphis account for 1,498 MW of operating gas turbine capacity". On the same page: "The vast majority of that belongs to xAI." The figure has grown "as xAI added dozens of temporary mobile turbines at the Southaven power plant site". The image caption reads "Satellite images of Colossus 1 and 2 outside of Memphis, TN. Captured May 13, 2026." The status is given "as of mid-2026".
- So "roughly 1.5 gigawatts" of operating turbines at the two Memphis-area Colossus campuses is supported, as Cleanview's mid-2026 count. It is source 28, not source 30. The "helped by" link to temporary turbines at Southaven is also Cleanview's own framing.
Evidence on the permit rule (upholds lane-12 note 1):
- Mississippi Free Press, Mar 6 2026, updated Apr 22 2026, https://www.mississippifreepress.org/xai-faces-fierce-opposition-over-southaven-mississippi-power-plant-permit/: MDEQ "has allowed xAI to forgo permits for the turbines currently housed at the Southaven plant," claiming that "federal rules exempt turbines classified as "temporary-mobile" engines" from permits "for up to a year." The article credits the exemption to federal rules, not Mississippi law, and names no specific regulation. It does not use "twelve months".
- Disputed: "environmental groups say [the new EPA language] affirms that temporary gas turbines do require permits", and "The update puts xAI's Southaven turbines at odds with federal law, the groups allege".
Source 30: lane 12 says the GEM page "was read and contains no turbine count". In fact https://gem.wiki/Colossus_2_power_station does not resolve to an article at all. It returned the GEM.wiki main page ("a project of Global Energy Monitor"; "last edited on 1 November 2026"), and https://www.gem.wiki/index.php?title=Colossus_2_power_station returned HTTP 404. Source 30 supports no part of the sentence and should be replaced or removed. The owner may want to check whether the page existed when the edition was published (15 Oct 2026). My search budget was gone, so I could not look for a renamed GEM article.
Not settled: I could not check whether the 1,498 MW had changed after mid-2026 (no search budget). Lane 12's other turbine counts (27, 35, 41 and 69) count different things, and none of them overturns Cleanview's capacity figure.
What the page should say: "xAI's two Colossus campuses outside Memphis ran about 1.5 gigawatts of gas turbines in mid-2026, Cleanview counts, a total that grew as xAI added dozens of temporary mobile turbines at Southaven, Mississippi. The state regulator lets those run without a permit, reading federal rules as exempting them for up to a year; environmental groups dispute that reading." Cite 28 and the Mississippi Free Press article; drop source 30.

## 12.5. "Meta is paying the full revenue requirement of three new Entergy gas plants in Louisiana for fifteen years." (cites 31)
Verdict: **amended**. Lane 12's "overstated" stands. Its claim that the LPSC minutes could not be read is wrong, and its True-as needs two fixes.
Reopened:
- Cited source 31, https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/entergy-obtains-approval-to-construct-three-gas-facilities-to-serve-metas-2gw-data-center-in-louisiana returned "HTTP 403 Forbidden" to me as well. Not read, and not routed around.
- LPSC minutes, Aug 20 2025, https://lpsc.louisiana.gov/docs/minutes/August-20-2025-Minutes.pdf. Lane 12 says this "returned binary PDF data that could not be read, and the docket text was not found in it." That is wrong. The PDF downloads (115 KB, 7 pages) and reads normally as a document. Docket U-37425 is item Ex. 5 on pages 2 and 3: "U-37425 - Entergy Louisiana, LLC, ex parte. In re: Application for approval of generation and transmission resources in connection with service to a single customer for a project in North Louisiana." Commissioner Campbell's motion reads "I move that we accept Staff's recommendation and approve the settlement entered into by Entergy Louisiana, LPSC Staff, Sierra Club, Walmart, and Southern Renewable Energy Association." The outcome: "the Commission voted to accept the Settlement Agreement filed into the record on August 7, 2025. The motion passed 4:1", with "Commissioner Lewis objecting." Campbell's motion speaks of "numerous protective conditions to mitigate the risk that other electric customers throughout Louisiana will be adversely impacted". The minutes do not state the settlement's payment terms, the 15 years, or a revenue requirement. They confirm the approval, the vote and the date of the settlement, but not the terms the edition describes.
- WWNO, Aug 20 2025, https://www.wwno.org/coastal-desk/2025-08-20/entergys-plan-to-power-new-meta-data-center-gets-regulatory-approval: the deal "will fund most of the costs for 15 years, including 100% of power plant expenses," and it does not cover "the transmission line". Entergy's Larry Hand: "It's a 15-year-deal, so we can't predict everything." Hand also says that if Meta does not renew in 2041 the plants will be used to replace aging ones. WWNO has no "revenue requirement", no "Laidley" and no docket number.
- SAN, Aug 21 2025, updated Aug 22, https://san.com/cc/louisiana-moves-ahead-with-contentious-gas-power-plants-for-meta-data-center/: Meta builds "through a subsidiary, Laidley LLC". "Entergy has a 15-year agreement with Laidley to cover the costs of the new gas power plants." "At least half a billion dollars in new transmission costs will be paid for by all utility customers, according to Entergy's application." Two Richland Parish plants are expected to start operating in 2028, and the third "is expected to begin operating in 2029." No revenue requirement.
- Negative still open: "full revenue requirement" is not on any page I could read. The settlement agreement filed Aug 7 2025 in U-37425 is the primary that would settle it. I could not reach it: it is not in the minutes, the Entergy news index (https://www.entergy.com/news/, page 1 of 323, Oct 2026 items only) did not surface it, and I had no search budget. The phrase may well be in the settlement, since "revenue requirement" is standard ratemaking language, so the owner should read that document before cutting the phrase outright.
Fixes to lane 12's True-as:
1. "which all utility customers pay for" states as fact what SAN attributes to Entergy's application ("according to Entergy's application"), and SAN's figure is "at least half a billion dollars" of transmission costs. Keep the attribution.
2. Tense: the plants are expected to start operating in 2028 and 2029 (SAN), so the edition's present-tense "is paying" for fifteen years describes a contract term, not payments now under way. "Has agreed to" is the smallest change the sources bear.
What the page should say: "Under a settlement Louisiana's regulator approved 4-1 in August 2025, Meta's subsidiary has a 15-year deal to cover the cost of three new Entergy gas plants, though not the transmission line." Restore "full revenue requirement" only if the U-37425 settlement agreement is read and says so. Add the LPSC minutes as a citation next to or in place of DCD source 31.
