## 24.1. The US put 174 million dollars into a gallium plant in Western Australia.
Skeptic verdict: amended (the lane's verdict "overstated" and its true-as stand; the sourcing lines need correcting)

Reopened:
- war.gov release https://www.war.gov/News/Releases/Release/Article/4587183/ : HTTP 403 to me as well. Not read.
- defence-industry.eu, 1 Sep 2026, https://defence-industry.eu/u-s-department-of-war-announces-estimated-174-million-equity-investment-to-secure-gallium-supply-through-new-alcoa-facility-in-australia/ . All five of the lane's quotes are on the page verbatim. Two of them are cut off mid-sentence, and the full sentences add useful scope: "The dollar value of the investment remains subject to exchange-rate movements when the transaction is completed." and "The financing structure gives the department an equity position in a facility operated by Alcoa in partnership with Australian and Japanese participants." The page also says "plans an estimated $174 million equity investment in a new gallium production facility at Alcoa’s Wagerup alumina refinery in Australia" and "projected to produce 100 metric tons of gallium metal annually."
- A better primary for the location and partners: Alcoa press release, "Australia, Japan, the United States and Alcoa Break Ground on Gallium Project in Western Australia", 24 Aug 2026, https://news.alcoa.com/press-releases/press-release-details/2026/Australia-Japan-the-United-States-and-Alcoa-Break-Ground-on-Gallium-Project-in-Western-Australia/default.aspx : "the new facility co-located at Alcoa's Wagerup alumina refinery in Western Australia". This release gives no US dollar amount, does not name the Department of War, gives no tonnage and gives no production date (jobs come "once operational").

Findings:
1. "Western Australia" is not on the defence-industry.eu page, which says only "Australia". The lane's true-as uses "Western Australia" with no source for it. Alcoa's own 24 Aug 2026 release supplies it. Add that release as the source for the location and the groundbreaking date.
2. The lane says the quotes are "defence-industry.eu, quoting the release". But the page sets out these sentences as its own reporting, not as quotations from the release. The only quotation marks are around Assistant Secretary of War Michael Cadenazzi: "This landmark agreement with our key allies, Australia and Japan, will secure a vital supply of gallium". The attribution should read "defence-industry.eu, reporting the release". Strictly, then, the $174 million figure is a secondary's account of a primary that neither the lane nor I could open.
3. The lane's note cites a Business Wire release of 14 Jul 2026 announcing a final investment decision. That is not verified: the lane got it from "secondary searches" and gives no URL. The Alcoa groundbreaking release does not mention a final investment decision. Alcoa's press-release RSS feed (https://news.alcoa.com/rss/pressrelease.aspx) has no matching item, though the feed may be truncated. Either drop the FID date or give it a URL.
4. The true-as is accurate and is the smallest change. The verdict "overstated" holds: the $174 million is planned and estimated, its dollar value depends on the exchange rate at completion, and nothing shows it has been paid in.

The page should say: "The US Department of War has announced a planned equity investment, estimated at $174 million, in a gallium plant at Alcoa's Wagerup refinery in Western Australia. Australia and Japan are partners. The plant is projected to make 100 tonnes of gallium a year." Sources: defence-industry.eu, 1 Sep 2026, reporting the war.gov release (which could not be opened, 403), and Alcoa, 24 Aug 2026.

## 24.2. 2026 MP Materials begins heavy rare-earth separation at Mountain Pass.
Skeptic verdict: amended (the verdict "overstated" holds; the lane's "could not settle" rests on a wrong pointer, and a later primary was missed)

Reopened:
- MP 10-K for FY2025, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1801368/000180136826000008/mp-20251231.htm : the lane's quote is there verbatim, in Item 1 (Business), midstream: "The Company is currently advancing the construction of the HREE Facility, with commissioning expected in 2026." The next sentence begins "Initially, the HREE Facility is expected to primarily produce terbium and dysprosium products principally for use in the Magnetics segment". Item 7 (Factors Affecting Our Performance, "Maximizing Upstream and Midstream Production Efficiency") is closer to the edition's wording: "In 2026, we expect to begin refining HREE with initial production of terbium and dysprosium."
- The lane said the 10-Q's Note 15 "would settle it". I opened it. The 10-Q for the period ended 30 Jun 2026 has Note 15, "Government Grants", as XBRL report R22: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1801368/000180136826000048/R22.htm . It does not settle anything. It covers grant accounting only: "to design and build a facility to process HREE at Mountain Pass (the “HREE Facility”)" and "During the six months ended June 30, 2026 and 2025, the Company recorded HREE Facility grant amounts of $5.0 million" and "$12.2 million". It says nothing about status or commissioning. The lane's pointer is wrong.
- A later primary the lane missed: MP's Q1 2026 results release (Exhibit 99.1 to the 8-K of 7 May 2026), https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1801368/000180136826000027/mpmcq12026er.htm . CEO James Litinsky: "We advanced key growth initiatives, expanding operations at Independence and breaking ground on 10X," "with scaled heavy rare earth separation commissioning activities set to begin imminently at Mountain Pass."
- MP's Q2 2026 results release (Exhibit 99.1 to the 8-K of 6 Aug 2026), https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1801368/000180136826000047/mpmcq22026er.htm . It reports a long-term offtake agreement with a new US aerospace and defence customer for "separated gadolinium", "expanding HREE business at attractive economics". In its forward-looking statements it refers to "the Company's ability to expand its heavy rare earth separation capabilities". It does not say that heavy rare-earth separation commissioning has started or that output has begun. It does not mention terbium or dysprosium.
- MP's 8-K list on EDGAR (https://www.sec.gov/cgi-bin/browse-edgar?action=getcompany&CIK=1801368&type=8-K) shows no results 8-K after 6 Aug 2026. Q3 2026 results were not out as of this check.

Findings:
1. The verdict "overstated" holds. No MP filing or release I read says that heavy rare-earth separation has begun.
2. The lane's note misses that MP updated its timing after the 10-K. On 7 May 2026 MP said that "scaled" heavy rare-earth separation commissioning was "set to begin imminently". That is still a forecast, not a start. The 10-K's Item 7 sentence ("In 2026, we expect to begin refining HREE...") is the closest primary to the claim as worded, and it is an expectation.
3. Struck from the lane: "the Note 15 text would settle it". Note 15 is grant accounting. The 10-Q's MD&A, which might hold a status line, was beyond what WebFetch returned of the main document, which stopped at Note 14. I could not run a search for a later MP statement (search budget exhausted). That is a gap, not proof of absence.
4. The lane's point about the edition's source 88 (Creamer Media, about Energy Fuels' White Mesa, not MP) was not re-tested here: the URL was not refetched. Treat it as the lane's reading.

The page should say: "MP Materials expected to begin refining heavy rare earths at Mountain Pass in 2026, starting with terbium and dysprosium (FY2025 10-K). In May 2026 it said 'scaled heavy rare earth separation commissioning activities' were 'set to begin imminently'. No MP filing or release through its Q2 2026 results says separation has begun."

## 24.3. 2028 MP's Texas magnet plant commissions, 7,000 tonnes a year.
Skeptic verdict: amended (the verdict "holds" stands; the lane's location note is wrong, because a primary names the site, and one cost figure is unconfirmed)

Reopened:
- MP 10-K for FY2025 (URL as above), Item 7, Factors Affecting Our Performance, "Development of Our Downstream Manufacturing Capabilities": "The 10X Facility is expected to begin commissioning in 2028, and once completed and scaled," "it will produce an estimated 7,000 MTs of magnets per year." Both of the lane's quotes are verbatim. On Independence: "When combined with the Independence Facility's 3,000 MTs per year of magnets," and "to a projected 3,000 MTs of magnets annually." The lane's caution not to merge the two figures is correct.
- The primary that overturns the lane's location note: MP 10-Q for the period ended 30 Jun 2026, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001801368/000180136826000048/mp-20260630.htm . Note 1: "construct a second domestic magnet manufacturing facility in Northlake, Texas (the "10X Facility")," and construction "has commenced". Note 6: "purchased a 120‑acre site in Northlake, Texas, for approximately $80 million" "where construction of the 10X Facility is currently underway."
- MP Q2 2026 results release (URL as above), forward-looking statements: "risks related to our ability to satisfy the conditions necessary to receive the Texas incentives related to the 10X Facility".

Findings:
1. The lane says "the Texas siting comes from secondary reporting". It does not have to. MP's own 10-Q for Q2 2026, which the lane cites under 24.2, names Northlake, Texas, and says construction is underway. Cite the 10-Q for the location and drop the REBusiness secondary, which the lane did not open.
2. The lane says "the 10-K says more than $1.25 billion" for the project cost. I did not find a 10X cost figure in the 10-K. Three WebFetch windows covered the whole converted text (about 295,000 characters), and none reported a $1.25 billion figure or any 10X capital cost. The 10-Q excerpt had no sentence containing "billion" either. The figure may sit in a table the converter flattened, but as it stands it is unconfirmed. Remove it, or quote it with its location in the filing. Either way, cost is not part of the claim.
3. Commissioning in 2028 and 7,000 tonnes a year are MP's expectation and estimate. The lane's true-as keeps them as forecasts, which is right.

The page should say: "MP Materials expects its 10X magnet plant in Northlake, Texas, now under construction, to begin commissioning in 2028 and, once completed and scaled, to produce an estimated 7,000 tonnes of magnets a year." Sources: FY2025 10-K for the date and tonnage; Q2 2026 10-Q for the location.

## 24.4. 2027 Energy Fuels Phase 1 commercial: up to 1,000 tonnes of NdPr a year.
Skeptic verdict: amended (the verdict "wrong" holds; the true-as overreaches on continuity and leans on an unopened release when a primary is available)

Reopened:
- Energy Fuels release, 10 Jun 2024 (URL as in lane). Verbatim: "it has achieved commercial production of separated neodymium-praseodymium"; "full design capacity of its new Phase 1 REE separation circuit (850 to 1,000 metric tons ("tonnes") of NdPr per year)"; "The Company expects to have commercial quantities of separated NdPr available for shipment by the end of June 2024." The lane's quotes check out.
- Energy Fuels 10-Q, period ended 30 Jun 2026, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001385849/000138584926000029/efr-20260630.htm , Note 1: "Additionally, the Mill has produced separated neodymium/praseodymium (“NdPr”) at commercial scale" "as well as on-spec dysprosium (“Dy”) oxide and terbium (“Tb”) oxide at pilot scale." The lane's quote is verbatim.
- A primary the lane did not open, which replaces the unopened 11 Jun 2026 release: Energy Fuels, "Commercial-Scale 'Heavy' Rare Earth Plant Now Under Construction in Utah", 29 Jul 2026, https://investors.energyfuels.com/2026-07-29-Commercial-Scale-Heavy-Rare-Earth-Plant-Now-Under-Construction-in-Utah . Verbatim: "The Mill has the current commercial capacity to produce up to 1,000 tonnes per annum (tpa) of separated NdPr oxide." "construction has begun on an expansion of its White Mesa Mill in Utah"; "expected to be completed by the end of 2027, with respect to the addition of the Tb and Dy circuits" and "by the end of 2028 with respect to the addition of the Sm, Eu and Gd circuits"; Tb and Dy capacity "expected to be commissioned in Q4 2027"; the company "plans to further expand the Mill in 2029 to increase overall capacity to 6,294 tpa NdPr, 80 tpa Tb, and 288 tpa Dy oxides." This release does not use the labels "Phase 1" or "Phase 2".
- Energy Fuels Q2-2026 results, 5 Aug 2026, https://investors.energyfuels.com/2026-08-05-Energy-Fuels-Announces-Q2-2026-Results : repeats the end-2027 (Tb, Dy) and end-2028 (Sm, Eu, Gd) completion dates. It gives no 2026 NdPr production tonnage and does not say whether NdPr separation is running continuously.

Findings:
1. The verdict "wrong" holds. NdPr commercial production was first achieved in June 2024, not in 2027.
2. "Has produced NdPr at commercial scale since mid-2024" claims more than the sources show. The 10-Q says the Mill "has produced" NdPr at commercial scale. The July 2026 release gives a capacity ("current commercial capacity to produce up to 1,000 tpa"). Neither says production has run continuously since mid-2024, and the Q2-2026 results give no NdPr output. Say "first achieved commercial NdPr production in June 2024" instead.
3. The lane's heavy-rare-earth timing ("late 2027 to early 2028", from the unopened 11 Jun 2026 release reached via stockwatch) should give way to the company's own 29 Jul 2026 release. That release says Tb and Dy circuits complete by the end of 2027 (commissioned Q4 2027), Sm, Eu and Gd circuits by the end of 2028, and a 2029 expansion to 6,294 tpa NdPr. The lane's "nearly 6,300" is consistent with that.
4. The lane's "use the range" (850 to 1,000) is not required. Energy Fuels' own July 2026 release says "up to 1,000 tonnes per annum", so the edition's "up to 1,000 tonnes" is sourced to a primary. Either form is supportable. The 850 to 1,000 range comes from the 2024 press release, not from "the filings" as the lane says.
5. The 10-Q adds scope worth keeping: Dy and Tb oxide so far only "at pilot scale".

The page should say: "Energy Fuels first achieved commercial production of separated NdPr at its White Mesa Mill in Utah in June 2024 and says the Mill has current commercial capacity of up to 1,000 tonnes a year. Its heavy rare-earth expansion, under construction since July 2026, is expected to add terbium and dysprosium circuits by the end of 2027 and samarium, europium and gadolinium circuits by the end of 2028."

## 24.5. Rio Tinto's Quebec gallium pilot, 4 tonnes.
Skeptic verdict: upheld (verdict "overstated" and the true-as stand; two small corrections to the notes)

Reopened:
- Rio Tinto release, 2 Mar 2026, https://www.riotinto.com/en/news/releases/2026/rio-tinto-advances-gallium-metal-rd-project-in-partnership-with-the-government-of-canada . The paragraphs run in this order: pilot plant, demonstration plant, funding, commercial-scale plant. Verbatim: "Rio Tinto will start construction of a pilot plant at its Complexe Jonquière in Saguenay, Canada"; "The new plant is expected to be operational in 2027." (this follows the pilot sentence, so it refers to the pilot); "Plans are underway to build a demonstration plant with a capacity of up to 4 tonnes of gallium per year on the same site." Findings:

2. Funding is in the primary. The lane gave it as "per secondary coverage, not checked against the release", but the release says: "The Government of Canada has conditionally approved a non-repayable contribution of up to C$18.95 million", "in addition to the C$7 million committed by the Government of Québec in December 2024." The release ties the money to "the project", not to the pilot or the demonstration plant specifically.
3. Small quote fix: the lane's 40-tonne quote ends with a full stop, but the sentence continues: "The transition to a commercial-scale plant could see Rio Tinto's production reach 40 tonnes annually, representing approximately 5% of global production." The meaning is unchanged. The 40 tonnes stays outside the claim.
