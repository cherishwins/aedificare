What the IEA settles. Its export-control table (p. 39, "Market share (2025)", orange bars keyed "share of refining") gives China 99% of gallium, 94% of germanium, 94% of refined graphite, 91% of rare earths, 76% of tungsten and 71% of lithium refining: every China figure the page prints. Its own data for the four magnet rare earths (annex p. 363; chart p. 31) put China's 2025 refining at 75 of 89 kt REE (84%) and the United States at 5 (about 6%), and its refined battery-grade graphite at China 2,139 and the US 13 of 2,266 kt. No IEA figure supports a US rare-earth share of "~2%", and the US graphite share is under 1%, not zero. The IEA also contradicts two of the skeptic's proposals: it names radar for gallium nitride ("gallium nitride in high-power radar modules", p. 206), and it puts rare earths in gas turbines ("yttria-stabilised zirconia or other rare earth zirconates", pp. 205 to 206) and in servers ("certain rare earth elements such as neodymium, dysprosium and yttrium", p. 203).

## For the owner

- **"China refines 91 percent of the world's rare earths" is the IEA's table, and the IEA's own data say less for magnet rare earths.** P. 39 prints 91% for 2025 without saying which rare earths; for the four magnet rare earths the same report gives 75 of 89 kt (84%) and "85% of magnet rare earth separation" capacity, and its p. 31 chart puts China's 2023 share near 91%. The prose keeps 91 (printed, attributed, consistent with the IEA's "over 90%"); the 08 row moves to the magnet figures because they are the only IEA scope with a US cell. Keeping both on the page with the note is the settled choice; keeping 91 in the row and dropping the US bar instead is yours.
- **"94% of magnets" (chain, magnet node) has no source found.** It is not in the IEA report's text and not on the charts read. If you have where it came from, it goes back with that source; otherwise it stays cut.
