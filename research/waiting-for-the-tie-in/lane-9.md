## 9.1. [02 · The machines] and since 26 August 2026 an executive order bars Chinese bulk-power equipment from new transactions on the US grid. (attributed to Executive Order 14421) (cites 18)
Verdict: overstated
Primary: The White House, "Declaring a National Emergency to Secure the United States Bulk-Power System" (Executive Order 14421, signed 26 Aug 2026). https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/08/declaring-a-national-emergency-to-secure-the-united-states-bulk-power-system/ . Cross-check: DOE page https://www.energy.gov/node/4860229 ; Orrick (cites 18) https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2026/09/Executive-Order-Restricts-US-Imports-of-Certain-Bulk-Power-System-Equipment
Read: 2026-11-01
Quote: "any acquisition, importation, transfer, or installation of any foreign-produced bulk-power system electric equipment" (Sec. 2(a), as returned by a fetch of the whitehouse.gov page; not checked against the Federal Register text). "where the transaction was initiated after the date of this order," (Sec. 2(a), same fetch). Orrick: "imposes no immediate obligations on private parties." DOE: the order "authorizes the U.S. Secretary of Energy to prohibit certain acquisitions, imports, transfers, or installations of foreign-produced bulk-power system electric equipment."
True as: Since 26 August 2026 an executive order lets the Energy Secretary prohibit acquisitions, imports, transfers and installations of foreign-produced bulk-power equipment tied to covered foreign entities, for transactions started after that date, once the Secretary makes the required findings.
Notes: (1) The order is conditional. Restrictions apply only after the Secretary of Energy makes findings, so it does not bar equipment outright on signing. (2) The order number is 14421 per the White House page and DOE; some early commentary says 14420. (3) On the country: the fetched text of Sec. 2 and the Covered Foreign Entity definition (Sec. 5(e), per the fetch) names no country, China included. Orrick says countries "currently covered by the definition include, most notably, China and Russia." That is a secondary reading and the basis for "Chinese" in the claim. (4) "New transactions" is a paraphrase of "initiated after the date of this order," and it also reaches existing equipment through Sec. 2(b) conditions. (5) I did not open the Federal Register text of the order. Search for the Federal Register citation gave 91 FR 55995 (31 Aug 2026) in commentary only, unverified. (6) I did not check whether DOE has made any determination under the order.

## 9.2. [03 · The chain (Electrical steel)] 50% tariff on non-US metal inside imported transformers. (attributed to chain source: Federal Register) (cites none in list)
Verdict: overstated
Primary: Bureau of Industry and Security (Commerce), "Adoption and Procedures of the Section 232 Steel and Aluminum Tariff Inclusions Process", 90 FR 40326, published 19 Aug 2025 (Federal Register doc 2025-15819; mirror read at frtracker). https://frtracker.app/fr/2025-15819 . Federal Register page https://www.federalregister.gov/d/2025-15819 returned a redirect to an unblock page, not followed.
Read: 2026-11-01
Quote: From the 19 Aug 2025 notice (via frtracker mirror): "will be assessed on these derivative products for the value of the steel or aluminum content in each product." The notice lists HTSUS 8504 codes in its annexes (e.g. "8504.23.00; 8504.33.00; 8504.90.9634"). Secondary, TED magazine, 22 Aug 2025: "transformers containing non-U.S. steel and aluminum components will incur a 50 percent tariff" and "calculated on the value of those components." "The tariffs took effect on August 18, 2025." Proclamation 11021 (Apr 2026) and the 1 Jun 2026 proclamation, effective for covered goods entered on or after 8 June 2026 (EY, 2 Jun 2026), changed the method for derivatives.
True as: From 18 August 2025, a 50% tariff applied to the value of non-US steel and aluminum content in listed transformers. Under the 2026 proclamations the rates and basis for derivatives changed, and I did not establish the current treatment of transformers.
Notes: (1) The 50% rate and the metal-content basis are from the 2025 regime. The Federal Register text I could read does not state 50% itself; that figure comes from TED and law-firm summaries. (2) The chain source cites no URL, so the attribution "Federal Register" rests on the 2025 notice I found. (3) Present tense is not supported: the basis was replaced in 2026. Tiered rates are 50% for primary metal, 25% for most derivatives and 15% for some machinery (EY, Proclamation 11021), and an alternative non-US-content method applies from 8 June 2026. I did not find which tier, if any, covers transformers. (4) Litigation over the full-value method was pending as of the search results (secondary). (5) Did not open Proclamation 11021 or the 1 Jun 2026 proclamation text; EY page did not mention transformers.

## 9.4. [10 · The other queue] its ban on gallium and germanium sales to the US until 27 November. (attributed to MOFCOM (secondary compilation)) (cites 86)
Verdict: overstated
Primary: MOFCOM Announcement No. 72 of 2025 (9 Nov 2025), suspending the second paragraph of MOFCOM Announcement No. 46 of 2024. Not opened; gazette search showed no text. Secondary: Global Trade Alert intervention 2025/72 (search snippet); Xinhua via GlobalSecurity (search snippet, page returned 402).
Read: 2026-11-01
Quote: Not fetched verbatim. Xinhua account (search snippet, paraphrased): the paused element is the second clause of Announcement No. 46 of 2024, which had barred dual-use exports of gallium, germanium, antimony and superhard materials to the US in principle; the suspension runs to 27 November 2026. Global Trade Alert logs it as "set to expire on 27 November 2026."
True as: China has suspended, until 27 November 2026, the clause of its 2024 dual-use export ban that covered gallium, germanium, antimony and superhard materials to the US. The clause on US military end users stayed in force, per Global Times/ANI as reported in search results.
Notes: (1) "Its ban ... until 27 November" reads as if the ban holds until that date. It is a suspension of one clause. (2) Sources conflict on whether the military clause was addressed; Fastmarkets says the announcement did not address it. (3) The claim's "sales" is loose: the measure is an export prohibition to the US, and licensing still applies (secondary). (4) I did not open MOFCOM's own text; the claim rests on search snippets and secondary reports.

## 9.5. [10 · The other queue] Its April 2025 licensing of seven heavy rare earths was never lifted. (attributed to MOFCOM (a negative)) (cites 86)
Verdict: overstated
Primary: MOFCOM and General Administration of Customs, Announcement No. 18 of 2025 (4 Apr 2025), export control on medium and heavy rare earth items. https://english.mofcom.gov.cn/Policies/AnnouncementsOrders/art/2025/art_0dd87cbee7b045bf93fabe6ab2faceee.html
Read: 2026-11-01
Quote: "a decision is made to implement export control on the following items:" and "This announcement shall come into effect on the date of issuance." Items listed include samarium, gadolinium, terbium, dysprosium, lutetium, scandium and yttrium. Secondary (SCMP, search snippet): MOFCOM confirmed on 18 Dec 2025 that some exporters' general licence applications had been received and approved.
True as: China's April 2025 licensing of seven medium and heavy rare earths has no end date in its announcement, and the October 2025 suspension did not list it. Since December 2025 some exporters have received general licences under it.
Notes: (1) "Heavy" understates the list: the items are seven medium and heavy rare earths (secondary). (2) "Never lifted" is a negative. The announcement text has no expiry or suspension clause, and I found no revocation, but I did not run a full-text search of later MOFCOM notices, so I cannot assert it. (3) General licences eased the regime: exporters can get year-long permits, and most exports still use single-use licences (US-China Business Council, secondary). (4) Taylor Wessing (Apr 2026) describes the licence regime as current, but its page returned 403 when fetched; the snippet only.
