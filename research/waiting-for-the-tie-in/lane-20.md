## 20.1. Generation capacity added, 2025 | ~540 | 53 | GW | NEA year-end totals, net; EIA. China: 315 GW solar, 119 wind, 78 to 95 coal. (attributed to NEA; EIA) (cites 44, 45)
Verdict: overstated
Primary: Xinhua via english.www.gov.cn, news item on 2025 wind and solar additions, 12 Feb 2026. https://english.www.gov.cn/archive/statistics/202602/12/content_WS698d93cbc6d00ca5f9a091bb.html ; EIA, "New U.S. electric generating capacity expected to reach a record high in 2026", Today in Energy, 20 Feb 2026. https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php?id=67205
Read: 2026-11-01
Quote: gov.cn (Xinhua): "China's newly installed solar and wind power capacity exceeded 430 million kilowatts in 2025, an increase of 22 percent year on year". EIA: "In 2025, 53 GW of new capacity was added to the grid, the largest capacity installation in a single year since 2002."
True as: China's installed generating capacity rose by roughly 540 GW in 2025, from about 3.35 TW to 3.89 TW (my subtraction of two NEA-based year-end totals). China added 315 GW of solar and 119 GW of wind in 2025 (NEA, as reported). The US added 53 GW of new capacity to the grid in 2025 (EIA). Coal additions were 78 GW per CREA/GEM or 95 GW per Carbon Brief; these are tracker figures, not NEA or EIA.
Notes:
- The "~540" is not a figure NEA states. It is the difference between two year-end totals. End-2025 (3.89 TW) comes from secondary reports of NEA data: a Chinese-language report of 38.9 亿千瓦, and an English secondary report of 3.89 TW, up 16.1%. End-2024 (about 3.35 TW, 33.5 亿千瓦) comes from NEA-based reports. I did not open an NEA primary page.
- Solar 315.07 GW and wind 119.33 GW come from NEA's 28 Jan 2026 release as reported by pv-magazine India, pvtime and TaiyangNews. I did not open the NEA release. The cited pv-magazine article (cite 44) was not fetched directly.
- Scope mismatch: China's total covers all generation sources, including distributed solar. The EIA 53 GW is described as added "to the grid". The EIA article does not say whether small-scale solar is included. The two numbers are not like-for-like. EIA's own framing of the inventory is utility-scale.
- Coal "78 to 95" is not from NEA or EIA. CREA/Global Energy Monitor report 78 GW; Carbon Brief reports 95 GW. The two conflict. I saw these only in search summaries; neither tracker page was opened.
- Other US figure: FERC data, as reported by pv magazine, show about 36.6 GW of new utility-scale builds in 2025 (36,551 MW). That differs from EIA's 53 GW. Say which source is used.
