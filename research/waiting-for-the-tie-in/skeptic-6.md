## 6.2. "GE Vernova's backlog and slot reservations reached 116 gigawatts in July, and it is now taking reservations for 2031 delivery."
Researcher's verdict: overstated. **Skeptic: amended.** The verdict stands, but the reasons change. The researcher's "not checked" primary, GE Vernova's own call transcript, is published and readable. It (a) moves the 2031 half of the claim from press reporting onto the primary, and (b) shows that only 53 of the 116 GW is backlog. That is a bigger scope problem for the figure, chain and carousel than anything the researcher flagged.

Evidence:
- GE Vernova, "GE Vernova 2Q 2026 Results & Outlook", call transcript, Wednesday 22 July 2026. https://www.gevernova.com/sites/default/files/gev_webcast_transcript_07222026.pdf (linked from https://www.gevernova.com/investors/events). Strazik, p. 2: "In Gas Power, we shipped three gigawatts while signing 20 gigawatts of orders and slot reservation agreements in the quarter in countries like the US, Brazil and Qatar to grow our total gigawatts under contract from 100 to 116 gigawatts sequentially. This includes orders for 52 heavy-duty units and 61 aeroderivatives in the quarter."
- Same, p. 3: "Backlog grew from 44 to 53 gigawatts, and SRAs increased from 56 to 63 gigawatts." … "We now expect at least 125 gigawatts under contract by the end of the year." … "We had a strong first half and now have agreements signed into '31."
- Same, p. 4: "we expect to end the year with at least 125 gigawatts under contract, making us mostly sold out through '30, and to have sold more than half of the 30 gigawatts of '31 production slots by the end of this year."
- Same, p. 12 (Q&A, to Nigel Coe, Wolfe Research): "Nigel, I think, as we said, for '31, yes, we'll be more than halfway contracted by the end of the year. There are active discussions for '32 and beyond, but I would say we need more time before we can articulate the timing of contracting '32."
- GE Vernova release, 22 Jul 2026 (cite 09), confirmed: "Gas Power equipment backlog and slot reservation agreements grew from 100 to 116 GW". Quarter ended 30 June 2026. No mention of 2031. https://www.gevernova.com/news/press-releases/ge-vernova-reports-second-quarter-2026-financial-results-raises-2026-financial
- Utility Dive (cite 10), 23 Jul 2026: "The manufacturer's gas turbine order backlog ended the the second quarter of 2026 at 116 GW" (sic), and the key-figure box: "Gas turbine order backlog, up from 100 GW in Q1 2026". It also says "combined backlog of gas turbine orders and slot reservations to reach 125 GW by the end of the year." On 2031, two fetches of the page gave "GE Vernova is taking reservations for 2031 deliveries now, said CEO Scott Strazik." and a fragment, "be more than halfway contracted [for 2031] by the end of the year,". https://www.utilitydive.com/news/ge-vernova-gas-turbine-backlog-climbs-to-116-gw/826039/

Findings:

(b) Scope, new. Of the 116 GW, 53 GW is backlog and 63 GW is slot reservation agreements, so most of it is SRAs. The prose sentence ("backlog and slot reservations") is accurate. Three other places are not:
  - the figure caption "Large gas-turbine backlog · GW · mid 2026" with GE Vernova at 116;
  - the chain card "Backlogs: GE Vernova 116 GW, Siemens 69, Mitsubishi 35";
  - carousel slide 05, "Backlogs: GE Vernova 116 GW."
All three call the whole 116 "backlog", and the caption calls it "large". The bars also mix definitions. Siemens' 69 is backlog without its 26 GW of SRAs, so like for like it is GE Vernova 53 against Siemens 69 (backlog), or 116 against 95 (backlog plus SRAs). As drawn, the chart shows GE Vernova at nearly twice Siemens. On firm backlog Siemens is larger.
The 116 also includes aeroderivatives: 61 aeroderivative units were ordered in the quarter alone. So it is not a large-frame figure. It sits next to Mitsubishi's 35, which is large-frame only (6.4). Utility Dive's "gas turbine order backlog" is wrong on GE Vernova's own numbers, and the researcher took its headline at face value.
(c) 2031, overturned. The researcher said the 2031 part "rests on Utility Dive and on third-party call transcripts" and that "the official release is silent on 2031". The release is silent. GE Vernova's own transcript is not: "now have agreements signed into '31". "Taking reservations for 2031 delivery" holds on the primary.
(d) The researcher's Utility Dive quote is not verbatim. "GE Vernova is taking reservations for 2031 deliveries and expects to 'be more than halfway contracted [for 2031] by the end of the year'" merges two passages, and my fetches returned the first as "...for 2031 deliveries now, said CEO Scott Strazik." Replace it with the transcript.
(e) Outside the listed claim, flagged for the owner. Slide 05's headline "SOLD OUT TO 2031." and the section 02 heading "Sold out to 2031." go further than any source in this lane:
  - GE Vernova says "mostly sold out through '30" and "more than half" of 2031 by year-end, which is not 2031 sold out.
  - MHI's CFO (Q&A, 6 Aug 2026): "the orders booked in Q1 are generally scheduled for delivery between 2028 and 2030", and "projects currently under discussion with customers will generally be delivered around 2030 to 2031".
  - Wood Mackenzie has "order books sold through 2027" (cite 13).
The source-backed form is "Sold out to 2030" or "Booking into 2031".

The page should say: "GE Vernova's backlog and slot reservations reached 116 gigawatts by the end of June, and it has signed agreements for 2031 delivery." The smallest fix is "in July" → "by the end of June". The figure, chain card and slide 05 should either:
  - compare like with like: backlog only, GE Vernova 53 and Siemens 69; or backlog plus reservations, GE Vernova 116 and Siemens 95; or
  - label GE Vernova's bar "116 (backlog and slot reservations; 53 backlog)".
If the caption keeps "large", it needs a note that GE Vernova's figure includes aeroderivatives. Cite the transcript for 2031.

## 6.5. "GE Vernova targets 30 GW a year by 2030." (slider note, 05 · The model)
Researcher's verdict: overstated. **Skeptic: overturned.** The researcher's reason fails: GE Vernova presents 30 GW as a plan it is investing in and selling against, so "targets" is fair. The real problem is one of scope. The slider is "Deliveries a year from 2030", next to "Turbine deliveries to the US a year, to 2029", and GE Vernova's 30 GW is one maker's worldwide output. It is not US deliveries.

Evidence:
- GE Vernova transcript, 22 Jul 2026 (link in 6.2), Strazik, p. 4: "given that we've now reached our 20-gigawatt annualized run rate and are on track for 24 gigawatts in '28, we now see further opportunity to serve this growing demand with 30 gigawatts of annual output in '30 in a capital-efficient manner, utilizing lean and incremental machinery in our existing factory footprint, and have already secured significant supply chain capacity, all funded by customer down payments."
- Same, p. 10, analyst Nicole DeBlase (Deutsche Bank): "I think the 30 gigawatts by 2030 is new, although not surprising."
- Same, p. 11, Strazik: "most of that 30 gigawatts for '30 will already be sold this year, and over half of the '31 gigawatts will be on contract by the end of this year." p. 12: "We will invest in incremental machines beyond 400 to ultimately get to the 30 gigawatts".
- Same, p. 2 and p. 11: the orders and reservations are "in countries like the US, Brazil and Qatar". Taiwan ("north of 10 gigawatts of Hs on contract"), Saudi Arabia, Mexico and Southeast Asia are named as markets.
- GE Vernova release (cite 09), confirmed: "we remain on track to deliver 20 GW of annual gas turbine output in the third quarter of 2026" … "with 24 GW in 2028, and we are implementing actions to produce 30 GW in 2030."
- The slider definition, from the page source (source/waiting-for-the-tie-in.html, firm generation group): "['turb','Turbine deliveries to the US a year, to 2029',5,40,1,22,'GW','Assumption. Fixed by existing backlogs.']" and "['turb2','Deliveries a year from 2030',5,60,1,30,'GW','GE Vernova targets 30 GW a year by 2030.']".

Findings:
(a) "Targets" holds. The company has a dated output level (30 GW in 2030), says it is buying machines to reach it, says it has secured supply chain for it, and is selling 2030 and 2031 slots against it. An analyst on the call treated it as a new plan. That is a target in ordinary usage. The researcher's "not a formal target" is a distinction the company's own words do not support.
(b) The researcher's point that "expand ... capacity" is "a reporter's paraphrase" is also weak. Capacity is how the analyst framed it on the call, and how Strazik answered: machines and factory footprint.
(c) Scope, new and flagged for the owner. The note supports the slider's default of 30 GW a year of deliveries from 2030. The slider sits under a US-deliveries slider. GE Vernova's 30 GW is (i) one maker of three, (ii) worldwide output, with customers in Brazil, Qatar, Taiwan, Saudi Arabia and others, and (iii) output, not deliveries. It does not support 30 GW as a US-wide delivery figure. It could be too high (GE ships abroad) or too low (Siemens and MHI also deliver to the US). The default is an assumption, and the note should say so.
(d) "Up from 20 GW today" in the researcher's true-as should be "a 20 GW annualised run rate from the third quarter of 2026". Strazik puts recent output at "about three gigawatts of output a quarter", moving "to five gigawatts of output a quarter, starting in the third quarter" (p. 10).

The page should say (slider note): "Assumption. For scale: GE Vernova alone plans 30 GW a year of output worldwide in 2030." The smallest fix is to add "Assumption." and "worldwide" to the existing note: "Assumption. GE Vernova targets 30 GW a year worldwide by 2030."
