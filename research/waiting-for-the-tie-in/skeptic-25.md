## 25.1. Engineering bachelor's degrees a year | 1.40M | 145k | China MoE, 2021; NCES, 2020–21.
Verdict: amended. The researcher's scope point is right but too weak. The NCES primary, which the researcher did not open, shows that the 145k also counts mechanic and repair technologies and construction trades, and NCES gives an engineering-only figure of 126,037.
Evidence:
- SWE quote confirmed verbatim: "more than 145,000 bachelor's degrees in engineering and engineering technology awarded in the 2020-2021 academic year." SWE credits "Source: NCES (2022), Digest of Education Statistics, Table 325.45". https://swe.org/research/2025/us-degree-attainment/
- Better primary. NCES Digest 2022, Table 325.45, title: "Degrees in engineering and engineering technologies conferred by postsecondary institutions, by level of degree and sex of student: Selected academic years, 1949-50 through 2020-21". The 2020-21 bachelor's row gives a total of 145,041 (males 111,731, females 33,310). The table note says it includes "degrees in engineering, engineering-related technologies, mechanic and repair technologies," and, from 1969-70, construction trades. https://nces.ed.gov/programs/digest/d22/tables/dt22_325.45.asp
- NCES Digest 2022, Table 322.10 ("Bachelor's degrees conferred by postsecondary institutions, by field of study"), 2020-21 values: Engineering 126,037; Engineering technologies 19,004. Footnote 3 on the second row: "Includes Engineering technologies and engineering-related fields; Construction trades; and Mechanic and repair technologies/technicians." 126,037 + 19,004 = 145,041, so the two tables agree. https://nces.ed.gov/programs/digest/d22/tables/dt22_322.10.asp
- China 1.40M: still not settled. The cited MoE page and the MoE 2021 statistical bulletin (https://www.moe.gov.cn/jyb_sjzl/sjzl_fztjgb/202209/t20220914_660850.html) both return the redirect loop described above. I could not search. I neither confirm nor refute 1.40M, and I cannot say whether it counts graduates or degrees, or whether it is 本科 工学 only.
Scope: NCES counts degrees conferred in the 2020-21 academic year. The MoE series is a calendar-year count, usually of graduates (毕业生). The two years overlap but do not match, and a degree and a graduate are different units.
What the lane file should say instead:
- "True as: NCES counts 126,037 US bachelor's degrees in engineering in 2020–21 (Digest Table 322.10). The 145,041 total (Table 325.45, the figure SWE cites) adds engineering technologies, mechanic and repair technologies, and construction trades. The China 1.40M is unverified."
- The edition's row should either read "126k" for the US under the label "Engineering bachelor's degrees", or keep 145k and relabel the row "Engineering and engineering-technology bachelor's degrees". In both cases the China cell needs the same scope or a flag, and it stays unverified until the MoE table is opened. Cite NCES directly as well as SWE.
- Notes: replace "the SWE sentence counts engineering and engineering technology combined" with the NCES table titles and the 126,037 / 19,004 split.
Rerun for owner: the 2021 MoE table 普通本科分学科学生数 (the 工学 graduates column), opened in a browser; and any secondary source that reprints it (for example, CSET or MacroPolo work on Chinese engineering graduates).

## 25.2. STEM doctorates a year | ~77k | ~40k | CSET projection for 2025. 54% of US engineering doctorates go to temporary-visa holders.
Verdict: amended. The researcher is right that 54% is not in CSET, and I confirm that across the full 29-page PDF. But the negative stopped short. The primary for the visa share is NSF's Survey of Earned Doctorates, which gives 55.7% for engineering in 2023, not 54%. The researcher's unverified field-count sentence is now confirmed verbatim, and the full PDF adds scope points the row should carry.
Evidence:
- CSET executive summary (PDF p. 1), confirmed verbatim: "Based on current enrollment patterns, we project that by 2025 Chinese universities will produce more than 77,000 STEM PhD graduates per year compared to approximately 40,000 in the United States." The researcher split this into two quotes, but it is one sentence. https://cset.georgetown.edu/wp-content/uploads/China-is-Fast-Outpacing-U.S.-STEM-PhD-Growth.pdf
- Same page, the field-count sentence the researcher could not confirm: "For the United States, this includes data on seven academic fields: life sciences, geosciences, mathematics and statistics, computer science, physical sciences, engineering, and medical sciences. For China, we include four academic fields tracked by its Ministry of Education: science, engineering, agriculture, and medicine."
- Table 3 (PDF p. 12): 2025 (projected) gives US 39,959 and China 77,179. The last observed year is 2019 (US 33,759, China 49,498). Table 1 notes: "2019 is the most recent year with available data." So the "2025" figures are projected from 2019 data.
- Table 1 (PDF p. 5), STEM excluding health sciences, 2025 projected: US 35,622 and China 59,053. PDF p. 18 says China's "Medicine" field "also includes some clinical fields as well as Chinese medicine", and that "our counts will also include some individuals with non-STEM or non-research degrees."
- Method (Appendix B, PDF p. 13): China is projected from PhD entrants, "for every 100 students who enter a Chinese STEM PhD program, an average of 93 students obtains a PhD six years later". The US is projected from Council of Graduate Schools entrant growth of "2.85 percent per year".
- International share in CSET (PDF p. 8): "International students made up about 42 percent of STEM PhD graduates in the United States between 2010 and 2019, with especially high shares in computer science and engineering." CSET gives no engineering-only percentage, and no "54" appears anywhere in the document (I read all 29 pages, including the appendices and endnotes).
- Better primary for the visa share. NCSES, Doctorate Recipients from U.S. Universities: 2023 (NSF 25-300, 2 Dec 2024), Figure 8, "Doctorate recipients on temporary visas, by trend broad field: 2003 and 2023": Engineering is 57.2% in 2003 and 55.7% in 2023. Mathematics and statistics is 53.2% in 2023. Note: "Percentages are based on the number of doctorate recipients who reported citizenship." https://ncses.nsf.gov/pubs/nsf25300/figure/8
- NSB, The State of U.S. Science and Engineering 2024 (13 Mar 2024), on 2021 degrees: temporary visa holders "earned more than half of doctoral degrees in computer and information sciences (59%), engineering (60%)". The fetch tool reported that the same sentence gives 54% for mathematics and statistics; I did not get that clause verbatim. https://ncses.nsf.gov/pubs/nsb20243/talent-u-s-and-global-stem-education-and-labor-force
Reading: no primary I opened gives 54% for engineering. The two NSF figures for engineering are 55.7% (SED, 2023) and 60% (NSB, 2021). The 54% sits closest to the mathematics and statistics values (53.2% in SED 2023; 54% in NSB 2021, per the fetch summary), so the row may have taken the wrong field. That is an inference, not shown.
What the lane file should say instead:
- "True as: CSET (Aug 2021) projected, from 2019 data, that China would produce more than 77,000 STEM PhD graduates a year by 2025 against about 40,000 in the United States. The US count covers seven fields and the China count four, including medicine, which CSET says counts some non-research degrees. Excluding health sciences, the projection is 59,053 against 35,622. On visas: NSF's Survey of Earned Doctorates puts temporary-visa holders at 55.7% of 2023 US engineering doctorate recipients who reported citizenship."
- The edition's row should read: "CSET projection for 2025, made in 2021. About 56% of US engineering doctorates in 2023 went to temporary-visa holders (NSF SED)." It should cite https://ncses.nsf.gov/pubs/nsf25300/figure/8 alongside CSET. Drop "54%" unless its own primary is found.
- Notes: delete "I did not get that field-count sentence verbatim ... check it against the PDF before publication" and replace it with the verbatim sentence above.
Rerun for owner: whether NCSES has released SED 2024 data (expected late 2025) with a newer engineering share; and whether CSET or MoE has published actual 2025 Chinese PhD graduate counts, which would replace the projection.

## 25.3. Trade and technical graduates a year | ~10M | ~97k | China: all vocational graduates. US: registered apprenticeship completions, all trades. Not like for like.
Verdict: amended. The researcher's quotes, figures and "True as" hold. Three corrections: the edition's endnote gives the Xinhua article a title it does not carry; the Xinhua sentence's data years can be pinned more closely; and the "no later year" negative is true only of the page opened, because DOL publishes later national data in a dashboard.
Evidence:
- Xinhua, confirmed verbatim: "These institutions annually produce approximately 10 million high-quality technical personnel." The preceding figures: "According to data released by the Ministry of Education in 2023", "there are 9,752 secondary vocational schools in the country", "with over 17.8 million students", and "In 2022, there were 1,521 higher vocational schools with an enrollment of 5.46 million students." The article also says: "At present, China has the world's largest vocational education system." No other annual output figure appears. https://english.news.cn/20240326/762ffa75271b458bbf4bdfbdaba0aa60/c.html
- Xinhua headline, per the page: "Xinhua Headlines: China trains new-generation workers on vocational education boom", dated 2024-03-26 and datelined Shenyang. The edition's endnote (astro line 153) calls it "China's vocational education, Mar 2024", which is not its title.
- DOL FY2021 page, confirmed: header "Fiscal Year | Active Apprentices | New Apprentices | Total Completers | Active Programs | New Programs"; row "2021 | 593,690 | 241,849 | 96,915 | 27,385 | 2,879"; and "96,000 apprentices graduated from the apprenticeship system in FY 2021." https://www.dol.gov/agencies/eta/apprenticeship/about/statistics/2021

## 25.4. H-1B registrations, FY2027 change | n/a | −38.5% | Fragomen. After the $100,000 fee, since vacated in one circuit.
Verdict: amended. The researcher is right that "vacated in one circuit" is wrong. But the file has four faults:
- Its Clearinghouse "Quote" contains the researcher's own words inside the quotation marks.
- Its "True as" states the FY2027 registration count as a USCIS fact, though no USCIS page shows it.
- It does not say that the two FY numbers it divides may be different measures.
- One of its causal notes (duplicate registrations) is undercut by USCIS's own table.
Evidence:
- USCIS, "H-1B Electronic Registration Process" (last reviewed/updated 09/21/2026), registration table, FY2026 row: Total Registrations 358,737; Eligible Registrations 343,981; registrations for beneficiaries with no other eligible registration 336,153; with multiple eligible registrations 7,828; Selected 120,141. Footnote: "The count of eligible registrations excludes duplicate registrations". FY2026 text: "The number of eligible unique beneficiaries for FY 2026 (approximately 339,000)". The table has no FY2027 row. https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/temporary-workers/h-1b-specialty-occupations/h-1b-electronic-registration-process
- Negative hunt for the FY2027 count. USCIS's two FY2027 H-1B alerts give no counts. The 31 Mar 2026 alert, "FY 2027 H-1B Initial Registration Selection Process Completed", gives no registration, eligible-registration, beneficiary or selection figures (https://www.uscis.gov/newsroom/alerts/fy-2027-h-1b-initial-registration-selection-process-completed). The 17 Jul 2026 alert, "USCIS Reaches Fiscal Year 2027 H-1B Cap", gives only the "65,000" regular cap and the "20,000" advanced-degree exemption (https://www.uscis.gov/newsroom/alerts/uscis-reaches-fiscal-year-2027-h-1b-cap). The H-1B cap season page has no FY2027 figures. So 211,600 rests on secondary reports and on Fragomen, which neither the researcher nor I could open (HTTP 403).
- Arithmetic: 211,600 / 343,981 − 1 = −38.5%, confirmed. But 343,981 is USCIS's "Eligible Registrations" measure. If 211,600 is a different measure (the researcher's file calls it "properly submitted"), the base changes. Against FY2026 total registrations (358,737) the change is −41.0%; against unique beneficiaries (about 339,000) it is −37.6%. Which FY2026 base Fragomen used is unverified.
- Ogletree (Amanda R. Goodman, 2 Oct 2026), confirmed verbatim: in Global Nurse Force v. Trump, a federal district court in California on 30 Sept 2026 "blocked federal agencies from enforcing or implementing policies tied to the $100,000 H-1B payment requirement", "unless and until they comply with the rulemaking requirements of the Administrative Procedure Act." The order covers implementation by USCIS, CBP and the State Department; the article does not say whether it is nationwide. A federal district court in Massachusetts "has also blocked the requirement," and "that decision is currently on appeal before the U.S. Court of Appeals for the First Circuit." Proclamation 11069, issued 18 Sept 2026, extends the restriction "through September 21, 2027." https://ogletree.com/insights-resources/blog-posts/federal-court-issues-new-order-blocking-agency-implementation-of-100000-h-1b-fee
- Clearinghouse: HTTP 403 to me, so I cannot confirm the quote. Its last clause, "but the page does not describe it as vacating the proclamation itself", refers to "the page" and so cannot be page text. It is the researcher's comment placed inside quotation marks. https://clearinghouse.net/case/47032/
- Weighted selection: the USCIS cap season page says "USCIS has adopted a weighted selection process for FY 2027" favouring higher wage levels. The registration page links a final rule whose Federal Register URL is dated 2025/12/29. https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/temporary-workers/h-1b-specialty-occupations/h-1b-cap-season
Reading:
- "Vacated in one circuit" is wrong on three counts. The orders are district-court orders, not circuit rulings. There are two of them, in the First and Ninth Circuits' districts. And Ogletree describes the California order as blocking agency implementation pending APA rulemaking, while the proclamation itself was extended to September 2027.
- The researcher's causation note blames the drop partly on "USCIS's effort to cut duplicate registrations". USCIS's table shows that duplicates had already been removed from the FY2026 base: only 7,828 FY2026 eligible registrations were for beneficiaries with multiple registrations, and the eligible count "excludes duplicate registrations". Duplicates cannot explain much of a fall from that base. Weighted selection, new for FY2027, remains a candidate. Neither source I opened attributes the fall to the fee.
- The D.C. ruling (upheld, December 2025) and the Massachusetts date (8 June 2026) in the researcher's "True as" were not confirmed by any page I opened. Ogletree mentions neither.
What the lane file should say instead:
- Quote: strike "but the page does not describe it as vacating the proclamation itself" from inside the Clearinghouse quotation marks and move it to Notes as the researcher's reading. Mark the Clearinghouse quote unconfirmed (403 to the skeptic).
- "True as: Secondary reports, attributed to USCIS and repeated by Fragomen (unopened, 403), put FY2027 H-1B registrations at 211,600. That is about 38.5% below USCIS's FY2026 eligible-registration count of 343,981 (USCIS registration table). USCIS itself has published no FY2027 count on its registration page (updated 21 Sept 2026). The $100,000 payment has been blocked by two federal district courts: in Massachusetts (on appeal to the First Circuit) and in California (Global Nurse Force v. Trump, 30 Sept 2026, blocking agency implementation pending APA rulemaking). Proclamation 11069 (18 Sept 2026) extends the restriction through 21 Sept 2027."
- The edition's row note should read: "Fragomen, from USCIS data. Registration count, not petitions. The $100,000 fee has been blocked by district courts in Massachusetts and California and extended by proclamation to Sept 2027." Drop "since vacated in one circuit". Do not state or imply the fee as the cause.
- Notes (4): replace "to cut duplicate registrations" with "USCIS's FY2026 eligible count already excludes duplicates (only 7,828 multiple-registration entries), so duplicates explain little; weighted selection, new for FY2027, is a candidate."
Rerun for owner: the Fragomen page in a browser, to confirm it gives −38.5% and names the FY2026 base; any USCIS release of FY2027 registration data; the D.D.C. ruling of December 2025 (Chamber of Commerce v. DHS) and the Massachusetts order's date and appeal status on the dockets.
