## 7.1. "Wood Mackenzie counted about 110 gigawatts on order at the end of 2025 against 60 to 70 gigawatts of global factory capacity a year, with prices heading for roughly 195 percent above 2019."
Researcher's verdict: overstated. **Skeptic: amended.** The quotes are verbatim and the true-as holds. Two points are added. (a) The figure label "Global factory output a year 60–70" says "output", but the source says "capacity". Capacity is a ceiling, not output, and the researcher did not flag this. (b) On "a year", the release's own chart is relevant and the researcher did not look at it.

Evidence:
- Wood Mackenzie, "Gas turbine prices soar 195% as market faces supply-demand crisis", 01 April 2026: "global orders sat at 110 gigawatts (GW) at the end of 2025, but global manufacturing capacity is only capable of 60-70 GW." / "with the market anticipated to reach US$600/kW by end-2027—a 195% increase since 2019." / "This supply constraint, compounded by six-year lead times and order books sold through 2027". The figures are attributed to the report "The US gas turbine market: navigating manufacturing scarcity and demand growth", which the release names in plain text with no link. "per year", "a year" and "annual" (applied to the 60–70 GW) do not appear. https://www.woodmac.com/press-releases/gas-turbine-prices-soar-195-as-market-faces-supply-demand-crisis/
- The same release's only body image, "US-based manufacturing capacity from 2025 to 2026", shows capacity in GW for each calendar year (GE Vernova about 11.5 to 17, Mitsubishi Power about 6 to 9, Siemens Energy about 12 to 18). https://www.woodmac.com/siteassets/press-release-images/nabufacturing-capacity.png
- Same release: "GE Vernova is spending over US$160 million to increase production from approximately 50 large-frame turbines annually" "to 70–80 units by late 2026."

Findings:
(a) Quotes, date and global scope: upheld. The release itself writes "global" for both figures. The researcher's caveat stands: the report's title is about the US, and I did not open the report either (it is paywalled and not linked).
(b) "A year": the researcher is right that the 60–70 GW sentence gives no time unit. The release's chart does treat manufacturing capacity as GW per calendar year, so an annual reading is how Wood Mackenzie uses the term. But the chart is US-based capacity and not the global 60–70. Weak support, not a source for the words. The researcher's advice to drop "a year" stands as the safe course.
(c) "Output" (new): the figure caption "Global factory output a year 60–70" turns a capacity ceiling into output. The source says capacity.
(d) 195%: a forecast for end-2027, to US$600/kW. The stat line ("by end 2027 against 2019") already carries the date. The prose "heading for" is fair, but it should carry the date too.

The page should say: "Wood Mackenzie counted about 110 gigawatts on order at the end of 2025 against 60 to 70 gigawatts of global manufacturing capacity, and expects prices to reach US$600 per kW by the end of 2027, 195 percent above 2019." Figure label: "Global manufacturing capacity 60–70 GW" (not "factory output a year"). Smallest fixes: delete "a year"; "output" → "capacity" in the figure; add "by the end of 2027" after "heading for roughly 195 percent above 2019".

## 7.3. "Single-crystal blades and rotor forgings from a few global suppliers" (chain, Turbine castings; also Status: "The ceiling under 60 to 70 GW a year of OEM output.")
Researcher's verdict: overstated. **Skeptic: amended.** The researcher's advice to drop "and rotor forgings" is overturned. The lane's own cite 59 (Tianxia Gongchang) names rotor forgings as a bottleneck with very few presses worldwide. The researcher read all of cite 59 for 7.4 but did not search it for this claim. The claim is right on substance, but it needs Tianxia as a second source. The researcher's true-as also narrows Wood Mackenzie's words, and the Status line is unsupported.

Evidence:
- Wood Mackenzie (cite 13), 1 April 2026. The whole sentence, which stands as its own paragraph: "Hot-section component manufacturing—particularly single-crystal blade production—remains the industry's critical bottleneck," "as these precision processes can only be performed at scale by a handful of global suppliers." It names no supplier and no component other than blades. "Rotor", "forging" and "casting" do not appear. The passage does not refer to the 60–70 GW capacity figure. https://www.woodmac.com/press-releases/gas-turbine-prices-soar-195-as-market-faces-supply-demand-crisis/
- Tianxia Gongchang Industry Research Institute (cite 59), 18 July 2026. https://www.faxiangongchang.com/en/reports/china-heavy-gas-turbine-2026
  - Ch. 13: "the global casting capacity able to supply advanced single-crystal and directionally solidified blades for gas turbines" "at scale has long been monopolized by two American firms — Precision Castparts Corp. (PCC) and Howmet" "which together hold 70–80% of the global high-end blade market while supplying all three of GE, Siemens, and Mitsubishi"
  - Ch. 13: "the tightest bottleneck is precisely not in the final-assembly shops but in blade casting and large rotor forgings"
  - Ch. 24: "the world's ten-thousand-tonne presses for rotor forgings can be counted on one's fingers"
  - Ch. 14: "the capability to supply heavy-duty gas turbine rotor forgings in volume still awaits validation" (about Chinese suppliers)

Findings:
(a) Rotor forgings: the researcher is right that Wood Mackenzie does not support them. Tianxia (cite 59) does: "large rotor forgings" are a bottleneck, and the presses that make them "can be counted on one's fingers". Tianxia is a secondary compilation and names no forging supplier, but it supports "from a few global suppliers" for both parts. Keep the phrase, and add cite 59 and its attribution.
(b) Blades: Tianxia gives a sharper figure than "a few": two firms, PCC and Howmet, hold 70–80% of the global high-end blade market.
(c) True-as narrows the source. Wood Mackenzie's "these precision processes" refers to "hot-section component manufacturing", with single-crystal blades as the main case ("particularly"). The researcher's true-as limits it to single-crystal blade production alone.
(d) Status line, not tested by the researcher. "The ceiling under 60 to 70 GW a year of OEM output" ties the blade bottleneck to the 60–70 GW figure. Neither source makes that link. Wood Mackenzie calls blade production "the industry's critical bottleneck", and Tianxia says "the tightest bottleneck". The 60–70 GW is "capacity", not "output", and has no "a year" (see 7.1).

The page should say: "Single-crystal blades and large rotor forgings, each from a handful of global suppliers; two US firms, PCC and Howmet, cast 70 to 80 percent of high-end blades." Attribution: "Wood Mackenzie; Tianxia Gongchang (secondary compilation)". Sources: 13, 59. Status: "The industry's critical bottleneck." Smallest fixes: add cite 59 and its attribution; Status "The ceiling under 60 to 70 GW a year of OEM output" → "The industry's critical bottleneck."

  - Iraq: Ch. 8, "Dongfang Electric exported 2 more units to Iraq", but Ch. 9, "Order two: Iraq, 2 units." The report contradicts itself, and no delivery date is given.

  - Ch. 24: "roughly US$600 million total into U.S. plants over two years" is GE Vernova's US plant investment (same paragraph: "an additional US$160 million invested in the Greenville, South Carolina plant"). It is not Jereh's.

(d) Period. The latest source is dated 18 July 2026. The edition is dated 15 October 2026, and UBS forecast a first US export "in 2026". The negative is three months staler than the page. I could not run a search for a US delivery between July and October 2026 (search budget exhausted), and the researcher did not search news either. This negative is not cleared for the edition's date. The page should say: "No source we cite records a Chinese large-frame turbine delivered to the United States; as of April 2026, Chinese deliveries were concentrated in emerging economies, and the first North American order, for Canada, came in March 2026." Attribute to Jiemian (14) and Tianxia Gongchang (59). Slide 15 and the 09 row: "None delivered" → "None delivered to the US in our sources (to July 2026)". ## 7.5. "Dongfang's first self-developed F-class export was a 50 megawatt unit to Kazakhstan in late 2025."
