## 5.1. PJM short of its reliability requirement "three times running, the first shortfalls in its history" (PJM)
Researcher's verdict: wrong. **Skeptic: amended. The verdict stands. The true-as gets the 2027/28 figure wrong, and the "unresolved" conflict is settled.**

Evidence (primary, PJM news release, 14 Jul 2026, read from the saved PDF): https://www.pjm.com/-/media/DotCom/about-pjm/newsroom/2026-releases/20260714-pjm-capacity-auction-procures-138318-mw-of-generation-resources.pdf
- Subhead: "Total Capacity Cleared Is 6,831 MW Short of Reliability Requirement"
- "The capacity of the resources procured in the auction, plus FRR resources, is short of PJM's reliability requirement by 6,831 MW"
- "including a shortfall of approximately 6,500 MW in the previous capacity auction (for the 2027/2028 Delivery Year). These most recent auctions were the first in PJM history in which the entire RTO fell short of the reliability requirement."
- "This was the third consecutive auction with the price collar." (That third is the price collar, not the shortfall. It supports the researcher's guess about where "three" came from.)

Evidence (primary, PJM 2027/2028 Base Residual Auction Report, 17 Dec 2025): https://www.pjm.com/-/media/DotCom/markets-ops/rpm/rpm-auction-info/2027-2028/2027-2028-bra-report.pdf
- "total procured capacity in the RTO of 145,777.1 MW UCAP or 6,516.6 MW UCAP below the RTO Reliability Requirement"
- "Since the RPM cleared 6,623.2 MW UCAP less than the IRM"

Evidence (primary, PJM 2026/2027 Base Residual Auction Report, 22 Jul 2025): https://www.pjm.com/-/media/DotCom/markets-ops/rpm/rpm-auction-info/2026-2027/2026-2027-bra-report.pdf
- "total procured capacity in the RTO of 146,243.8 MW UCAP or 139 MW UCAP above the RTO Reliability Requirement."
- "The reserve margin procured for the entire RTO, which includes FRR and RPM load, is 18.9%, or 0.2 percentage points lower (or 309 MW ICAP lower) than the target reserve margin of 19.1%"

Findings:
(a) "Three times running" is wrong, as the researcher said. PJM itself counts two: 2027/28 and 2028/29.
(b) "The first shortfalls in its history" holds, on PJM's own wording, with PJM's qualifier "the entire RTO". The researcher had this only from a search summary. It is now on PJM's page.
(c) The true-as gives 2027/28 as "about 6,600 MW short". The researcher's own quote (POWER) says "roughly 6,500", and PJM's release says "approximately 6,500 MW". PJM's report has two figures: 6,516.6 MW for all procured capacity including FRR against the Reliability Requirement (the same basis as the 6,831 MW), and 6,623.2 MW for RPM alone against the IRM. Like for like with 6,831, the figure is about 6,500.
(d) The 2026/27 "conflict" the researcher left unresolved is two measures in the same report. In UCAP against the Reliability Requirement, the measure PJM uses for the shortfalls, 2026/27 was 139 MW over. On PJM's estimated ICAP reserve margin it was 309 MW ICAP under the 19.1% target, a gap PJM itself labels an estimate. "Met the requirement" is right on the reliability-requirement basis. The edition should not count 2026/27 as a shortfall.
(e) Secondary renderings: the stat "6,831 MW PJM shortfall, 2028/29 auction. Third in a row." must lose "Third in a row" (say "Second in a row."). The chain line "PJM 6.8 GW short for 2028/29." holds.

The page should say instead: "PJM, the grid that serves Virginia, has now cleared its capacity auction short of its reliability requirement twice running, the first times in its history that the whole region has fallen short." The true-as should read: "PJM's 2028/29 capacity auction, held July 2026, cleared 6,831 MW short of its reliability requirement. That is the second straight shortfall, after about 6,500 MW in the 2027/28 auction held December 2025, and PJM calls them the first in its history in which the entire RTO fell short. The 2026/27 auction cleared 139 MW above the requirement."

## 5.5. "NERC: 10-year summer peak up 224 GW, mostly data centres." (NERC)
Researcher's verdict: overstated. **Skeptic: amended. The "projection, not observed" fix stands. The "mostly data centres" fix is wrong: NERC's own sentence supports it.**

Evidence (primary, NERC 2025 LTRA, URL as 5.4):
- p. 25: "Over the 10-year period, aggregated assessment area summer peak demand is forecast to rise by over 224 GW. This is 69% higher than last year's 10-year growth projection of 132 GW. ... The 10-year aggregated winter peak demand is forecast to rise by over 245 GW"
- p. 9 and p. 27: "New data centers for artificial intelligence and the digital economy account for most of the projected increase in North American electricity demand over the next 10 years."
- p. 5: "This 2025 LTRA assessment period includes projections for 2026–2035" and "Projections in this assessment are not predictions of what will happen".
- p. 27: "data center and large load growth forecasts in the LTRA are likely to be more conservative than predictions from the technology industry".

Findings:
(a) "Up 224 GW" reads as observed. NERC says "forecast to rise by over 224 GW". The researcher's fix (1) stands.
(b) The researcher's fix (3) rests on POWER's paraphrase ("Artificial intelligence data centers and the digital economy account for most"), which reads as two drivers. NERC's own sentence is "New data centers for artificial intelligence and the digital economy". The subject is data centers, and "for AI and the digital economy" modifies it. "Mostly data centres" is a fair short form of NERC. The other drivers (industrial, transport, crypto, heat pumps, demographics) appear in NERC's Figure 10 as regional drivers. They do not change "most".
(c) Two scope points the researcher missed. NERC's figure is "over 224 GW", a floor. And it is aggregated across North American assessment areas (US, Canada and part of Baja California), not the US alone. NERC's "most" sentence is about "North American electricity demand" generally, not summer peak specifically. Neither needs a change at slider length, but the true-as should carry them.
(d) Winter 245 GW is now confirmed on NERC's page (the researcher had it from a search summary).
(e) For the owner, not a finding: the edition's model chart (carousel slide 10) labels its 2032 endpoint "224 delivered" (GW of chips). If the model takes NERC's 224 GW of North American summer-peak growth over 2026–2035 as chips delivered by 2032, that is a change of scope, period and geography. I could not see the model's code or notes, so I cannot say whether it does. CLAIMS.md line 482 lists the defaults without 224.

The page should say: "NERC: 10-year summer peak forecast to rise 224 GW, mostly data centres." The true-as should read: "NERC's 2025 Long-Term Reliability Assessment (January 2026) forecasts aggregated North American summer peak demand to rise by over 224 GW over 2026–2035, and says new data centers for artificial intelligence and the digital economy account for most of the projected increase in demand."
