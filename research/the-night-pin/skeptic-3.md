## 1. "The first securities-fraud case against an alternative-data vendor"; SEC settled with App Annie and Schmitt on 14 Sep 2021. [04]
Researcher's verdict: wrong (the quoted words are not in the release).
Skeptic: AMENDED. The verdict should be "overstated", not "wrong", and for a different reason.
- The researcher's reason doesn't hold. The edition (line 77) has no quotation marks: "The first securities-fraud case against an alternative-data vendor came out of this layer." It is a paraphrase. The quote marks came from the claim summary, not the edition. So "the quoted wording does not appear" is not a fault in the edition.
- The real fault is scope. The edition's "first" is unqualified. The source claims only the SEC's first. Release, https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2021-176 (targeted fetch, clause verbatim): "...which is the SEC's first enforcement action charging an alternative data provider with securities fraud." Two searches found no earlier case against such a vendor by anyone else, but nothing covers that wider claim either.
- Date and "settled" upheld, now from the order. Order p. 1: "Release No. 92975 / September 14, 2021". Section II: "Respondents have submitted Offers of Settlement (the "Offers") which the Commission has determined to accept." Release: "have agreed to settle securities fraud charges".
- I checked the release clauses the researcher took from a summarising fetch with targeted verbatim fetches. They match.
What the page should say: "The SEC's first securities-fraud case against an alternative-data vendor came out of this layer. On 14 September 2021 the SEC settled with App Annie and its co-founder, Bertrand Schmitt." (Only "The" changes to "The SEC's".)

## 2. Aggregated/anonymised promise; late 2014 to mid-2018 use of non-aggregated, non-anonymised data to adjust estimates sold to trading firms; consents representation. [04]
Researcher's verdict: holds, no change needed.
Skeptic: AMENDED (the facts hold; the attribution is missing).
- Every element is confirmed in the order (34-92975.pdf):
  - Promise to companies, ¶2: "App Annie tells Connect users that it will generate estimates of app performance using their Connect Data, but that it will only use Connect Data in aggregated and anonymized form to generate those estimates."
  - Period, ¶1: "Between late 2014 and mid-2018 (the "relevant period")". The practice ended according to ¶48: "In June 2018, after the Company had learned of the SEC's investigation, App Annie discontinued all post-model estimate alteration practices based on non-aggregated and non-anonymized Connect Data."
  - Use, ¶3: "during the relevant period App Annie used confidential Connect Data, in its non-aggregated and non-anonymized form, to alter Intelligence estimates generated by the statistical model in order to make the estimates closer to Connect users' actual app metrics, and thereby more valuable to App Annie to sell." ("Adjust" is a fair paraphrase. ¶40 itself says "automatically adjusted the model-generated estimates".)
  - Consents, ¶3: "App Annie and Schmitt represented to Intelligence subscribers that Intelligence estimates were generated through a statistical model that used aggregated and anonymized Connect Data, and that Connect users had consented to App Annie using their confidential data in this way."
  - Trading firms, ¶3: "more than 100 trading firms paid for Intelligence subscriptions". Subscribers also included companies that offered apps (¶13), so "sold to trading firms" is true but not exclusive.
- What the researcher missed: these are findings in a settled order, and the respondents did not concede them. Order Section II: "without admitting or denying the findings herein". The one exception is Section V, where Schmitt admits the findings for bankruptcy-discharge purposes only. The release frames them the same way: "According to the SEC's order". The edition states them as plain fact ("App Annie had told... it used..."). The source says what the SEC found.
What the page should say: "According to the SEC's order, App Annie had told the companies sharing their app data that it would be aggregated and anonymised. From late 2014 to mid-2018 it used the non-aggregated, non-anonymised data to adjust the estimates it sold to trading firms, while telling those firms its process respected the consents it had obtained. App Annie and Schmitt neither admitted nor denied the findings."

## 3. App Annie paid $10 million; Schmitt paid $300,000 and accepted a three-year bar. [04]
Researcher's verdict: overstated ("paid" is not in the source).
Skeptic: UPHELD, with the order as the better primary.
- Order IV.C(1): "App Annie shall, within ten (10) days of the entry of this Order, pay a civil money penalty in the amount of $10,000,000 to the Securities and Exchange Commission". IV.C(2) sets the same terms for Schmitt: "$300,000". The order requires payment. It does not record that payment was made.
- The negative ("paid" is not in the source) was hunted. The release, the order, the SEC AP summary listing and the law-firm alerts found by search all say "agreed to pay" or "ordered to pay". None records payment.
- Bar, order IV.B: "Schmitt be, and hereby is, prohibited from serving or acting as an officer or director of any issuer that has a class of securities registered pursuant to Section 12 of the Exchange Act ... or that is required to file reports pursuant to Section 15(d) ... for a period of three (3) years from the entry of this Order." "Public company" is the release's own shorthand ("prohibited from serving as an officer or director of a public company for three years"). "Accepted" fits a consent order.
What the page should say (the researcher's wording, kept): "App Annie agreed to pay a $10 million penalty. Schmitt agreed to pay a $300,000 penalty and accepted a three-year bar from serving as an officer or director of a public company."

## 4. Berkeley Haas satellite study: 4.8m images, RS Metrics "later combined with Orbital Insight", 67,000 stores, 44 retailers, 2011-2017; "funds earned 4 to 5 percent" in three days; short side; individual investors. [05]
Researcher's verdict: overstated.
Skeptic: AMENDED. The verdict stays "overstated", but two of the researcher's negatives are overturned and the rewording changes.
- Overturned negative 1, Orbital Insight. The researcher wrote that "later combined with Orbital Insight" is "not in either source as read" and should be dropped. It is in both sources.
  - Haas, https://newsroom.haas.berkeley.edu/how-hedge-funds-use-satellite-images-to-beat-wall-street-and-main-street/ (Laura Counts, 28 May 2019): "The researchers later added in more images from competing firm Orbital Insight, which covers the same companies".
  - Paper, p. 555, fn. 1: "In an additional analysis, we merge store-level parking lot data from RS Metrics and Orbital Insight and find that investors with access to both vendors could formulate even more profitable strategies targeting earnings announcements." Also: "Orbital Insight, however, started selling parking lot traffic signals to investors beginning in mid-2015".
  - So the data were combined. The firms were not: a search found no RS Metrics and Orbital Insight merger. The edition's phrase can be misread as a corporate merger, so make it say "data".
- Overturned negative 2, the 4 to 5 percent. The researcher wrote that the paper does not state a figure of this size: "A spread of about 4.6 points is implied by the paper's two figures but not stated there." The paper states it.
  - Paper p. 562, Table 3 ("Formulating a Trading Strategy Using Satellite Imagery Data"), buy-minus-sell, 3-day window centred on the quarterly earnings announcement:
    - Before stock loan fees: 4.60% raw, 4.64% market-adjusted, 4.76% factor-adjusted.
    - After stock loan fees: 4.57% raw, 4.62% market-adjusted, 4.73% factor-adjusted.
  - Text, p. 562: "In terms of abnormal returns, the spread between the buy and sell portfolios is 4.76%, which is statistically significant and economically important."
  - Window, pp. 561-562: "cumulated over the 3-day window centered on the quarterly earnings announcement."
  - So "4 to 5 percent in three days" is supported. "Funds with the data earned" is not. The figure is the return of a hypothetical long/short strategy (Table 3: "a trading strategy that buys (short sells) retailers..."), not realised fund earnings. Haas words it the same way: "The informational advantage yields 4% to 5% in the three days around quarterly earnings announcements".
- Counts upheld as the researcher amended them. Paper p. 553 (repeated p. 556): "4.7 million daily observations across 67,078 unique store locations for 44 major U.S. retailers between 2011:Q1 and 2017:Q4". The 4.8 million figure comes from the 2019 Haas article about the working paper ("4.8 million images of parking lots at 67,000 individual stores"). The paper does not use it. Print the published paper's figure.
- Short side upheld. P. 562: "the absolute magnitude of returns is nearly twice as large for the short-sell portfolio relative to the buy portfolio."
- Individual investors upheld.
  - P. 565: "under the current disclosure regime (FINRA Rule 4560), the general public has access to short-interest data only twice per month and only with a significant delay."
  - P. 566: "As short sellers actively target retailers with bad news for the quarter, individual investors are net buyers of such retailers."
- "Researchers at Berkeley Haas": the JFQA byline lists Katona and Patatoukas at UC Berkeley, Painter at Saint Louis University and Zeng at the National University of Singapore. The Haas piece presents it as Haas research. No change proposed, flagged only.
What the page should say: "Researchers at Berkeley Haas studied 4.7 million daily parking-lot observations from RS Metrics, later combined with Orbital Insight's data, covering 67,078 stores belonging to 44 major US retailers from 2011 to 2017. A strategy that traded on the data, buying retailers with rising counts and shorting those with falling ones, beat the market by about 4.6 percent, net of stock-loan fees, in the three days around earnings announcements. The edge was largest on the short side: funds bet against retailers heading for bad quarters while individual investors, who can see short interest only twice a month and with a lag, were buying the same stocks." (The rest of the paragraph is unchanged. 4.62% is Table 3 Panel B, market-adjusted, after stock loan fees.)
