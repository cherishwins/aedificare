## 3. [07]
Verdict: overstated
Primary: (a) FTC press release "FTC Takes Action Against Mobilewalla for Collecting and Selling Sensitive Location Data", 3 Dec 2024 (proposed order). https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/12/ftc-takes-action-against-mobilewalla-collecting-selling-sensitive-location-data . (c) FTC press release "FTC Finalizes Order Prohibiting Gravy Analytics, Venntel from Selling Sensitive Location Data", 14 Jan 2025. https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/01/ftc-finalizes-order-prohibiting-gravy-analytics-venntel-selling-sensitive-location-data . (d) Mobilewalla Decision and Order, Docket C- (proposed form, Matter 2023196), PDF pp. 4 to 9 read as page images. https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/2023196mobilewallaorder.pdf
Read: 2026-10-30
Quote (a), protesters: "It also used audience segments to create a June 2020 report analyzing people who protested the death of George Floyd and determined the protesters' racial backgrounds and whether they lived in the cities in which they protested."
Quote (a), pregnancy centres: "the company collected location data from women who visited pregnancy centers" (sentence as returned by WebFetch of the proposed-order release; a segment, not the whole sentence).
Quote (a), home: "The Federal Trade Commission will prohibit data broker Mobilewalla, Inc. from selling sensitive location data, including data" / "that reveals the identity of an individual's private home, to settle allegations the data broker sold such information without taking reasonable steps to verify consumers' consent."
Quote (b): "The Federal Trade Commission finalized an order banning data broker Mobilewalla Inc. from selling sensitive location data" ; "The Commission voted 4–1 to approve the final order and send responses to the commenters," ; "with Commissioner Melissa Holyoak voting no."
Quote (c): "will be prohibited from selling, disclosing, or using sensitive location data except in limited circumstances" (sentence continues with national security and law enforcement exceptions); vote "5-0 to approve the final order" after two substantive comments (per the release, as summarised by the fetch tool).
Quote (d), Provision III: "must not sell, license, share, disclose, transfer, or otherwise use in any products or services Sensitive Location Data" ; Definition M: "'Sensitive Location Data' means any consumer Location Data associated with a Sensitive Location." ; Definition G (Deidentified): "Location Data that is linked to a mobile advertising identifier or an individual's home address is not Deidentified."
True as: In December 2024 the FTC proposed orders against Gravy, Venntel and Mobilewalla. The FTC alleged Mobilewalla used audience segments to create a June 2020 report analysing people who protested, and that it collected location data from women who visited pregnancy centers. The Mobilewalla order, finalised 14 January 2025, bars it from selling sensitive location data, which the FTC says includes data that reveals the identity of an individual's private home. The Gravy and Venntel order, also finalised 14 January 2025, bars selling, disclosing or using sensitive location data, with national-security and law-enforcement exceptions.
Notes:
- Protesters: the source describes audience segments used to make a report about protesters (June 2020), not segments of protesters as such. Overstated in wording.
- Health clinics: the source says women who visited pregnancy centers (for pregnancy-targeted segments), and says "visits to health clinics" as a general example of sensitive data. "Women visiting health clinics" merges the two.
- Home: the Dec 2024 release says the ban covers data revealing "an individual's private home". In the Mobilewalla order text I read (proposed form, PDF pp. 4 to 9), "Sensitive Location" lists categories (medical facilities, religious organisations, correctional facilities, union offices, LGBTQ+ services, shelters, protests, military sites) and does not list homes; "home address" appears only in the definition of Deidentified. I did not read the rest of the order (PDF p. 10 onward), and the finalised text was not opened, so "explicitly covers" rests on the press release's wording. Scope is narrower than the edition's phrasing.
- Protests appear in the order as a Sensitive Location category ("locations of public gatherings ... during political or social demonstrations, marches, and protests"). That is relevant to the protest example, but it is not what the FTC alleged.
- Status: Gravy/Venntel and Mobilewalla were proposed 3 Dec 2024 and finalised 14 Jan 2025. Both finals bar the sale; Gravy's has national-security and law-enforcement exceptions.
- "Bar sale" is accurate for all three; "selling" in the edition is a narrower verb than the order's "sell, license, share, disclose, transfer, or otherwise use".

Idaho), Document 137-1, "Proposed Stipulated Order", filed 05/04/26. https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/ftc_v._kochava_-_proposed_stipulated_order.pdf (not opened; search snippet only).
Read: 2026-10-30
Quote: "from selling, sharing or disclosing sensitive location data without consumers' affirmative express consent" ; "The FTC filed the proposed order in the U.S. District Court for the District of Idaho." ; "The Commission vote approving the stipulated final order was 2-0." ; "NOTE: Stipulated final orders have the force of law when approved and signed by the District Court judge."
True as: The FTC's proposed Kochava order, filed in federal court in May 2026, would bar Kochava and its subsidiary from selling, sharing or disclosing sensitive location data without consumers' affirmative express consent.
Notes:
- Status: the May 2026 document is a proposed stipulated order. It has force of law only once the court approves and signs it. The FTC case page lists a later Stipulated Order for Injunction and Other Relief dated June 26, 2026 (search snippet only; not opened). Secondary coverage gives conflicting entry dates (June 25 or 26), which I could not reconcile. The edition's "order ... filed in May" should say "proposed" unless the June entry is checked.
- Consent wording: "affirmative express consent" is the FTC's term. The edition's "express consent" drops "affirmative"; minor, but the FTC's wording is the one to use.
- Scope: the sale ban has the consent condition plus a requirement that the data be used only for a service the consumer directly requested (per a law-firm summary, not checked against the order text).
- Yale Notice & Comment (secondary) argues the order narrows the definition of sensitive locations; not checked here.
