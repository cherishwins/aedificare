## 1. Gravy/Venntel, 17 billion signals from about a billion smartphones [07]

Skeptic verdict: **amended** (the researcher's "overstated" is too strong; the line holds in substance and needs a tense change and a better primary).

Evidence:
- Better primary: FTC complaint, In the Matter of Gravy Analytics, Inc. and Venntel, Inc., File 212-3035, para. 9 (read as page image): "Through these various suppliers, Respondents claim to "collect, process and curate" over 17 *billion* signals from approximately a *billion* mobile devices on a *daily* basis." https://ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/2123035gravyanalyticscomplaint.pdf
- Same complaint, para. 2: "Venntel is a wholly-owned subsidiary of Gravy Analytics." Para. 10: "These location signals, gathered from consumers' mobile phones, identify consumers' precise geolocation".
- FTC release, 3 Dec 2024, confirmed on reopening: "claimed to collect, process, and curate more than 17 billion signals from around a billion mobile devices daily." https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/12/ftc-takes-action-against-gravy-analytics-venntel-unlawfully-selling-location-data-tracking-consumers
- The cited secondary [07], The Record (Suzanne Smalley, 3 Dec 2024), is where the edition's wording comes from: "Gravy Analytics and Venntel say they gather more than 17 billion signals from about a billion smartphones every day". https://therecord.media/ftc-location-data-brokers-gravy-venntel-mobilewalla

Findings against the researcher:
- The researcher left the release quote unconfirmed past a fetch trim. The complaint (para. 9) now confirms the figure in full and is the better primary.
- "Smartphones" is not just the edition's own narrowing. It comes verbatim from [07], and the complaint itself says the signals are "gathered from consumers' mobile phones" (para. 10). "Mobile devices" is the FTC's count unit, so "devices" or "phones" is safer than "smartphones". This is minor.
- The real scope problem is tense. The claim is reported in an FTC complaint of December 2024. The edition prints "say" (present) in October 2026, and nothing shows the companies still make it.

Print instead: "Gravy Analytics and its subsidiary Venntel claimed, according to the FTC in December 2024, to collect more than 17 billion signals a day from about a billion mobile devices. [07]"

## 2. Mobilewalla, more than 500 million ad IDs; an estimated 60 percent from ad auctions [07][02]

Skeptic verdict: **amended** (I uphold the researcher's scope findings on the 60 percent; I amend one verb and the sourcing).

Evidence:
- FTC release, 3 Dec 2024, reopened: "The FTC's complaint alleges that from January 2018 to June 2020," / "Mobilewalla collected more than 500 million unique consumer advertising identifiers" / "paired with consumers' precise location data." https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/12/ftc-takes-action-against-mobilewalla-collecting-selling-sensitive-location-data
- FTC complaint, Docket C-4811 (final version issued with the Decision and Order), para. 11, read as page image: "Mobilewalla has estimated that approximately 60% of its consumer data came from RTB exchanges between January 1, 2018, and June 30, 2020." Para. 12: "approximately 77 million unique advertising identifiers paired with location information in 2018, over 273 million ... in 2019, and approximately 269 million ... in 2020." Para. 14: "more than 2 billion unique advertising identifiers over this period." https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/Mobilewalla-Complaint.pdf
- [02] EFF (Cohen and Hongo, 5 Mar 2026), which the researcher did not open: "For example, the FTC found that location data broker Mobilewalla collected data on over a billion people," "with an estimated 60% sourced from RTB auctions." The "estimated 60%" links to the FTC complaint. https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2026/03/targeted-advertising-gives-your-location-government-just-ask-cbp

Findings:
- I confirm the researcher's quotes verbatim. The 60 percent is Mobilewalla's own estimate as recorded in the complaint. It covers "consumer data", not location data alone, over Jan 2018 to Jun 2020. EFF's "the FTC found" overstates an allegation that rests on a company estimate. The complaint is the source to cite, not [02].
- The "500 million" figure appears in the FTC release, not in the complaint. The complaint gives annual counts and a 2 billion total. Cite the release for it.
- Against the researcher: "matched ... to precise location" is not quite accurate. The FTC says Mobilewalla "collected" identifiers "paired with" location; the pairing came in the bid requests and supplier data (complaint paras. 10, 17). "Matched" implies Mobilewalla did the matching.
- "Ad auctions" is a fair gloss for "RTB exchanges".

Print instead: "**More than 500,000,000 advertising IDs** paired with precise location were collected by Mobilewalla between January 2018 and June 2020, the FTC alleges. Mobilewalla itself estimated that about 60 percent of its consumer data in that period came from ad auctions. [07]" (cite the FTC complaint for the 60 percent in place of [02]).

## 3. Mobilewalla segments of protesters and of women visiting health clinics; order bars sale; covers homes; three orders bar sale [07]

Skeptic verdict: **overturned** (the researcher's "overstated" does not survive the complaint and the final order; the edition holds, with one optional precision).

Evidence:
- Protesters. Mobilewalla complaint, para. 31: "For example, Mobilewalla has created retroactive geo-fences around the sites of political rallies or protests to create audience segments of the consumers that attended those rallies or protests." Para. 32: "Additionally, Mobilewalla used such an audience segment to publish a report in June 2020 that analyzed individuals who protested the death of George Floyd". https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/Mobilewalla-Complaint.pdf
- Clinics. Complaint, para. 30: "Mobilewalla has created geo-fences around pregnancy centers and maternity clinics and searched through its vast amounts of location information to determine which consumer devices visited those healthcare centers in order to build its audience segments of pregnant women and expectant families." (Repeated in para. 55.) [07] has: "Mobilewalla also allegedly tracked women to health clinics to sell audience segments of pregnant women".
- Homes. Final Decision and Order, Docket C-4811, Provision V, "Prohibition on the Sale, Licensing, or Disclosure of Private Residence Data": "must not sell, license, or disclose Location Data that may determine the identity or the location of an individual's private residence (e.g., single family homes, apartments, condominiums, townhomes)." https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/Mobilewalla-D%26O.pdf (PDF p. 7, read as page image).
- Mobilewalla sale ban. Same order, Provision III: "must not sell, license, share, disclose, transfer, or otherwise use in any products or services Sensitive Location Data". Definition L lists ten categories, including "(9) locations of public gatherings of individuals during political or social demonstrations, marches, and protests". Provision III has a proviso for data converted so that it is no longer Sensitive Location Data or Location Data.
- Mobilewalla final. FTC release, 14 Jan 2025: the FTC "finalized an order banning data broker Mobilewalla Inc. from selling sensitive location data"; vote 4-1, with Commissioner Holyoak voting no. https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/01/ftc-finalizes-order-banning-mobilewalla-selling-sensitive-location-data
- Gravy/Venntel final. FTC release, 14 Jan 2025: "Gravy Analytics and Venntel will be prohibited from selling, disclosing, or using sensitive location data" ... "except in limited circumstances involving national security or law enforcement."; vote 5-0. https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/01/ftc-finalizes-order-prohibiting-gravy-analytics-venntel-selling-sensitive-location-data

Findings against the researcher:
- Protesters: the researcher said the source describes segments used to make a report, "not segments of protesters as such". The complaint alleges both: segments of people who attended rallies or protests (para. 31), and a segment used for the June 2020 report (para. 32). The edition's "audience segments of protesters" is accurate as an FTC allegation.
- Homes: the researcher's negative ("does not list homes"; "explicitly covers" rests on the press release) is overturned. The researcher stopped at PDF p. 9 of the proposed-order package and never opened the final order. The final order has a stand-alone Provision V on private residences. "Mobilewalla's explicitly covers data showing where people live" holds as written. Strictly, Provision V is a separate ban, not part of "sensitive location data", but the edition does not claim otherwise.
- Clinics: "women visiting health clinics" is a fair but broad gloss. The complaint names pregnancy centers and maternity clinics, and the segments were of "pregnant women and expectant families". This is optional to change.
- Dates: proposed 3 Dec 2024, finalised 14 Jan 2025 for both orders. The edition's "In December 2024 the FTC alleged ... and its order bars" is correct, and present-tense "bars" is right because the orders are final. The Gravy/Venntel ban has national-security and law-enforcement exceptions; the edition's "bar the sale" stays true.

Print: line 159 as written, or with the optional precision "...of protesters and of women visiting pregnancy centers and maternity clinics, and its order, finalised in January 2025, bars the company from selling sensitive location data. [07]". Line 167, first two sentences, as written; optionally add "with exceptions for national security and law enforcement in Gravy and Venntel's".

## 6. Kochava's order, filed in May 2026, bars sale of sensitive location data without the consumer's express consent [18]

Skeptic verdict: **amended**. I uphold "overstated". I replace the researcher's rewording, because the order was entered by the court on 25 June 2026 and the consent exception needs three conditions, not one.

Evidence:
- Entered order, primary: FTC v. Kochava, Inc., No. 2:22-cv-00377-BLW (D. Idaho), Document 138, "Stipulated Order for Injunction and Other Relief", header "Filed 06/25/26". Signature page: "DATED: June 25, 2026", signed "B. Lynn Winmill, U.S. District Court Judge" (p. 30, read as page image). Provision XVIII: "this Order will terminate 10 years from the date it is entered by the Court." https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/Kochava-Order.pdf
- Provision II, "Prohibitions on the Sale or Disclosure of Sensitive Location Data" (pp. 9-10):
  - Ban: "must not sell, license, transfer, share, or disclose in any products or services Sensitive Location Data associated with the Sensitive Locations that Defendant CDS has identified within 90 days of the entry of this Order as part of the Sensitive Locations Data Program".
  - Exception: "Provided, however, that the prohibitions in this Provision II do not apply if Defendants have a direct relationship with the consumer related to the Sensitive Location Data, the consumer has provided Affirmative Express Consent, and the Sensitive Location Data is used to provide a service directly requested by the consumer."
- Definition M, "Sensitive Locations" (p. 7): "(1) medical facilities; (2) religious organizations; (3) locations of entities held out to the public as predominantly providing education or childcare services to minors; (4) locations held out to the public as providing temporary shelter or social services to homeless, or survivors of domestic violence; or (5) military or federal law enforcement installations, offices, or buildings."
- Definition G (pp. 6-7): data used for "Security Purposes", "National Security purposes conducted by federal agencies or other federal entities", or "response by a law enforcement agency to an imminent risk of death or serious bodily harm to a person, is not Precise Location Data."
- FTC case page, last updated June 26, 2026, lists: "May 4, 2026 ... [Proposed] Stipulated Order for Injunction and Other Relief" and "June 26, 2026 ... Stipulated Order for Injunction and Other Relief". https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/ftc-v-kochava-inc
- FTC release (May 2026), reopened; the researcher's quotes are confirmed. The release also says the ban applies "unless they obtain a consumer's affirmative express consent" and "the data is used to provide a service directly requested by the consumer." https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/05/ftc-ban-kochava-subsidiary-selling-sensitive-location-data-settle-charges-they-sold-location-data
- [18] PPC Land, 5 May 2026: "It was filed in the U.S. District Court for the District of Idaho on May 4, 2026"; the order "is today before the court for entry". It also says "The only exception applies when defendants have a direct relationship with the consumer related to that specific location," ... "the consumer has provided affirmative express consent," ... "and the data is used to provide a service directly requested by the consumer." https://ppc.land/ftc-closes-kochava-location-data-case-with-strict-consent-rules/

Findings against the researcher:
- Status: the researcher left the court's entry unopened and "could not reconcile" June 25 vs 26. The order itself settles it. The judge signed it and it was filed on 25 June 2026; 26 June is the FTC's posting date. The researcher's rewording "proposed ... would bar" is therefore out of date for an edition dated 30 Oct 2026. The order is in force. The edition's "filed in May 2026" describes the proposed order. It should say "entered in June 2026" (lines 167 and 197).
- Consent: "without the consumer's express consent" understates the ban. Consent alone does not lift it. It takes a direct relationship with the consumer, affirmative express consent, and use for a service the consumer directly requested, all three together. The researcher marked the third condition as "not checked against the order text"; it is now confirmed in Provision II, together with the direct-relationship condition the researcher missed.
- Scope, for the owner: Kochava's Sensitive Locations list has five categories. Mobilewalla's has ten; the Kochava list has no protests, union offices, LGBTQ+ venues, correctional facilities or immigrant services. The edition's "sensitive location data" across all four orders is still literally true, but the orders do not cover the same places.

Print instead (line 167): "Kochava's order, entered by a federal court in June 2026, bars the sale of sensitive location data unless the company has a direct relationship with the consumer, the consumer gives affirmative express consent, and the data is used for a service the consumer asked for. [18]" (cite the court order or the FTC release alongside [18]). Line 197: change "the Kochava order filed in May 2026" to "the Kochava order entered in June 2026".
