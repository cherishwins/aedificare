## 1. Voter file fields. "Almost every state's file carries name and home address. Many add party, voting history and year of birth. A few add phone numbers, sex or race." [12]

Researcher: could not open. No rewording.
**Skeptic: amended.** The 403 on NCSL is real. But the claim can be tested against a readable federal compilation built partly from that NCSL page, and the researcher did not look for one. That compilation supports the sentence in substance and corrects two of its words.

What I reopened:
- NCSL, legacy URL cited by the edition: https://www.ncsl.org/research/elections-and-campaigns/access-to-and-use-of-voter-registration-lists.aspx. Result: "HTTP 403 Forbidden".
- NCSL, current URL (the legacy one has moved): https://www.ncsl.org/elections-and-campaigns/access-to-and-use-of-voter-registration-lists. Result: also "HTTP 403 Forbidden". I did not route round it, and I did not use an archive copy. A search snippet gives the page's own summary: "States have varied requirements on who is eligible to request a list of voters, what information the list contains, what information is kept confidential and how the information contained in voter lists may be used." The Voter Reference Foundation cites the page as "last updated July 2025" (https://www.voterreferencefoundation.com/voter-data-101-voter-list-access/). That date is second-hand.
- **Better readable source.** U.S. Election Assistance Commission, "Voter Roll Privacy", "Last Updated: 1/3/2023", https://www.eac.gov/sites/default/files/2024-03/Voter_Roll_Privacy.pdf. Its state table's footnote says: "Sources for this research include NCSL's Access To and Use of Voter Registration Lists". As of 23 Dec 2025 EAC's current page still links it: https://www.eac.gov/election-officials/voter-lists-registration-confidentiality-and-voter-list-maintenance.

What the EAC table says, per state, in its column "What Info is Given to Requester" (50 states plus DC, read page by page):
- **Name and address.** Explicit in most rows, e.g. Alaska "Names, addresses, party affiliation."; Michigan "Name, address, year of birth". Other rows are generic, e.g. Alabama "All voter registration information, except what is confidential."; Connecticut, Kansas, Kentucky and New Jersey "Not specified." No row excludes name and address. "Almost every" holds.
- **Party.** Many rows (AK, AZ, CA, CO, DE, IL, MD, MS, NE, NH, NY, NC, OK, OR, RI, UT, WV, WY). Holds.
- **Voting history.** Many rows (AZ, CO, DE, DC, ME, MD, MN, MS, MO, NE, NH, NC, ND, OH, PA, SD, TN, VA, WI). Holds.
- **Year of birth.** Explicit in about nine rows (e.g. Arizona "birth year", Colorado "year of birth", Nebraska "birth year"). But seven rows give the **full date of birth**: Maine "DOB", Maryland "DOB", Missouri "DOB", Nevada "DOB", Pennsylvania "DOB", South Carolina "DOB", Texas "DOB". Illinois gives "age". North Carolina gives "age but not date of birth". "Year of birth" understates what several files release.
- **Phone numbers.** Ten rows: AZ, CA, DE, IL, MN, NE, NV, OR, SC, UT (Utah "phone number if listed"). Ten states is more than "a few".
- **Sex or gender.** Six rows: Illinois "sex", Maryland "sex", North Carolina "sex", South Carolina "sex", Virginia "gender", Wyoming "gender". "A few" holds.
- **Race.** Two rows: North Carolina "sex, race" and South Carolina "Name, sex, race". "A few" holds.

True as (smallest change): "Almost every state's file carries name and home address. Many add party, voting history and year or full date of birth. Some add phone numbers; a few add sex or race." [12, plus the EAC Voter Roll Privacy compilation]

Notes:
- The EAC table is dated 1/3/2023. NCSL's page (July 2025, second-hand) may differ. Until someone opens NCSL in a browser, cite EAC as well, or instead.
- The EAC table describes what is **given to requesters**, and that can vary by requester (Delaware: "Full DOB is only provided to certain voter list requesters"; Virginia: voting history "available to candidates, parties, etc. for political purposes"). The edition's "carries" should be read as "releases to at least some requesters".
- No characterisation of Anthropic is involved.

## 2. NCSL: 29 states non-commercial only; 9 more election, political or government only; 12 silent; none expressly allows any use. The "38 states" sum (sections 10 and 12). [12]

Researcher: could not open. No rewording.
**Skeptic: amended.** The researcher's "could not open" stands for NCSL itself: both NCSL URLs return 403 (see claim 1). But the negative "cannot be checked without the NCSL text" is wrong. EAC publishes a readable state table that cites NCSL's page as a source and uses the same purpose categories. Its split is **not** 29 / 9 / 12. Its total of restricted states is 38.

Evidence, from the EAC "Voter Roll Privacy" PDF (Last Updated: 1/3/2023), https://www.eac.gov/sites/default/files/2024-03/Voter_Roll_Privacy.pdf:
- Page 1 map legend, verbatim: "Purpose Limit ● Non-commercial ● Not specified. ● Political ● Political and Government". The text beside it: "Most states prohibit the commercial use of voter file information, but some of this information may be publicly available."
- My count of the column "Purpose Limitations for Voter File Requests", one entry per state, with no state in two categories:
  - **"Non-commercial purposes.": 26.** AZ, CA, GA, ID, IL, IN, IA, KS, KY, LA, MD, MS, MO, MT, NE, NH, NJ, OH, OR, SC, TX, VT, WA, WV, WI, WY.
  - **Election, political, government or law-enforcement purposes: 12.** HI "Election-related and government purposes."; ME "Political purposes."; MN "Election-related, political, or law enforcement purposes."; NV "Election-related purposes."; NM "Election-related and government purposes."; NY "Election-related purposes."; ND "Election-related purposes."; PA "Election-related or law enforcement purposes."; RI "Election-related purposes."; SD "Election-related purposes."; TN "Political purposes."; VA "Political purposes."
  - **"Not specified.": 12.** AL, AK, AR, CO, CT, DE, FL, MA, MI, NC, OK, UT. DC is also "Not specified." but is not a state.
  - 26 + 12 + 12 = 50. In this table the categories do not overlap, and no category says any use is allowed.
- Second EAC document, "Availability of State Voter File and Confidential Information", "UPDATED October 29, 2020", https://www.eac.gov/sites/default/files/voters/Available_Voter_File_Information.pdf. Sources: "National Conference of State Legislatures, United States Elections Project, State Election Officials". Verbatim: "In general, even the least restrictive states prohibit the commercial use of voter file information". This is in tension with "12 are silent on use". "Not specified" in a purpose column does not prove that no other statute bars commercial use.

Scope and date problems:
- **Split.** 29 / 9 is not what the readable NCSL-derived table shows (26 / 12). NCSL's July 2025 revision may have moved three states. Nobody has read it, so the edition cannot attribute "29" and "9" to NCSL as a checked figure.
- **Category label.** The edition's second group, "election, political or government purposes", omits law enforcement, which appears in Minnesota's and Pennsylvania's entries. The edition's figure caption ("election or government only") also drops "political", which is the whole of the Maine, Tennessee and Virginia entries.
- **"Silent" is moving.** Alabama is "Not specified." in EAC's table. In 2026 Alabama's legislature passed HB67, whose title reads "to prohibit the use of voter data or information for commercial purposes" (engrossed text, https://alison.legislature.state.al.us/files/pdf/SearchableInstruments/2026RS/HB67-eng.pdf). A tracker reports it passed on 17 Apr 2026, and the enrolled text says "This act shall become effective on June 1, 2026" (https://alison.legislature.state.al.us/files/pdf/SearchableInstruments/2026RS/HB67-enr.pdf, seen as a search snippet). I found no confirmation that the governor signed it. If it is law, the "silent" count is at most 11 and the restricted count is at least 39 as of the edition date.
- **A different 2026 tally.** Ballotpedia, 24/28 July 2026 (https://news.ballotpedia.org/2026/07/28/twenty-one-states-and-d-c-make-voter-files-openly-available/): "twenty-one states and Washington, D.C., make their voter files openly available", where "the only restrictions on how the voter file can be used are on commercial use"; "Fifteen states have usage-restricted availability"; "Twelve states have both requester-restricted and usage-restricted availability"; two are requester-restricted only. This is Ballotpedia's own scheme, not NCSL's. It records no state with no use restriction at all, which supports the edition's "no state expressly allows any use".
- **"38 states" (sections 10 and 12).** In the EAC table, 26 + 12 = 38. So the total survives on the readable evidence even though the split does not. A use limited to election, political, government or law-enforcement purposes excludes a trading desk. Kansas, for example, says "solely for political campaign or election purposes" is not commercial use (K.S.A. 25-2320a, https://ksrevisor.gov/statutes/chapters/ch25/025_023_0020a.html, seen as a search snippet). That supports "restricted against exactly that". The total is fair as "at least 38", given Alabama.

True as (smallest change, if NCSL's live page still cannot be read):
"According to a state-by-state table compiled by the U.S. Election Assistance Commission from NCSL and other sources (last updated January 2023):
- **26 states** restrict use of the voter list to non-commercial purposes.
- **12 more** restrict it to election, political, government or law-enforcement purposes.
- **12** specify no purpose limit. The table records no state that expressly allows any use." [12, EAC]
Sections 10 and 12: keep "38 states". If Alabama's HB67 is confirmed as law, say "at least 38".
The waffle figure becomes 26 / 12 / 12.
If a human opens NCSL's live page and it really says 29 / 9 / 12, the edition's wording can stand, with the category relabelled to include "political" and "law enforcement" as NCSL words them.

## 3. VoterMatch. "On 18 June 2026 ... Nexxen and ... L2 launched VoterMatch. It joins L2's national voter file to Nexxen's ad-auction logs so that campaigns can see which registered voters an ad reached and how often. Neither company disclosed the matching method or match rates." [13]

Researcher: overstated. Reworded to "log-level ad data" and to "Nexxen's launch release does not describe the matching method or give match rates."
**Skeptic: amended.** The date, the parties, the datasets and the purpose all hold. So does the "ad-auction" fix. The researcher's negative is too narrow: the release is a joint announcement by both companies, and I searched L2's own site as well. The researcher also missed the release's own word "modeled".

Primary: https://nexxen.com/nexxen-and-l2-data-launch-votermatch-bringing-greater-precision-transparency-and-performance-to-political-advertising/ (read 2026-10-30, two extraction passes; the second quoted every sentence containing the key words).
- Date and parties (dateline, verbatim): "New York, June 18, 2026 — Nexxen, the advertising technology platform powered by unique data and media, and L2 Data (“L2”),". This is the release's own date. Both companies are announcers, and the release quotes L2's president, Paul Westcott.
- Datasets: "By combining Nexxen’s log-level data with L2’s comprehensive National Voter File, VoterMatch provides campaigns". Vargas Caro (Nexxen): "connect two historically siloed datasets – deterministic voter records and log-level DSP data". The words "auction" and "available" do not appear on the page. Keep the researcher's change to "log-level ad data", or say "log-level data from its demand-side platform".
- Purpose: "political advertisers’ understanding of which voters have been reached, and how frequently."
- **Scope point the researcher quoted but did not use:** the benefit bullet reads "More precise reach visibility, moving from estimates to voter-level modeled exposure". By the release's own wording, the per-voter exposure is modeled, not observed. "Campaigns can see which registered voters an ad reached" states as fact what the release calls modeled.
- Method and rates (negative, tested): neither extraction found a sentence on identifiers, hashed emails, IP or household linkage, a match rate, a percentage or a count of voters. The only accuracy language is in L2's boilerplate ("Known for its accuracy and depth") and in the forward-looking-statements disclaimer ("statements regarding the accuracy or effectiveness of Voter Match").
- The negative also holds against both of the following.
  - ExchangeWire's 18 June 2026 piece (https://www.exchangewire.com/blog/2026/06/18/nexxen-l2-data-introduce-precision-targeting-with-votermatch/) is a PressBox repost and adds no method or rates.
  - A domain-restricted search of l2-data.com for "VoterMatch" returned no VoterMatch page. The closest is a DataMapping page on matching "FirstName", "LastName", "AddressLine", "City", "State" to L2 IDs, which is about a different product.
  - An open search for VoterMatch match-rate figures found only the release and its syndications (Yahoo Finance, Investing.com, StockTitan, Finviz, Chartmill/GlobeNewswire).
- Not opened: investors.nexxen.com (the researcher recorded a 403; I did not retry it). It is a copy of the same release.

True as: "On 18 June 2026, the ad-tech company Nexxen and the data firm L2 launched VoterMatch. It combines L2's national voter file with Nexxen's log-level ad data so that campaigns can see, in the companies' words, 'which voters have been reached, and how frequently', which the release itself calls 'voter-level modeled exposure'. The launch release does not describe the matching method or give match rates." [13]
If the owner wants the shorter "Neither company disclosed the matching method or match rates", it holds as of 30 Oct 2026 for the joint release and L2's site. It is still safer to tie the negative to the release.

## 4. Mobilewalla 2020. "In June 2020 Mobilewalla published the race, gender and age mix of protesters in New York, Los Angeles, Minneapolis and Atlanta, derived from phone data. Its founder said no agency had asked for it." [19]

Researcher: overstated. Reworded to "mobile-device location data" and "Its CEO, Datta, said the report was not prepared for law enforcement or a public agency."
**Skeptic: amended.**
- I uphold the core finding: "no agency had asked" is a sharpening. Wikipedia caused it, and the edition copied it.
- I overturn the researcher's doubt about "founder": Mobilewalla's own page settles it.
- I reject the researcher's change from "phone data", which is a larger change than the evidence needs.

Evidence:
- Original reporting: Caroline Haskins, "Almost 17,000 Protesters Had No Idea A Tech Company Was Tracing Their Location", BuzzFeed News, "Posted June 25, 2020", https://www.buzzfeednews.com/article/carolinehaskins1/protests-tech-company-spying. Verbatim excerpts (the extraction tool cut long sentences, so these are opening portions):
  - "Mobilewalla CEO Anindya Datta told BuzzFeed News that the data analysis that made the George Floyd Protester Demographics"
  - "Datta said Mobilewalla didn’t prepare the report for law enforcement or a public agency, but rather to satisfy its own" [employees' curiosity, per the researcher's fuller extraction]
  - "And they did so completely unaware that a tech company was using location data harvested from their cellphones to predict" [their race, age, and gender, per extraction]
  - "Just over two weeks later, that company, Mobilewalla, released a report titled “George Floyd Protester Demographics:"
  - Datta on why (extraction, quoted): "It's hard to tell you a specific reason as to why we did this."
- **Where "no agency had asked" came from.** The edition's cited source, Wikipedia (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobilewalla), says "Datta proffered that the report was prepared to satisfy the curiosity of its employees, and not on behest of any law enforcement or public agency." It cites BuzzFeed for this. "Not on behest of" (not at the request of) is Wikipedia's gloss. BuzzFeed's words are "didn’t prepare the report for law enforcement or a public agency", which is about purpose or client. It does not say whether anyone asked. The edition's "no agency had asked for it" follows Wikipedia, not the reporting. The researcher is right.
- **Founder.** Mobilewalla's leadership page (https://www.mobilewalla.com/about/leadership) lists "Anindya Datta" as "Founder, CEO, Chairman". "Its founder" holds. BuzzFeed calls him "Mobilewalla CEO". Either title is true.
- **Phone data.** BuzzFeed's description of this report is "location data harvested from their cellphones". The "stew of location data, device IDs, and browser histories" sentence describes how the company builds profiles in general, and all of that is phone-derived. "Derived from phone data" is accurate. The smallest true-to-source variant is "derived from cellphone location data". The researcher's "mobile-device location data" is not wrong, but it is not needed.
- **Date.** Wikipedia's citation for Mobilewalla's report is dated 17 June 2020. IndraStra reposted Mobilewalla's announcement on "Thursday, June 18, 2020" ("Mobilewalla, a consumer data and analytics company, today released a new report.", https://www.indrastra.com/2020/06/New-Report-reveals-Demographics-of-BLM-Protests-006-06-2020-0017.html). The data window was 29 to 31 May 2020. "June 2020" holds as the publication date.
- **Cities and categories.** The IndraStra repost of the release covers "Atlanta, Los Angeles, Minneapolis, and New York", with race, gender and age breakdowns. This matches the edition.
- **Better primary not reachable.** Mobilewalla's report and methodology PDFs, linked from the release (https://f.hubspotusercontent40.net/hubfs/4309344/MW%20Protester%20Demographic%20Analysis.pdf and .../Mobilewalla%20Protester%20Insights%20Methodology.pdf), both return "HTTP 404 Not Found". The fetch tool cannot reach the archive.org copy. As the researcher said, do not print percentages from this lane. The race figures differ between IndraStra's repost (e.g. Black share "18% in Atlanta, 11% in Minneapolis, 13% in New York, and 3% in Los Angeles") and other secondary accounts.
- Source 19 should cite BuzzFeed News (25 June 2020) as the reporting, not Wikipedia alone.

True as: "In June 2020 Mobilewalla published the race, gender and age mix of protesters in New York, Los Angeles, Minneapolis and Atlanta, derived from phone data. Its founder and chief executive, Anindya Datta, said it had not been prepared for law enforcement or a public agency." [19, cite BuzzFeed News, 25 June 2020]

No characterisation of Anthropic is involved in this lane.
