## 1. The Government Surveillance Reform Act, introduced in March 2026, would require a court-authorised warrant before federal agencies buy Americans' information from data brokers. It has not passed. [01][20][21]
Verdict: wrong
Primary: H.R. 7901, 119th Congress, "Government Surveillance Reform Act of 2026," introduced in the House 12 Mar 2026 (text as introduced, govinfo). https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/BILLS-119hr7901ih/html/BILLS-119hr7901ih.htm ; bill record https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/BILLS-119hr7901ih
Read: 2026-10-30
Quote: "IN THE HOUSE OF REPRESENTATIVES" / "March 12, 2026" / "Mr. Davidson (for himself, Ms. Lofgren, Ms. Jayapal, and Ms. Jacobs)"
Quote (Sec. 201 heading): "PROHIBITION ON FEDERAL LAW ENFORCEMENT PURCHASE OF PERSONAL DATA FROM DATA BROKERS."
Quote (new 18 U.S.C. 2702(e)(2)(A)(i)): "Subject to clauses (ii) through (vii), a Federal law enforcement agency may not obtain in exchange for anything of value covered personal data if--"
Quote (clause (ii), the only compilation exception): "A Federal law enforcement agency may obtain in exchange for something of value covered personal data as part of a larger compilation of data which includes personal data about persons who are not covered persons, if--"
Quote (new (f)(4)(A), query rule): "Except as provided in subparagraphs (B) and (C), no officer or employee of a Federal law enforcement agency may conduct a query of personal data, including personal data already subjected to minimization, in an effort to find records of or about 1 or more particular covered persons."
Quote ((f)(4)(B)(i), the only court-order reference): "such covered persons are the subject of a court order issued under this title or the Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Act of 1978 (50 U.S.C. 1801 et seq.) that would authorize the Federal law enforcement agency to compel the production of the covered personal data during the effective period of that order;"
Quote (govinfo details page): "Last Action Date" "March 12, 2026"; sponsor "Warren Davidson(OH)"; referred to "the Committee on the Judiciary" and to the House Permanent Select Committee on Intelligence.
True as: The Government Surveillance Reform Act, introduced in the House in March 2026, would bar federal law enforcement agencies from buying Americans' personal data from data brokers, with exceptions. It has not passed.
Notes:
- Date: "introduced in March 2026" holds (12 Mar 2026).
- Warrant: the introduced purchase rule has no warrant route. Sec. 201 bars a "Federal law enforcement agency" from obtaining covered personal data for value, subject to clauses (ii)-(vii), which are exceptions (compilations, whistleblower, compelled process with cost reimbursement, employment, background checks with consent, lawfully obtained public data). None is a warrant or court-order route to buy. The only court-order reference is a query exception in (f)(4)(B)(i), which governs queries about covered persons, not purchases.
- Scope: "federal agencies" is broader than the bill's "Federal law enforcement agency."
- Read limit: govinfo was read in segments (first 100,000 of 269,944 characters). Sec. 201 was read in the segments quoted. The remaining characters were not read, so "no warrant in the bill" is not established for the whole text.
- Senate companion (S. 4082 per Modern Action and policysoup, unverified) and sponsor releases (lee.senate.gov, wyden.senate.gov 403; davidson.house.gov 404) not opened. A later or Senate version could differ; not checked.
- Proton (source 20) says the bill "would require a warrant before agencies can search Americans' data collected under Section 702" and says it "would close the loophole that lets the government buy personal data from brokers." So the warrant the sources describe is for searching 702 data, not for buying data.
- "Has not passed": the govinfo record shows last action 12 Mar 2026 (cached snapshot). A search summary reports it "Active, referred to Judiciary." No live congress.gov check was possible.

## 2. Its vehicle is the renewal of Section 702 of FISA. [20][21]
Verdict: overstated
Primary: H.R. 7901 text as introduced (govinfo, above). Proton, 12 Jun 2026, updated 15 Jun 2026. https://proton.me/blog/fisa-702-expiring-surveillance-reform ; Brennan Center resource page, updated 27 Oct 2026. https://www.brennancenter.org/our-work/research-reports/section-702-foreign-intelligence-surveillance-act-fisa-2026-resource-page
Read: 2026-10-30
Quote (H.R. 7901, Sec. 111): "Four-year extension of section 702" would change the sunset to "effective April 20, 2030." (fetch summary of govinfo text)
Quote (Proton): "would require a warrant before agencies can search Americans' data collected under Section 702." ; "It would close the loophole that lets the government buy personal data from brokers"
Quote (Brennan, data-broker items): "Congress Must Close Data Broker Loophole by Prohibiting Government Purchases of Americans' Sensitive Data" (17 Mar 2026); coalition letter urges Congress not to reauthorize Section 702 "Without Closing the Data Broker Loophole."
True as: The Government Surveillance Reform Act, introduced in March 2026, would reauthorize Section 702 for four years and would separately bar federal law enforcement purchases of data from brokers.
Notes:
- The GSRA is one reauthorization bill, and it contains both the four-year 702 extension (Sec. 111) and the broker purchase bar (Sec. 201, separate title). The broker bar is not the 702 renewal.
- Other 702 vehicles existed in 2026: the April 2026 short-term extensions (H.R. 8322 and the 30 Apr 2026 extension to 12 June, per Roll Call) and H.R. 9238 (failed 11 Jun 2026, 198-218). The GSRA was not the only vehicle, so "its vehicle" is an overstatement of which bill carries the renewal.
- Brennan's resource page does not name the GSRA (fetch summary).

## 3. Section 702 lapsed in June 2026 after Congress failed to agree on reauthorization. [20][21]
Verdict: holds
Primary: Roll Call, "Congress clears short-term FISA extension," posted 30 Apr 2026. https://rollcall.com/2026/04/30/congress-clears-short-term-fisa-extension/ ; Recording Law, "FISA Section 702 Lapses for the First Time After House Vote," published 14 Jun 2026 (secondary). https://www.recordinglaw.com/news/fisa-section-702-surveillance-lapse/ ; Proton, 12 Jun 2026. https://proton.me/blog/fisa-702-expiring-surveillance-reform
Read: 2026-10-30
Quote (Roll Call, posted April 30, 2026 at 5:53pm): "The House on Thursday passed a short-term extension of a key surveillance power." "The final tally was 261-111." "would extend Section 702 of the Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Act through June 12."
Quote (Recording Law): "reached its statutory sunset at midnight on June 12, 2026." "failed 198 to 218 under suspension of the rules" (H.R. 9238, a short-term extension to 2 July)
Quote (Proton, 12 Jun 2026): "It expired over the weekend." "By rejecting a short-term extension, Congress allowed the controversial law to lapse." (quoted from a LinkedIn share text)
True as: Section 702 lapsed at midnight on 12 June 2026, after the House rejected a further short-term extension on 11 June 2026 (198-218) and Congress did not reauthorize it.
Notes:
- The RISAA sunset was 19/20 April 2026 (per the RISAA setting; not checked in the statute). Search summaries say H.R. 8322 became law 18 Apr 2026 with a sunset of 30 Apr 2026; the govinfo text shows "effective April 30, 2026," but no enactment date is printed. Then the 30 Apr extension to 12 Jun passed 261-111. So the path was two short-term patches, then lapse.
- "Failed to agree on reauthorization": holds for the 11 June vote, which failed under suspension (two-thirds needed).
- Recording Law says "first time" since the FAA 2008. Single secondary source; not checked.
- Axios (11 Jun 2026) returned 403. The vote tally and date rest on Recording Law.

## 4. Existing authorizations run to March 2027. [20][21]
Verdict: holds
Primary: Brennan Center, "Section 702 of FISA: 2026 resource page," updated 27 Oct 2026 (titles only; body not read in full). https://www.brennancenter.org/our-work/research-reports/section-702-foreign-intelligence-surveillance-act-fisa-2026-resource-page ; Proton, 12 Jun 2026 (quoting Rep. Jamie Raskin via CBS). https://proton.me/blog/fisa-702-expiring-surveillance-reform ; Recording Law, 14 Jun 2026.
Read: 2026-10-30
Quote (Brennan resource titles): "Section 702 Surveillance Will Continue Until March 2027 Even if the Statute Lapses" (resource dated June 9, 2026); "Section 702 Surveillance Authority Will Not Lapse if Reauthorized by March 2027" (resource dated September 17, 2026)
Quote (Proton, quoting Raskin via CBS News): "current FISA authorizations will continue unaffected, at least through March 17, 2027,"
Quote (Recording Law): "The March 2026 certifications from the Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Court remain in force, reportedly until around March 2027."
True as: Existing Section 702 authorizations (the FISC-approved certifications from March 2026) continue after the lapse and run to about 17 March 2027.
Notes:
- FISC approval date: a Brennan one-pager (search snippet, secondary to the Brennan site; PDF "bcj-702-march-2027-deadline-one-pager-9.22.26") says the court approved the most recent certifications on 17 March 2026, keeping authority to 17 March 2027. Not opened directly.
- A CRS sidebar (22 Jun 2026, search snippet) notes no such FISC order appears to have been made public; the March 2026 opinion's release is withheld per TechTimes. So the date rests on secondary reporting.
- "Run to March 2027" needs "reportedly" or "about" unless the FISC order is seen. The Raskin quote says "at least through," so the minimum is 17 March 2027.

## 5. As of late October 2026 no data-broker purchase restriction had been enacted. [21]
Verdict: overstated
Primary: No federal enactment found (search only). Montana SB 282 (2025), signed 5 May 2025 per search summary; EFF, "Montana Becomes First State to Close the Law Enforcement Data Broker Loophole," 14 May 2025. https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2025/05/montana-becomes-first-state-close-law-enforcement-data-broker-loophole
Read: 2026-10-30
Quote (EFF, 14 May 2025): "Montana has become the first state to close the data broker loophole." "the government may not use money to get access to information about electronic communications." The article does not say whether a warrant, a subpoena or both are required for purchases.
Quote (CDT, 2026 NCSL overview, search snippet only): Colorado HB 1037 and Rhode Island H8119 "neither have yet been enacted into law."
Quote (Brennan, 27 Oct 2026, fetched): the page lists "Congress Must Close Data Broker Loophole" (17 Mar 2026) and a 19 Mar 2026 coalition letter; it does not report any enacted purchase restriction, and it does not mention a state law (fetch summary).
True as: As of late October 2026, no federal law restricting government purchases of Americans' data from brokers had been enacted that I could find. Montana enacted a state restriction in 2025.
Notes:
- The sentence as written is unqualified: "no data-broker purchase restriction had been enacted." Montana SB 282 (2025) is a state purchase restriction that appears to be in force, so the unqualified sentence is contradicted. The sentence should say "federal."
- The federal negative: one search (general web) found no enacted federal law. That is not a full check. A congress.gov Public Laws search for 2026 was not possible (403). Treat as "not found," not "confirmed none."
- Montana details: EFF says the law bars government spending on certain broker data categories; it does not give the warrant/subpoena rule. Search summaries disagree on warrant vs subpoena. The statute was not opened (legiplex or mt.gov not fetched).
