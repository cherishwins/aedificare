## 1. On 14 September 2021 the SEC settled with App Annie and its co-founder Bertrand Schmitt; it was "the first securities-fraud case against an alternative-data vendor". [04]
Verdict: wrong
Primary: US Securities and Exchange Commission, Press Release 2021-176, "SEC Charges App Annie and its Founder with Securities Fraud" (headline; body says settled), dateline 14 Sep 2021. https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2021-176
Read: 2026-10-30
Quote: "Washington D.C., Sept. 14, 2021 —" (dateline)
Quote: "The Securities and Exchange Commission today announced that App Annie Inc." "a leading alternative data provider for the mobile app industry," "and its co-founder and former CEO and Chairman Bertrand Schmitt," "have agreed to settle securities fraud charges for engaging in deceptive practices" "and making material misrepresentations about how App Annie's alternative data was derived."
Quote: "App Annie and Schmitt have agreed to pay more than $10 million to settle the matter," "which is the SEC's first enforcement action charging an alternative data provider with securities fraud."
True as: The SEC settled (agreed to settle) securities-fraud charges against App Annie and its co-founder Bertrand Schmitt on 14 Sep 2021; it was "the SEC's first enforcement action charging an alternative data provider with securities fraud."
Notes: The phrase in the edition's quotation marks ("the first securities-fraud case against an alternative-data vendor") does not appear in the release; the release's words are "first enforcement action charging an alternative data provider with securities fraud". The substance (first such SEC action) holds; the quoted wording does not. Quotes were taken from a summarising fetch of the release; not re-checked character by character against the HTML. Settlement, not a contested finding: the release says "agreed to settle".

## 4. Berkeley Haas: 4.8 million images from RS Metrics (later combined with Orbital Insight), 67,000 stores, 44 major US retailers, 2011 to 2017; funds with the data earned 4 to 5 percent in the three days around earnings announcements; the edge was largest on the short side; individual investors see short interest only twice a month with a lag and were buying the same stocks. [05]
Verdict: overstated
Primary (paper): Zsolt Katona, Marcus O. Painter, Panos N. Patatoukas, Jean Zeng, "On the Capital Market Consequences of Big Data: Evidence from Outer Space", Journal of Financial and Quantitative Analysis, vol. 60, no. 2 (Mar 2025), pp. 551-579, Cambridge University Press, open access (CC BY 4.0). DOI 10.1017/S0022109023001448. https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/2F5F99D68D1F8940F61578F198D6C005/S0022109023001448a.pdf/on-the-capital-market-consequences-of-big-data-evidence-from-outer-space.pdf (PDF read via local copy; article page https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/on-the-capital-market-consequences-of-big-data-evidence-from-outer-space/2F5F99D68D1F8940F61578F198D6C005)
Secondary (the cited piece, read for what it says): Berkeley Haas Newsroom, May 2019 (fetch summary dated 28 May 2019), "How hedge funds use satellite images to beat Wall Street and Main Street". https://newsroom.haas.berkeley.edu/how-hedge-funds-use-satellite-images-to-beat-wall-street-and-main-street/
Read: 2026-10-30
Quotes from the paper (p. 553, data section): "The store-level data set includes 4.7 million daily observations across 67,078 unique store locations for 44 major U.S. retailers between 2011:Q1 and 2017:Q4."
Quotes from the paper (p. 553): "Over the 3-day window around quarterly earnings announcements, the good news portfolio outperforms the market by +1.6% while the bad news portfolio underperforms by nearly −3%, net of stock loan fees."
Quotes from the paper (p. 553): "The absolute magnitude of returns is nearly twice as large for the portfolio of bad news retailers relative to the good news portfolio."
Quotes from the paper (p. 554): "The general public has access to short-interest data only twice per month and only with a significant delay." and "we find evidence that individual investors are net buyers of the stock of such retailers."
Quotes from the paper abstract (p. 551): "Satellite data enabled sophisticated investors with access to such data to formulate profitable trading strategies, especially by targeting the upcoming reports of retailers with bad news for the quarter."
Quote from Haas (fetch summary): "he landed 4.8 million images of parking lots at 67,000 individual stores across the U.S." (leading word truncated in the fetch output)
Quote from Haas (fetch summary): "The informational advantage yields 4% to 5% in the three days around quarterly earnings announcements,"
Quote from Haas (fetch summary): "Other companies such as Orbital Insight have followed suit,"
True as: The paper (Katona, Painter, Patatoukas and Zeng) studies satellite images of parking lots from RS Metrics, covering 4.7 million daily store-level observations at 67,078 locations of 44 major US retailers, 2011 to 2017. A trading strategy built on the parking-lot signal earned, over the three days around quarterly earnings announcements, about +1.6% on the long side (good-news retailers) and about −3% on the short side (bad-news retailers), each against the market and net of stock loan fees; the short side was about twice as large in absolute size. Individual investors see short-interest data only twice a month and with a significant delay, and were net buyers of the same retailers.
Notes:
- Count: the paper says 4.7 million daily observations at 67,078 locations; the Haas summary says 4.8 million images at 67,000 stores. Do not state 4.8 million as the paper's figure.
- "Funds earned 4 to 5 percent": not in the paper. The paper reports a strategy's portfolio returns relative to the market (+1.6% long, nearly −3% short, 3-day window, net of stock loan fees). The 4 to 5 percent appears only in the Haas summary ("informational advantage yields 4% to 5%"), and it is not stated as realised fund earnings. A spread of about 4.6 points is implied by the paper's two figures but not stated there.
- "Later combined with Orbital Insight": not in either source as read. Haas says "Other companies such as Orbital Insight have followed suit", i.e. a separate company. The paper thanks both RS Metrics and Orbital Insight for imagery data. A search for an RS Metrics and Orbital Insight merger found no report of one (search only, no primary checked). The edition's phrase should be dropped or sourced.
- "Edge largest on the short side": holds (paper, asymmetry statement).
- "Individual investors ... buying the same stocks": holds; the paper says "net buyers".
- Paper title and publication: the paper's earlier working-paper versions had other titles (SSRN abstract 3222741, which returned HTTP 403 to WebFetch and was not opened; not routed around). The JFQA version is the one read.
- Date: the edition says May 2019 for the Haas piece; the paper itself is 2025 (JFQA vol. 60 no. 2), with a 2019 working paper.
