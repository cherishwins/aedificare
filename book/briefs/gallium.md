# Brief: gallium (book chapter 1, the pilot)

**Material.** A video on China's hold over gallium and 6G, channel and
date unknown (drop 3 in `book/intake/2026-10-28.md`; the transcript ends
on a mangled sign-off). Advocacy in tone. Its claims are leads; the
chapter cites primaries only.

**For.** An edition, then chapter 1 of the book (`book/README.md`). It is
also the pilot of the split crew, so `COST.md` matters as much as the
verdicts.

**The question the chapter answers.** Where does America's gallium come
from, what has China actually restricted and when, and what does that
explain and not explain? The video's headline (F-35s flying without
radars because of gallium) is the test case: the jets are real, the cause
given for them may not be.

## Claims

Lane A: the F-35 radars
- A1. "Over 300 new F-35 jets have been delivered without radars because of China's export bans on gallium."
- A2. Lockheed puts ballast where the radar goes "so they don't somersault in flight".
- A3. Gallium nitride is what the advanced radars use (which radar: APG-81, APG-85?).
- Leads: The Aviationist, 12 Feb 2026, "Reports suggest F-35s are being delivered without radar amid APG-85 delays"; the F-35 Joint Program Office's testimony to the Senate Armed Services Committee, June 2026 (six F-35Bs accepted by the Marines); GAO's F-35 reports.

Lane B: supply
- B1. The US has produced no gallium for about 40 years; net import reliance 100% (USGS).
- B2. China is 99% of global production ("99 times more than the rest of the world").
- B3. Gallium is a by-product of alumina refining, so a US supply needs that industry first.
- B4. The National Defense Stockpile held no gallium when China's ban began.
- Leads: USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries, gallium (latest year); Defense Logistics Agency Strategic Materials annual materials plan.

Lane C: the restrictions
- C1. China is "cutting off exports" of gallium to Pentagon contractors and weapons makers outside China.
- C2. Leads to establish the dates, not to assume them: export licensing from 1 August 2023; a ban on exports to the United States announced in December 2024; any later suspension. MOFCOM announcements, and their English reporting by named outlets.

Lane D: the Pentagon's exposure
- D1. The Pentagon needs gallium for over 11,000 components.
- D2. 85% of the Pentagon's supply chains depend on Chinese suppliers.
- Both need their origin found before they are tested; if none, they are reported as unattributed and dropped.

Lane E: 6G (only what the chapter needs)
- E1. China's No. 55 Research Institute (CETC 55), under US sanction, built a gallium semiconductor for 6G and is producing 5 million.
- E2. A Chinese all-frequency chip covering 0.5 to 115 GHz; a white-light link over 1 km (Matter, Cell Press).
- E3. BCG: 5G has generated over $1 trillion of output, growing to $18 trillion by 2035.
- E4. A Trump administration memorandum making it US policy "to lead the world in 6G".

Drop: the 6G speed figures (21 TB in 18 seconds; 200 films a second). No
source is given and the chapter does not need them.
